1. The Burning Money Pit: Losing 64 Cents on the Dollar

For the last decade, Xbox's primary strategy was growth through massive acquisitions—buying up Bethesda ($7.5B) and Activision Blizzard ($69B). The idea was to build an unbeatable content library for their subscription service, Xbox Game Pass.

They bought a massive house they simply could not afford to maintain.

2. The Game Pass & Hardware Stagnation

Xbox bet everything on two major assumptions, and both missed the mark:

**The "Gen 9" Hardware Crash:** Xbox entered the current console generation with a much smaller player base than PlayStation, and hardware sales have completely stalled out across the industry.

**Game Pass Slowdown:** They expected Game Pass subscriptions to grow exponentially to cover the multi-billion dollar costs of funding massive first-party games. Instead, growth slowed to a crawl. Subscription revenue hasn't been enough to offset the loss of traditional $70 game sales on their platform.

3. Absolute Bureaucratic Bloat

Because Xbox acquired so many different massive publishers (each with their own internal corporate hierarchies), the management structure became a total mess.
