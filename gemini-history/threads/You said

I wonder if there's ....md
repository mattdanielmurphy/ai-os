The uneven distribution you're dealing with is a classic real-world example of **granular segregation** (often called the "Brazil Nut Effect"). When a bag of unevenly sized or weighted items is shaken during transit, the smaller, denser particles—like broken icing chunks and rice cake dust—sift through the gaps and settle at the bottom.

Here are a few practical ways to counteract the physics of the snack bag:

1. The Inversion Method (Store Upside Down)

Store the bag resting on its top clip or upside down in your pantry.

**How it works:** Gravity pulls the loose icing and dust down to the "top" of the bag while it sits.

**The payoff:** When you flip it right-side up to open it, that accumulated icing cascades back down over the top layer of rice cakes, re-coating them on the way down.

2. The Horizontal Roll (Avoid Shaking)

If you shake a bag vertically to mix it, you actually accelerate the segregation, driving the small bits straight to the bottom.

**How it works:** Keep the bag sealed, turn it horizontally, and gently roll or rotate it end-over-end.

**The payoff:** This forces the materials to tumble across each other horizontally, which promotes blending rather than settling.

3. The Decant and Toss

If the bag is already opened and heavily segregated, the internal geometry of a narrow plastic bag works against you.

**How it works:** Pour the entire contents into a large bowl.

**The payoff:** You can give them a quick toss like a salad, allowing the loose icing and dust to redistribute evenly across the surface area of the mini rice cakes.
