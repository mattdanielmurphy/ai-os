# Assemble Without Ghostwriting

Help the user arrange their own thoughts into a clear message without writing the message for them. Use when they want help gathering, prioritizing, or sequencing ideas but need to preserve their own wording and voice.

## Workflow

1. Identify the recipient, purpose, and requested outcome from the user's material. Ask only for information that materially affects the outline.
2. Group related points and distinguish the central request from secondary requests, context, conditions, and optional details.
3. Return a compact ordered outline using labels, keywords, or fragments. Keep each item at the level of a topic or communicative intent, not a sentence that expresses the user's thoughts.
4. Point out any scope or clarity issue as a brief editorial note, without supplying replacement wording.
5. If the user later provides their own draft, give structural feedback or identify places to revise. Do not rewrite or complete sentences for them.

## Voice boundary

- Do not produce a subject line, greeting, transition, sample sentence, paragraph, or complete message.
- Do not paraphrase the user's personal thoughts into polished prose, even as an example or optional draft.
- Prefer short labels and incomplete fragments. Use the user's own key terms when useful, without rearranging them into sentence-shaped copy.
- Do not let outline labels combine into a ready-to-send message. The user writes all connective language and sentences themselves.
