# YouTube Highlight Reel Curation Case Study & Best Practices

## Failure Mode Analysis: Premature Cutoffs & Setup Bias

### The Problem Case Study
In a video transcript analysis, a segment was cut off at `8:36`:
- `[00:08:29]` *"…but the problem is the depth of the Shelf now what..."*
- `[00:08:36]` *(Segment cut off here)*
- `[00:08:43]` *"…you're generally going to find about 12 in deep a 14in 16 or 18… because your average seed starting tray… is about 21 in wide… so you're going to have overhang on both sides…"*

### Why the Mistake Happened
1. **Setup Bias over Payoff**: The speaker spent minutes discussing general context/background (rack materials, standard widths). The initial topic seemed to wrap up at `8:30`, triggering a cut just as he pivoted to the critical catch (shelf depth vs. tray overhang from `8:30` to `11:05`).
2. **Artificial Duration Pressure**: Strict duration limits caused premature truncation rather than following the core argument to its practical resolution.
3. **Conversational Transition Blindspot**: Vlog-style speakers bridge across conversational gaps before providing crucial counter-intuitive advice.

---

## Core Curation Principles

1. **The "Payoff Over Setup" Rule**: Start at the pivot, not the preamble. Cut the conversational fluff and start right where the speaker states the core problem, metric, or technique.
2. **The "+15s Cliffhanger Check"**: Always inspect the next 15–30 seconds of transcript beyond the planned end point. If the speaker uses bridging phrases (*"but the problem is..."*, *"here's the catch..."*), extend the segment through the resolution.
3. **Actionable Constraints**: Prioritize segments where specific numbers, failure modes, dimensions, or demonstrations are revealed over theoretical introductions.
