## Assistant

**Artist – Album** is significantly more common in standard written text, music libraries, metadata formats, and everyday usage.

Here is how the breakdown looks across different contexts:

Format	Usage Contexts
**Artist – Album**	Standard prose, tracklists, review headers, physical media spines (often), file naming conventions, and major streaming search/display interfaces.
**Album – Artist**	Catalog/inventory systems sorted alphabetically by release, discographies focused strictly on the work first, or specific archival databases.
Why "Artist – Album" is the Standard

**Hierarchy & Classification:** Broad-to-narrow hierarchy is the default for organizing media (e.g., *Author – Book*, *Director – Film*, *Artist – Album*). The artist serves as the parent container for the release.

**Grammar & Reading Logic:** Phrasing the creator first acts as the possessive anchor—reading as *"Pink Floyd – The Dark Side of the Moon"* naturally maps to *"Pink Floyd's [album] The Dark Side of the Moon"*.

**File Management:** When naming digital files without nested folders, placing the artist first keeps an entire discography grouped together chronologically or alphabetically when sorted by filename.
