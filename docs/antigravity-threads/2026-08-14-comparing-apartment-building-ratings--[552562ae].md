## Assistant

You are hitting on the classic **selection bias** and sample size problem with rental property reviews.

Here is why apartment ratings behave the way they do and how to accurately interpret them:

1. The "Negativity Bias" of Apartment Reviews

Unlike restaurants or hotels where people leave 5-star reviews because they had a nice evening, **nobody reviews their apartment building just because everything works normally.**

**The Baseline is Invisible:** When the heat works, water runs, and elevators operate, tenants simply live their lives.

**The Triggers are Severe:** People almost exclusively leave rental reviews when they are furious—damage deposit disputes upon moving out, unexpected maintenance delays, or noise complaints.

Because of this, the general baseline for mid-tier, high-turnover rental buildings often hovers between **2.8 and 3.5 stars**.

2. Does More Reviews Mean a Lower Rating?

Not necessarily lower by default, but **higher volume reflects sustained operational reality over time**, whereas low volume is volatile:

**Low review counts (e.g., 5–15 reviews at 4.2 stars):** High variance. Often skewed by incentivized reviews (e.g., "Review us during move-in for a chance to win a gift card!"), friends/staff, or a tiny, non-representative sample.

**High review counts (e.g., 231 reviews at 2.9 stars):** Statistically robust. A 2.9 across 200+ reviews is not just an anomaly; it indicates consistent friction points across multiple management cycles or building systems.

3. How to Filter the Signal from the Noise

To determine if a building like Windsor Park Plaza is genuinely worse than another option, filter out the emotional vents and look for systemic patterns:

Disregard / Lower Weight	Take Very Seriously
Single complaints about lost damage deposits	**Pest mentions** (bedbugs, cockroaches, mice) spanning multiple years
"Front desk staff was rude to me once"	**Chronic utility failures** (heating outages in winter, chronic hot water shutoffs)
Moving-day parking hassles	**Long-term elevator outages** (especially critical in high-rises)
Rent increase complaints (standard market reality)	**Sound isolation issues** (concrete build vs. paper-thin wood frame)

## Assistant

Creating a standard, legally recognized pay stub (Statement of Earnings) is straightforward. You do not need dedicated payroll software; you just need the standard fields and accurate deductions.

Step 1: Calculate the Exact Deductions

In Canada/Alberta, your pay stub needs to reflect gross pay and statutory deductions (CPP, EI, and Income Tax).

Go to the official **CRA Payroll Deductions Online Calculator (PDOC)**.

Select **Salary/Hourly**, province as **Alberta**, and your pay frequency (e.g., Bi-weekly, Semi-monthly, or Monthly).

Enter your gross pay for the pay period.

The tool will calculate:

**Gross Pay**

**Federal & Provincial Tax**

**CPP (Canada Pension Plan)**

**EI (Employment Insurance)**

**Net Pay** (Gross Pay minus Deductions)

Step 2: Assemble the Pay Stub

Under Alberta Employment Standards, a complete pay stub must contain the following details:

Plaintext

```
======================================================================
STATEMENT OF EARNINGS
======================================================================
EMPLOYER:                               EMPLOYEE:
[Business/Employer Name]                [Your Full Name]
[Address / City, Province, Postal]       [Your Address]
                                        SIN: XXX-XXX-[Last 3 digits]

Pay Period: [e.g., July 15, 2026 – July 31, 2026]
Payment Date: [e.g., August 1, 2026]
----------------------------------------------------------------------
EARNINGS                  HOURS      RATE           AMOUNT
Regular Wages             [Hours]    $[Rate/hr]     $[Gross Amount]
----------------------------------------------------------------------
TOTAL GROSS PAY:                                    $[Gross Amount]

DEDUCTIONS / WITHHOLDINGS:
  CPP (Canada Pension Plan)                         $[CPP]
  EI (Employment Insurance)                         $[EI]
  Federal & Provincial Income Tax                   $[Tax]
----------------------------------------------------------------------
TOTAL DEDUCTIONS:                                  -$[Total Deductions]

NET PAY (Direct Deposit / Cheque):                  $[Net Amount]
======================================================================

```

Step 3: Export to PDF

Put the template above into a document (Pages, Google Docs, Word, or Markdown) and format it cleanly.

Generate **two consecutive pay periods** (e.g., mid-July and end-of-July / early August).

Export both as clean **PDFs** to attach to your email.

Alternative: Letter of Employment

If you are paid casually or as a contract/independent helper rather than standard T4 payroll with deductions, have your dad write a brief **Employment & Income Verification Letter** on company or formal letterhead instead:

To: Morguard Leasing / Property Management

Date: August 14, 2026

**Re: Employment & Income Verification for [Your Full Name]**

This letter confirms that [Your Full Name] is employed with [Employer/Company Name] as a [Job Title] since [Start Date]. [Your Name] works [number] hours per week at an hourly rate of [Rate]/hr(averagingapproximately[Monthly Average]/month).

Please contact me at [Phone Number] or [Email] if you require any additional information.

Sincerely,

[Dad's Name / Signature]

[Title / Business Name]

Pairing your student aid assessment letter with either two generated pay statements or an employment letter will satisfy their checklist immediately.
