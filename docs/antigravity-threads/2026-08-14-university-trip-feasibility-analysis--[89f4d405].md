## Assistant

Taking a 2-week trip *during regular instruction weeks* in January is functionally unfeasible. Even with a reduced course load (2–3 courses / 40–60%), Computer Science courses move quickly with weekly coding labs, early assignment releases, and add/drop deadlines in mid-January. Missing two straight weeks of lectures and lab demos at the start of term sets you back significantly.

However, **taking a 2-week trip in January is completely doable if you leverage the Winter Holiday Break** right before classes start.

1. The Winter Break Window (Late Dec – Early Jan)

Because Fall term final exams wrap up before Christmas, you get a natural 2-to-2.5-week gap before Winter term starts:

**University of Alberta (2026–2027):**

**Fall Exams End:** Tuesday, Dec 22, 2026

**Winter Classes Begin:** Monday, Jan 4, 2027

*Trip Window:* ~12 days (Dec 23 – Jan 3). Depending on when your final exams actually land (often finishing earlier than the final day of the exam period), you can easily stretch this to **full 2 weeks**.

**MacEwan University (2026–2027):**

**Fall Exams End:** Friday, Dec 18, 2026

**Winter Classes Begin:** Wednesday, Jan 6, 2027

*Trip Window:* **18 consecutive days** off (Dec 19 – Jan 5). A 2-week trip in late December through early January fits seamlessly here.

2. Other Feasible Trip Windows

Throughout the rest of the academic year, your best options for travel are:

Fall Reading Week (November)

**Dates:** November 9–13, 2026

**Duration:** **9 full days** (combining both surrounding weekends).

Ideal for a 1-week trip without missing lectures or deadlines.

Winter Reading Week (February)

**Dates:** February 15–19, 2027

**Duration:** **9 full days** (anchored around Family Day).

Another clean 1-week break mid-semester.

Spring/Summer Term (May – August)

Once Winter exams end in late April (around April 22–24), you have roughly 4 full months off before the next Fall term unless you choose to register for accelerated spring/summer sessions.

Strategic Advantages of a Reduced Course Load

While a reduced load doesn't eliminate the risk of missing in-person labs or midterms during class weeks, it offers two major travel advantages during the term:

**Long Weekends:** CS course schedules can often be condensed into 2 or 3 days a week. With careful class scheduling during registration, you can easily create 4-day weekend gaps (e.g., Friday through Monday) for quick trips without skipping classes.

**Asynchronous Cushion:** You'll have significantly fewer total assignment cycles competing for your time, making it easier to work ahead before taking a 4- to 5-day getaway during Reading Weeks.
