Facial Biometric Analysis

The analysis shown in `image_f79b43.jpg` is a form of **facial biometric analysis**, specifically demonstrating **facial landmark detection** (also known as geometric morphometrics or nodal point mapping).

Here is a breakdown of how this technique works and why it is used:

**Nodal Points (Landmarks):** The letters A through R in the image identify specific, measurable anchor points on the human face. These typically include the outer and inner corners of the eyes, the width of the nose, the edges of the lips, the temples, and the jawline.

**Geometric Mapping:** By connecting these points with dashed lines, the analyst creates a unique geometric map or "mesh" based on the subject's underlying bone structure.

**Proportional Measurement:** The analysis relies on measuring the distances, angles, and ratios between these specific points. While a person's superficial appearance can change drastically over time due to aging, weight fluctuation, or styling, the fundamental spatial proportions of their skull and key facial features usually remain mathematically consistent.

**Context and Application**
In the context of this image—comparing a 1999 photo of Kim Jong-un to a more recent one ("Heute," meaning "Today" in German)—this technique is used for **identity verification**.

Forensic experts, intelligence agencies, and investigative journalists frequently use this exact type of proportional mapping to confirm if two images depict the same individual. For high-profile leaders like Kim Jong-un, this analysis is often deployed to investigate or debunk rumors of political body doubles by proving that the core facial geometry matches despite significant physical changes.
