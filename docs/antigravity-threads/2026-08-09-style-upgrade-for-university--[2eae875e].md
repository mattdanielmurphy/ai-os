## Assistant

You are hitting on a very common psychological barrier: the fear of the "perceived spotlight." When you live with your parents and see the same friends regularly, any sudden shift in your appearance feels magnified. That initial "Woah, look at this guy" reaction from your family is inevitable, but it is also completely temporary. It usually lasts about 48 hours before your new look just becomes your default state.

Taking control of your appearance before September is a highly practical goal, and your instincts on how to approach it are actually spot-on.

Here is a breakdown of how to tackle this without triggering massive imposter syndrome.

1. The Brother's Girlfriend Strategy

Your insight here is entirely correct. You are describing someone with **socially calibrated, mainstream taste**. She understands contemporary aesthetic baselines without leaning into extreme niche subcultures.

Asking her for help is the most efficient, cost-effective, and reliable route you can take. You are right that many people genuinely enjoy acting as a stylist or giving a makeover, especially when the person asking is a willing participant who respects their opinion.

**How to pitch it:** Keep it straightforward. Tell her you are upgrading your style before university starts, you trust her taste, and you'd love her brutally honest advice on a few outfits or a haircut direction. You could even offer to buy her and your brother lunch or coffee in exchange for a shopping trip.

2. The Edmonton Haircut

If you want to walk in, give a vague description of your lifestyle, and trust the professional to handle the rest, you need a high-end shop that prioritizes the consultation as much as the cut. You are paying for their eye and their expertise.

Here are two top-tier options in Edmonton where they will not mess up your hair:

**Weekly:** Located in the ICE District. It is a premium, modern space. They are extremely precise and used to modern styling. You can tell them exactly what you told me: "I haven't cared about my hair in a year, I'm starting university, I want to keep some length but look presentable. Tell me what works for my face."

**Barber Ha:** Located in the Garneau area. They have a long-standing reputation for excellent, consistent work and thorough consultations. They are highly trained and very good at bridging the gap between a classic barber experience and high-end salon expertise.

3. Upgrading the Wardrobe Safely

You do not need to jump from Walmart t-shirts to a completely different aesthetic. If you try to wear clothes that feel like a costume, you will look physically uncomfortable in them, which defeats the purpose.

**The Black T-Shirt is Fine:** A black t-shirt is fundamentally stylish and slimming. The issue is the *quality and fit* of the Walmart shirts. Your first step is simply buying better basics. A slightly thicker, well-structured black t-shirt (from places like Uniqlo, Muji, or RW&CO) looks intentional rather than lazy.

**Focus on Fit over Fashion:** The biggest mistake guys make when losing weight or upgrading their style is buying complicated patterns or trendy pieces. Stick to neutral, solid colors (black, navy, olive, grey). Focus entirely on how the fabric drapes on your current body.

**AI and Fiverr:** While AI rendering and remote Fiverr stylists exist, they lack the real-world context of how clothes fit your specific proportions or how a fabric moves. Lean on your brother's girlfriend first; her real-world, localized feedback will be significantly more valuable.

## Assistant

The hesitation about buying clothes is a highly practical calculation. Dropping weight steadily means your sizing will shift continuously over the next few semesters.

The optimal approach here is a "bridge wardrobe." You want to avoid over-investing in pieces that will look like a tent on you by December. Buy only the absolute essentials for the start of classes: three high-quality black or neutral t-shirts with a better drape, and perhaps one pair of versatile pants with a bit of stretch. You can rely on a minimal rotation for the fall, saving your primary clothing budget for when your body composition stabilizes.

Going back to Barber Ha for an apprentice cut is the perfect move. Apprentices in high-end shops take longer because they are meticulously checking their own work, and they usually have a senior barber review the final product. It is the best way to get premium attention without the premium price tag. Booking that appointment for late August ensures a sharp look right before you move into residence on the 26th.

## Assistant

To bridge the weight loss gap without burning money, you want **heavyweight, structured blank cotton t-shirts**.

Standard cheap tees (like thin Walmart multipacks) cling to love handles and thin out at the collar. Heavyweight cotton (~6.5 oz to 7.5 oz, or 200+ GSM) holds its own silhouette rather than clinging to yours, creating a clean, structured frame.

Here are the best options ranked by ease, price, and quality:

1. Uniqlo U Crew Neck T-Shirt (Best Overall)

**Price:** ~$25–$30 CAD per shirt

**Why it works:** Designed by Christophe Lemaire, the Uniqlo U tee is the gold standard for budget heavyweight t-shirts. It uses a thick, durable compact-cotton knit that doesn't show contours underneath, holds its collar after dozens of washes, and drapes cleanly.

**How to buy:** Try them on in person in Edmonton (Southgate Centre, West Edmonton Mall, or South Edmonton Common).  
Daily Hive

2. Comfort Colors 1717 (Best Value / Relaxed Fit)

**Price:** ~$10–$14 CAD per shirt

**Why it works:** Comfort Colors uses a 6.1 oz garment-dyed heavy cotton. They have a softer, broken-in feel than raw crisp cotton, but still have enough weight to drape flat instead of sticking to your body. The crewneck collar is thick and won't bacon/curl over time.

**How to buy:** Don't buy these at retail boutiques where they mark them up to $35+. Buy them blank online from Canadian wholesale suppliers like **T-shirt.ca** or **Blanks.ca** (no wholesale license required for consumers).  
Blanks CA

3. Shaka Wear Max Heavyweight or Pro Club Heavyweight (Best "Structured" Fit)

**Price:** ~$12–$18 CAD per shirt online

**Why it works:** These are classic 7.5 oz heavy-knitted street-style blank tees. They have tight collar bands and structured sleeves that don't flare out. Because the fabric is so dense, it completely hides torso contours and creates a square, clean silhouette.  
Stay Golden Custom

**How to buy:** Order online from blank apparel distributors in Canada, or check local skate shops/blank suppliers.

4. Kirkland Signature Heavyweight Tee (Cheapest Utility Pick)

**Price:** ~$5–$7 CAD per shirt (sold in Costco multi-packs)

**Why it works:** They are thick 100% combed cotton tees that punch way above their price tag in terms of fabric weight.

**The Caveat:** They run *very long* and quite boxy. If you are between sizes, size down, or plan to wear them casual/layered.

Recommendation for right now:

Pick up 3 or 4 **Uniqlo U Crew Neck** tees in black and dark charcoal. You can pop into Southgate or WEM, try on a couple of sizes to see which chest/shoulder fit looks best on your current body, and walk out with a solid set of shirts for under $100.
