Changes in taste perception are actually a known and increasingly documented side effect of GLP-1 receptor agonists like **Ozempic (semaglutide)**, even at low doses.

Here is what is likely happening, along with a few other factors to keep in mind.

1. How Ozempic Affects Taste

**GLP-1 Receptors on the Tongue:** GLP-1 receptors aren't just in the gut and brain—they are also expressed directly on taste bud cells (particularly those responsible for detecting sweetness and bitterness). Semaglutide interacts with these receptors, directly altering how taste signals are detected and sent to the brain.

**Brain Reward Modulation:** GLP-1 medications reduce dopamine responses to highly palatable foods (sweet, salty, fatty). Many people report that foods taste "dull," muted, or less intense, which is partly a neurological shift in how the brain processes food rewards.

**Altered Taste (Dysgeusia):** Some users report a specific metallic, sour, or abnormal taste in their mouth after starting semaglutide.

**Dry Mouth (Xerostomia):** Reduced fluid intake or direct side effects of the medication can cause mild dry mouth. Saliva is required to dissolve food molecules so taste buds can register them; a dry mouth immediately leads to a dulling of taste.

**Nutritional Changes:** Reduced appetite can sometimes lead to lower intake of key micronutrients—specifically **zinc**, which plays a major role in maintaining functional taste buds.

2. Other Potential Contributing Factors

While Ozempic is a prime candidate, a few other possibilities are worth considering:

**Age-Related Changes:** Around age 60–65, the total number of functional taste buds and olfactory (smell) receptors naturally begins to taper off slightly. However, sudden or noticeable changes are usually driven by an external trigger.

**Other Concurrent Medications:** Common medications prescribed alongside GLP-1s (such as certain blood pressure drugs, statins, or acid reducers) are notorious for causing taste disturbances.

**Post-Viral Effects:** A recent mild viral infection (including COVID-19 or common colds) can temporarily diminish smell and taste, even if other symptoms were mild.

3. Practical Next Steps

**Differentiate "Taste Loss" vs. "Preference Shift":** Ask if she can still detect basic tastes (salty, sour, sweet, bitter, umami) when eating simple foods, or if foods just taste unappealing/different. A loss of basic taste detection points to dryness or receptor changes; a loss of food enjoyment is a classic GLP-1 effect.

**Increase Hydration:** Ensure she is drinking enough water throughout the day or using an oral rinse for dry mouth if needed.

**Mention It to Her Prescribing Doctor:** At her next routine check-up, she should mention it to her healthcare provider. They can check her zinc levels, review any other medications she might be taking, and ensure there aren't underlying oral health causes.
