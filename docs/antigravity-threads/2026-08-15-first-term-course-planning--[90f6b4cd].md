Yes, exactly! Now that you have accepted your offer and are registering in classes, connecting with the disability/accommodations office is the next key move.

At the University of Alberta, this department is called **Accessibility Resources** (formerly ARDS, part of the Academic Success Centre).

Here is the exact step-by-step roadmap:

---

### Step 1: Submit Your Intake & Medical Documentation
1. **Where:** Go to the [U of A Accessibility Resources Website](https://www.ualberta.ca/current-students/academic-success-centre/accessibility-resources) or email them at `success@ualberta.ca` / `arrec@ualberta.ca`.
2. **Action:** Register as a new student with their online portal (ClockWork / Accommodate) and upload your medical documentation:
   * Your completed **Schedule 4 (Disability Verification Form)** that your doctor submitted to Alberta Student Aid.
   * Your **Audiology Report / Audiogram** (documenting your left ear high-frequency cliff).
   * Any clinical doctor's note / letter regarding mental health / pacing requirements.

---

### Step 2: Book Your Intake Appointment with an Accessibility Advisor
* **Timing:** Book this as soon as possible (August fills up quickly before September classes start).
* **What happens in the appointment:**
  * You will chat (virtual or in-person) with an assigned Accessibility Specialist.
  * You will discuss your specific functional limitations and establish your **Official Academic Accommodation Plan**.

---

### Step 3: Key Accommodations to Request & Lock In

1. **Exam Accommodations:**
   * **1.5× Extra Time** (and rest breaks if needed) for midterms and finals.
   * **Reduced-Distraction / Private Testing Room** in the Academic Success Centre (avoids noisy gymnasiums and echoey lecture halls).
2. **Classroom / Auditory Accommodations:**
   * Permission to use an **FM Wireless Transmitter Microphone System** (where the prof wears a small lapel mic that transmits directly to your receiver/earpiece).
   * Permission to use **AI Live Transcription / Captioning tools** (e.g., Otter.ai / Whisper / Mac transcription) during lectures so you don't exhaust cognitive energy trying to decipher high-frequency consonant sounds.
3. **Course Pacing Approval:**
   * Official institutional recognition of your **40% Reduced Course Load (2 courses / 6 credits)**.

---

### Step 4: Unlock the Assistive Technology Grant (CSG-DSE)
* During your intake meeting, ask your Accessibility Advisor to help you apply for the **Canada Student Grant for Services and Equipment for Students with Disabilities (CSG-DSE)**.
* **Funding:** Provides up to **\$20,000 per academic year** (100% non-repayable grant) for:
  * Ergonomic home study setup & noise-canceling hardware.
  * FM systems and auditory hardware.
  * Specialized computer hardware and transcription/speech-to-text software licenses.

---

### Summary of Next Move:
1. Lock in your placeholder **CMPUT 174** seat on Bear Tracks right now so you're enrolled in 6 credits.
2. Submit your intake application online to **U of A Accessibility Resources** so you're in line for your advisor intake meeting before September 1st.

Here is the breakdown of the introductory music courses and paths at the U of A:

---

### 1. What Exactly is MUSIC 100?
* **Course Title:** *Introduction to Music*
* **Prerequisites:** **None** (open to all students from all faculties).
* **What it covers:**
  * It is primarily an **active listening and music appreciation/context course** focused on Western classical traditions (from Medieval/Renaissance to Modern/Contemporary), with occasional connections to other genres.
  * **Core content:** Breaking down how music works (melody, rhythm, harmony, texture, timbre, form) by actively listening to and analyzing major works (symphonies, concertos, operas, chamber pieces).
  * **Workload/Format:** Lecture-based, listening tests/quizzes, short written reflections or concert response papers, and a final exam. No performance, piano playing, or sheet-music sight-reading required.

---

### 2. Music Theory & Composition: MUSIC 151 vs. Advanced Theory
At the U of A, introductory music theory is divided into two distinct tracks:

### **MUSIC 151 (Basic Music Theory / Music Theory Fundamentals)**
* **Prerequisites:** **None.** (It specifically exists for non-B.Mus students or beginners).
* **Who it’s for:** Non-music majors (like CS, Science, Arts) who want to learn how music is written and constructed from the ground up.
* **What it covers:**
  * Reading treble & bass clefs, time signatures, key signatures.
  * Scales, intervals, triads, chords, and cadences.
  * Basic four-part harmony, voice leading rules, and foundational analysis of short phrases.
* **Composition Element:** You will do short written voice-leading and harmonization exercises, giving you the direct building blocks for composing.

### **MUSIC 155 / 156 (Music Theory I & II for Majors)**
* **Prerequisites:** Requires a passing grade on the departmental **Theory Placement Exam (TPE)** or completion of MUSIC 151 / equivalent RCM Level 8 Theory.
* **What it covers:** In-depth tonal harmony, voice leading, modulation, and advanced structural analysis alongside ear training (MUSIC 156).

---

### 3. The 4 Main "Music Paths" Open to Non-Majors

```
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+

|                              U OF A MUSIC PATHWAYS FOR NON-MAJORS                     |

+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+

|  1. APPRECIATION & CULTURE   --> MUSIC 100 (Classical/Western), MUSIC 102 (World),    |
|                                  MUSIC 103 (Popular Music). [Zero Prereqs]            |
|                                                                                       |
|  2. THEORY & COMPOSITION     --> MUSIC 151 (Fundamentals) -> MUSIC 155/251            |
|                                  -> 200/300-level Electroacoustic / Composition.      |
|                                                                                       |
|  3. MUSIC TECHNOLOGY         --> MUSIC 245 / 445 (Electroacoustic Music / Audio Tech) |
|                                  *Huge overlap with Computer Science & Audio DSP!     |
|                                                                                       |
|  4. ENSEMBLES & PERFORMANCE  --> Choirs (Concert Choir, Madrigal), Symphonic Wind     |
|                                  Ensemble, Orchestra. [Audition required in Sept].    |

+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

---

### 4. Which One Fits Best for Your Goals?

1. **If you want to understand harmony, chords, and start writing music/piano pieces:**
   * **Choose `MUSIC 151`**. It teaches the actual language and notation of music without assuming you already know advanced theory.
2. **If you want a low-stress, engaging lecture class focused on listening and classical repertoire:**
   * **Choose `MUSIC 100`**.
3. **If you want to explore the intersection of CS and Music down the road:**
   * Taking **MUSIC 151** now opens the door for **Music Tech / Electroacoustic Music (MUSIC 245)** in future terms, where CS coding and audio synthesis merge.
