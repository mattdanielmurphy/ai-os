# BBC and HBO Co-production

## User

extras was bbc + hbo?

---

## Assistant

Yes, exactly. It was a joint co-production between the **BBC** and **HBO**.  
en.wikipedia.org

It originally aired on BBC Two in the UK and HBO in the US starting in 2005. The partnership made it much easier for Ricky Gervais and Stephen Merchant to secure high-profile American and international stars (like Ben Stiller, Kate Winslet, and Samuel L. Jackson) to play exaggerated, absurd versions of themselves.  
Wikipedia

## User

how often do joint coproductions occur?

---

## Assistant

Joint co-productions are actually **very common** in the television and film industry, though they often fly under the radar for average viewers.  
Industry Media

While a viewer usually just sees one splash screen logo (like Netflix or HBO), behind the scenes, international partnerships happen constantly—and they have become the predominant model for getting high-budget, "prestige" television off the ground.  
The Ankler

Why Networks Partner Up

Co-productions are primarily a **financial risk-sharing strategy**. High-end drama or comedy can cost anywhere from $3 million to over $10 million per episode. Few domestic networks outside of a couple of massive US giants want to absorb 100% of that financial risk alone.

**Splitting the Bill:** A local network (like the BBC or Channel 4 in the UK) contributes part of the budget for domestic broadcast rights. An international partner (like HBO, AMC, or PBS) covers the rest in exchange for US or global distribution.  
Den of Geek
+ 1

**Access to Talent:** US backing brings deep pockets to hire global A-list stars, while local public broadcasters bring access to top-tier writers, directors, and regional tax credits.

High-Profile Co-Production Networks

The **US + UK pipeline** is easily the most famous and active co-production engine:

US Partner	Frequent UK/International Partner	Famous Joint Productions
**HBO**	BBC / Sky	*Extras*, *His Dark Materials*, *Chernobyl*, *Rome*, *Band of Brothers*
**PBS (Masterpiece)**	BBC / ITV	*Sherlock*, *Downton Abbey*, *Foyle's War*
**AMC**	BBC / Channel 4 / Sky	*The Night Manager*, *Humans*, *Gangs of London*
**Amazon / Netflix**	BBC / Canal+ (France)	*Fleabag*, *Good Omens*, *The Fall*, *Black Mirror* (early seasons)

Beyond the UK, countries with smaller domestic markets (like Canada, Australia, France, and Germany) rely heavily on treaty-backed international co-productions to fund major period pieces, sci-fi, and documentaries.  
Screen Australia

Why They Don't Always Look Like Co-Productions

Most viewers miss how often this happens because **distribution rights get rebranded locally**.

A show might be co-funded 50/50 by a French network and an American streamer. When it releases in North America, it gets slapped with a "Netflix Original" or "HBO Original" badge, masking the fact that half the budget, crew, and production logistics came from an entirely different company overseas.

## User

whats the song at the end of extras

---

## Assistant

The song played over the end credits of *Extras* is **"Tea for the Tillerman"** by **Cat Stevens** (from his classic 1970 album of the same name).

It's a notably short track—just a minute long in full—featuring only piano and vocals.

The only exception in the series is the Season 2 episode featuring Chris Martin, where the Coldplay frontman performs his own cover version of the song over the closing credits.
