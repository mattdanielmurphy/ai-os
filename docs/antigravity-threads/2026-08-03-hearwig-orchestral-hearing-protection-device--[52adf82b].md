## Assistant

That device is a **Hearwig**, a specialized acoustic hearing protection shell designed for orchestral musicians.

What It Does & How It Works

**Hearing Protection:** Orchestral musicians sitting directly in front of loud sections (such as brass or percussion) are routinely exposed to dangerously high sound levels.

**Selective Acoustic Shielding:** The U-shaped, fabric-lined shell absorbs and deflects sound waves originating from behind the musician's head without muffling overall room acoustics.

**Ease of Use:** Unlike traditional earplugs—which can alter pitch perception or make a musician's own instrument sound disorienting inside their head—a player can simply lean back into the shell during loud passages or rests, and lean forward to play unhindered.

**Alternative to Plexiglas:** It serves as a personal, non-reflective alternative to bulky clear Plexiglas acoustic shields, preventing sound from bouncing back toward the players behind them.

## Assistant

Effectiveness

**Acoustic Reduction:** Lab and field testing by occupational health institutes show an overall sound reduction of roughly **3 to 10+ dB**, with higher attenuation at high frequencies coming directly from behind.

**Directional Shielding:** Unlike earplugs—which attenuate sound uniformly in all directions and induce the occlusion effect (making a musician's own voice or instrument sound disproportionately loud inside their head)—the Hearwig targets noise strictly coming from the brass or percussion section behind the player.

**Practical Ergonomics:** Musicians (frequently bassoonists, oboists, or horn players directly in front of heavy brass) lean back into the padded shell during loud passages or rests to shield their ears, then lean slightly forward to play uninhibited.

**No Wall Reflection:** Traditional large Plexiglas shields reflect high decibels straight back into the faces of the brass players sitting behind them. The Hearwig absorbs and disperses sound instead.

Cost

**Boutique Equipment:** Developed by Swedish orchestral musician and inventor Urban Eriksson, Hearwigs are niche, institutional-grade products sold directly to professional orchestras rather than through standard retail channels.

**Price Range:** While standard commercial Plexiglas acoustic shields cost around **$250 to $600 USD**, dedicated acoustic headrest systems like the Hearwig generally cost around **$400 to $800+ USD** per unit depending on mounting hardware and custom interior padding options. Orchestras typically purchase and maintain them as part of their stage setup inventory.
