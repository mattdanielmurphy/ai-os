## Assistant

First off: sit down, lean your head back or rest it between your knees, take slow breaths through your nose, and **stop climbing for today**. What you just experienced is a classic convergence of delayed gastric emptying, a sudden vasovagal / adrenaline spike from the drop, and underfueling.

Here is why that exact combination hits so hard on Mounjaro, and how to structure intense sessions like bouldering safely going forward.

What Just Happened to Your Body?

**The Delayed Gastric Emptying Trap:** Mounjaro slows down stomach emptying. When you ate a heavy, high-fat, high-carbing flatbread 30 minutes before climbing, it didn't digest—it just sat at the top of your stomach. The moment your core engaged and your heart rate spiked, your digestive tract revolted.

**The Adrenaline + Vasovagal Response:** Dropping 6 to 8 feet unexpectedly when you already have a heightened anxiety around landing (especially with a previous ankle injury) triggers a massive survival adrenaline dump. When the immediate fear passes, your body overcompensates with a parasympathetic drop (vasovagal response)—causing rapid dilation of blood vessels, sudden lightheadedness, cold sweat, and intense nausea.

**Underfueling Throughout the Day:** Skipping meals leaves your liver glycogen depleted. Drinking a juice box gives a quick spike of simple sugars, but if your stomach is sluggish and crammed with heavy pizza, liquid absorption gets delayed anyway, leaving you feeling nauseous while the sugar sloshes around.

How to Fuel & Time Meals for Intense Exercise

You don't need to completely fast before climbing, but the **timing and macronutrient composition** need to change drastically.

1. Timing Buffers

**Heavy/Solid Meals (Fats, Proteins, Heavy Carbs):** Stop eating **3 to 4 hours** before an intense session. On Mounjaro, a standard meal takes significantly longer to leave the stomach.

**Light Pre-Workout Snack:** **60 to 90 minutes** before climbing.

**Intra-Workout Quick Carbs:** Right before or during, if needed—stick strictly to liquid or rapid-absorbing carbs (electrolyte drinks, liquid chews).

2. Pre-Climbing Meal Strategy
Timing	What to Eat / Drink	What to Strictly Avoid
**3–4 Hours Before**	Lean protein + complex carbs (e.g., chicken breast with rice, oatmeal).	Heavy cheese, fried foods, pizza, high-grease flatbreads, raw high-fiber veggies.
**60–90 Mins Before**	Simple, low-fat, low-fiber carbs (e.g., a banana, plain toast, applesauce pouch).	Dairy, high-fat spreads, heavy protein bars.
**During Session**	Sip water with electrolytes or dilute fruit juice/sports drink.	Gulping large volumes of water at once (distends the stomach).
Managing Wall Anxiety and Energy on the Wall

When you are already low on energy, your forearms pump out faster. Panic kicks in because downclimbing requires controlled isometric tension—the exact strength you lose when flashed out.

**Downclimb Mapping:** Before pulling onto the wall, visually route your downclimb holds (the big jugs or easy volumes nearby) so you don't panic-look for foot placement when pumped.

**Post-Drop Reset:** If you ever have to take an unexpected drop, don't walk around instantly. Sit down on the pads immediately for 2 minutes to let your central nervous system settle before standing up.

Take it easy for the rest of the evening, sip small amounts of water or electrolytes, and keep your next meal very light (like a plain broth or toast) once the nausea completely subsides.

## Assistant

That’s extremely common on Mounjaro. Because GLP-1 medications blunt overall appetite and slow down digestion, high-protein foods—which are already inherently satiating—are usually the first thing people struggle to get down.

When you're bouldering and training regularly, a protein deficit hits hard: forearms stay pumped longer, recovery between sessions drags, and muscle mass starts taking a backseat.

Here is how to get your protein back up without forcing huge, heavy meals that make you feel stuffed or nauseous:

1. Ditch Large "Protein Meals" for Micro-Doses

Instead of trying to eat 30–40g of protein in a single sitting (which feels like a brick in your stomach on Mounjaro), split it into **15g to 25g doses** spread across the day.

**The Goal:** 4 to 5 small hits of protein.

**Why:** Your slowed gastric emptying actually helps here—it continuously feeds amino acids into your system over time without overwhelming your gut at once.

2. Prioritize Liquid & Soft Protein Options

Solid meats (like steak or dense chicken breast) require significant stomach acid and time to break down, which can trigger that heavy, sitting-in-your-chest feeling. Soft and liquid options bypass a lot of that mechanical digestion:

**Clear Whey Isolate:** Unlike creamy milk-based protein powders that feel heavy, clear whey drinks mix like juice or Gatorade and pack ~20g of protein with zero fat or heavy dairy feel.

**Greek Yogurt & Cottage Cheese:** High protein density without needing to chew through dense meat. (Adding a drop of honey or blending cottage cheese into a smooth sauce works wonders).

**Egg Whites:** Easy to scramble into a soft texture or fold into whole eggs to boost protein without adding heavy fats that delay digestion further.

**Ultra-Filtered Milk / Protein Milk:** Higher protein per cup than standard milk and usually lactose-free, making it easy to sip passively throughout the morning.

3. The "Front-Loading" Strategy

Since appetite tends to decline as the day goes on, **get 30–40% of your protein target in within two hours of waking up**.

If you wait until dinner when your stomach is already sluggish, trying to catch up on protein is almost impossible.

A quick smoothie or soft breakfast gets the bulk of the work done early when your stomach is emptiest.

4. Simple High-Protein Low-Volume Ideas
Option	Approx. Protein	Why it works on Mounjaro
**1 cup 0% Greek Yogurt**	~18–20g	Soft, cold, digested easily, no prep needed.
**1 scoop Clear Whey in cold water**	~20–25g	Feels like drinking flavored water, zero heaviness.
**1 cup Fairlife / Ultra-filtered milk**	~13–15g	Easy to sip passively while working or relaxing.
**3 egg whites + 1 whole egg**	~20g	Very low fat, soft texture, quick to eat.

Start by just adding **one** low-volume protein source (like a clear whey drink or a small yogurt) to your daily routine for a few days rather than trying to overhaul your whole diet overnight.

Your gut handles smaller doses exponentially better. Instead of one massive 2-scoop shake, split it into **two single-scoop drinks** (approx. 20–22g protein each) spaced **3 to 4 hours apart**.

Check the label on your powder:

**Whey Concentrate:** Contains higher levels of lactose, fat, and milk solids. It thickens up, digests slowly, and is a top offender for bloating and stomach distress on Mounjaro.

**Whey Protein Isolate (or Clear Whey):** Processed to filter out almost all fat, lactose, and heavy solids. It is vastly thinner, digests much faster, and doesn't coat your stomach.

3. Thin Out the Liquid Ratio

If a shake is thick like a milkshake, it will sit like a brick.

Mix 1 scoop with **more water or fluid than suggested** (e.g., 12–14 oz of water per single scoop instead of 8 oz).

Making it cold and thin lets it pass through the stomach far more effortlessly than a thick, creamy sludge.

4. Sip Slowly, Don't Chug

Chugging 16 oz of liquid in 30 seconds distends the stomach wall, which immediately signals your brain to feel nauseous—especially on a GLP-1.

Put your single-scoop shake in a insulated cup with ice and **sip it over 20 to 30 minutes** while working, driving, or relaxing.

Quick Reset Summary:

**Old way:** 2 scoops + thick liquid + chugged in 2 minutes = 45g protein, 4 hours of misery.

## User

How does ultra filtering milk give it MORE protein?

---

## Assistant

It comes down to simple separation rather than adding anything extra to the milk. Ultra-filtered milk doesn't have "synthetic" protein added—it's concentrated natural milk protein.

Here is how the filtration process works:

1. The Fine "Pore" Sieve

Raw milk is mostly made of water (~87%), along with lactose (milk sugar), fat, minerals, and proteins (casein and whey).

In ultrafiltration, cold milk is pushed under pressure through a series of specialized semi-permeable membranes. These membranes act like a microscopic sieve, separating components strictly by their molecular size:

**What passes through the filter (Permeate):** Water, lactose (sugar), and small dissolved minerals.

**What gets trapped/retained (Retentate):** Protein molecules (casein and whey) and calcium, which are physically too large to fit through the membrane pores.

2. Concentrating the Good Stuff

By filtering out a significant portion of the water and lactose, the remaining fluid becomes a **concentrated milk base**.

Think of it like boiling down a sauce to make it thicker and richer, but done mechanically without heat:

**Regular Milk:** 1 cup contains ~8g protein and ~12g sugar (lactose) diluted in mostly water.

**Ultra-Filtered Milk:** Because much of the water and lactose were removed, **1 cup contains ~13g–14g protein and only ~6g sugar**.

Why It Doesn't Trigger Nausea

Because it is 100% real fluid dairy that has simply been filtered, it maintains the **exact taste, texture, and mouthfeel of fresh milk**. There are no chalky added whey concentrates, thickeners, or artificial sweeteners that trigger the taste-aversions and heavy stomach feeling typical of GLP-1 medications.

## Assistant

No, **micro-filtered** and **ultra-filtered** milk are actually two different processes. That distinction completely explains why the nutrition label on Dairyland looks like standard milk.

Micro-Filtered Milk (Dairyland)

**The Goal:** Purity and shelf-life.

**How it works:** Micro-filtration uses a looser, wider filter membrane designed almost exclusively to strain out bacteria, spores, and impurities.

**The Result:** It leaves the nutrients alone. The natural water, lactose, and protein all pass straight through together. It tastes exceptionally fresh and lasts longer in the fridge, but its nutrition profile stays practically identical to regular pasteurized milk (8g–9g protein, 12g sugar per cup).

Ultra-Filtered Milk (Fairlife, Joyya)

**The Goal:** Concentrating nutrition.

**How it works:** Ultra-filtration uses a much tighter filter under higher pressure. It physically blocks and concentrates the large protein and calcium molecules while letting a chunk of the water and lactose drain away.

**The Result:** Higher protein (13g–14g) and half the natural sugars (~6g) per cup.

What to Look for at the Store

If you want that higher-protein milk in Canadian grocery stores, keep an eye out for:

**Fairlife 2%** (usually in a blue plastic bottle)

**Joyya 2%** (made by Saputo, usually in a round, ergonomic bottle)

Look specifically for the words **"Ultra-Filtered"** on the front of the jug—that's the key term that guarantees the 13g+ protein per cup!
