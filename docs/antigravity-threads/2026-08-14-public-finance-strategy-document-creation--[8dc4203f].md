## Module 1: Student Disability & Post-Secondary Financial Engines (Federal & Alberta)

### Canada Student Grant for Services and Equipment for Students with Disabilities (CSG-DSE)

- **Official Name & Administering Organization**: Canada Student Grant for Services and Equipment for Students with Disabilities (CSG-DSE), co-administered federally by Employment and Social Development Canada (ESDC) and provincially through Alberta Student Aid.

- **Exact Dollar Value & Financial Mechanism**: Provides up to $20,000 per academic year in non-repayable direct grant funding per individual applicant.

- **Eligibility Triggers & Gating Factors**: Requires formal verification of a permanent functional limitation. Under federal student aid policy, an undergraduate enrolled in a 40% reduced course load (two courses or six credits per semester) maintaining verified permanent disability status is legally classified as a 100% full-time student. Medical documentation must substantiate the severe bilateral sensorineural high-frequency hearing loss (>1.5 kHz), chronic tinnitus, and Major Depressive Disorder (cognitive stamina limits).

- **Application Portal, Deadlines & Form Codes**: Submitted via the Alberta Student Aid online portal using the *Schedule 4 (Application for Grant for Students with Permanent Disabilities)* form code. The University of Alberta Accessibility Resources (ARDS) office must review, endorse, and sign Section 3 and Section 4 of the Schedule 4 document. The application deadline is 45 days prior to the end of the study period.

- **Eligible Hardware, Software, & Human Services**:

- *Assistive AI Workstations*: High-performance computing laptops equipped with dedicated neural processing units (such as Apple M-Series Max or NVIDIA RTX workstations) required to execute local, real-time speech-to-text algorithms and neural transcription software without network latency.

- *Audio Accommodation Hardware*: Active noise-cancelling headphones (e.g., Sony WH-1000XM5) to mitigate chronic tinnitus in auditory study environments, paired with direct-streaming FM/DM hearing assistance technology.

- *Software & AI Subscriptions*: Enterprise licenses for real-time transcription, automated lecture summarization, and note-taking software (e.g., Descript, Otter.ai, specialized speech-to-text engines).

- *Human Services*: Specialized one-on-one computer science and AI peer tutoring, academic strategy coaching, and specialized note-taking services to compensate for missed auditory information during lectures.

- **Stacking & Clawback Rules**: 100% non-repayable grant income. CSG-DSE does not reduce baseline federal or provincial student loan entitlements, nor is it counted as taxable income under Section 56(1)(n) of the *Income Tax Act*. Unused equipment funds must be returned or accounted for with itemized receipts at the conclusion of the academic period.

### Canada Student Grant for Students with Disabilities (CSGD)

- **Official Name & Administering Organization**: Canada Student Grant for Students with Disabilities (CSGD), administered by ESDC via Alberta Student Aid.

- **Exact Dollar Value & Financial Mechanism**: Flat non-repayable grant of $2,800 per academic year ($350 per month of active study).

- **Eligibility Triggers & Gating Factors**: Triggered automatically upon verification of permanent disability status on the primary Alberta Student Aid full-time application. Compatible with a 40% reduced course load legally designated as full-time status.

- **Application Portal, Deadlines & Form Codes**: Integrated directly into the main Alberta Student Aid full-time application portal. Requires submission of Schedule 4 during the initial setup year.

- **Stacking & Clawback Rules**: Stacks additively with the Canada Student Grant for Full-Time Students (CSG-FT), which provides up to $4,200 per year for low-income students, as well as provincial grant allocations. CSGD has zero clawback against personal income, provincial social assistance, or CRA tax credits.

### Alberta Grant for Students with Disabilities (GFD)

- **Official Name & Administering Organization**: Alberta Grant for Students with Disabilities (GFD), administered by the Government of Alberta (Advanced Education).

- **Exact Dollar Value & Financial Mechanism**: Provides up to $3,000 per academic year in non-repayable provincial grant funding.

- **Eligibility Triggers & Gating Factors**: Intended to cover education-related costs where federal grant limits under CSG-DSE are exceeded or for specific provincial educational support costs not covered by federal funding. Requires full-time student status under disability provisions (minimum 40% course load).

- **Application Portal, Deadlines & Form Codes**: Processed via Alberta Student Aid Schedule 4 in conjunction with the institutional financial aid office.

- **Stacking & Clawback Rules**: Stacks directly on top of CSG-DSE and CSGD. Does not reduce provincial loan maximums and is fully tax-exempt.

### Medical Form Fee Reimbursement Provisions

- **Official Name & Administering Organization**: Medical Form Assessment Fee Reimbursement, administered by Alberta Student Aid.

- **Exact Dollar Value & Financial Mechanism**: Reimburses up to $3,500 per lifetime for out-of-pocket medical, psychological, or audiological assessment costs required to complete student disability paperwork.

- **Eligibility Triggers & Gating Factors**: Requires proof of payment to a registered medical practitioner, audiologist, or clinical psychologist for completing Schedule 4 documentation, psychoeducational assessments, or audiological evaluations.

- **Application Portal, Deadlines & Form Codes**: Requested by submitting an itemized receipt along with an Alberta Student Aid *Request for Reassessment* form via the online student portal.

- **Stacking & Clawback Rules**: Direct cash reimbursement. Fully non-taxable and exempt from income calculations across all student aid platforms.

### Alberta Student Aid Living Allowance & Excess Rent Reassessment Mechanics

Alberta Student Aid calculates full-time monthly living allowances using standardized baseline budgets. For a single independent student, the standard living allowance covers basic shelter, food, transportation, and personal care. The standard shelter cap within the living allowance calculation is $934 per month.

To optimize funding, students paying rent above $934 per month can request an excess rent reassessment. By submitting a signed residential lease agreement demonstrating actual rent costs exceeding the threshold, Alberta Student Aid can adjust the recognized shelter allowance upwards by up to $400 per month above the standard cap, raising the recognized monthly shelter budget to $1,334 per month. Over an eight-month academic year, this adjustment increases non-repayable grant and low-interest loan disbursements by up to $3,200.

Because a 40% reduced course load legally constitutes full-time student status under permanent disability accommodations, the applicant receives 100% of the full-time living allowance allocation while carrying less than half the standard academic course load.

### Spring/Summer Student Aid Funding Strategy

Continuous year-round financial coverage is maintained by structuring enrollment across the Spring and Summer terms:

- **Course Load Calibration**: Enrolling in one three-credit course during the Spring term (May–June) and one three-credit course during the Summer term (July–August) totals six credits across the combined 16-week period. Under permanent disability guidelines, this six-credit enrollment meets the 40% threshold for full-time classification across the Spring/Summer term.

- **Financial Impact**: Triggers a separate full-time Alberta Student Aid application cycle, unlocking four additional months of full-time living allowances (~$5,200 to $6,500), continuous health benefit coverage, and prorated distributions of the CSGD and GFD grants.

## Module 2: CRA Disability Tax Credit (DTC) & Federal Wealth Accumulation

### CRA Disability Tax Credit (DTC) Approval & 10-Year Retroactive Tax Reassessments

The CRA Disability Tax Credit (DTC) is a non-refundable tax credit governed by Section 118.3 of the *Income Tax Act*. Eligibility for the applicant relies on documented functional restrictions across two primary domains: hearing impairment (severe bilateral sensorineural high-frequency loss >1.5 kHz verified by an audiologist) and mental functions necessary for everyday life (cognitive stamina limitations and executive function impairments from Major Depressive Disorder verified by a physician or psychiatrist).

Form T2201 (Disability Tax Credit Certificate) must be completed by the medical practitioner and submitted to the CRA. Upon approval, the CRA issues a Notice of Determination specifying the eligible tax years.

Under Section 220(3.1) of the *Income Tax Act* (Taxpayer Relief Provision), an individual approved for the DTC can request retroactive tax reassessments for up to 10 prior calendar years.   

- **Valuation Formula**: The federal disability base amount ($9,872 for 2025/2026) combined with the Alberta provincial base amount ($16,882 for 2025/2026) reduces net income tax liability.   

- **Transfer Mechanics**: If the applicant had minimal tax liability in prior years, unused non-refundable tax credits can be transferred to a supporting relative (parent or spouse) using CRA Line 32600. This transfer can generate direct tax cash refunds ranging from $1,500 to $2,200 per back-filed year, providing a single retroactive payout if claimed for multiple prior years.   

### Registered Disability Savings Plan (RDSP) Wealth Compounding Architecture

Approval of the DTC unlocks access to the Registered Disability Savings Plan (RDSP), a tax-deferred capital accumulation vehicle featuring federal matching grants and bonds:

- **Canada Disability Savings Bond (CDSB)**: For low-income beneficiaries with adjusted family net income under $35,821, the federal government deposits $1,000 annually into the RDSP with $0 in personal contributions required. The lifetime CDSB cap is $20,000.   

- **Canada Disability Savings Grant (CDSG)**: Provides matching grants based on personal contributions and net income thresholds:   

- 300% match on the first $500 contributed annually ($1,500 grant).   

- 200% match on the next $1,000 contributed annually ($2,000 grant).

- Total: $3,500 in annual federal matching grants for a $1,500 personal deposit. The lifetime CDSG cap is $70,000.   

- **10-Year Unused Entitlement Carry-Forward**: Under federal RDSP rules, an individual can claim up to 10 years of historical bond and grant entitlements upon opening an account. The maximum annual matching payout in a single carry-forward year is capped at $10,500 for grants and $11,000 for combined grants and bonds.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Zero-Contribution Baseline | $0 | $1,000 | $0 | $1,000 |
| Partial Match Tier | $500 | $1,000 | $1,500 (300% Match) | $3,000 |

| Maximum Carry-Forward Year | Variable ($3,500+) | $1,000+ (Accrued) | $10,500 (Capped Max) | $11,000+ |

Depositing $1,500 annually yields $4,500 in annual federal funding ($1,000 bond + $3,500 grant), resulting in $6,000 in total invested capital per year. Funds invested in low-cost index ETFs within an RDSP compound tax-deferred and are exempt from asset limits for provincial social assistance and student financial aid.

### Canada Disability Benefit (CDB) Integration

- **Official Name & Administering Organization**: Canada Disability Benefit (CDB), administered by ESDC and the CRA.   

- **Financial Value**: Direct non-taxable monthly income supplement paying up to $2,400 per year ($200 per month).   

- **Eligibility & Phase-Out Thresholds**: Requires DTC approval and age between 18 and 64. The full $2,400 benefit is paid to individuals with adjusted net family income below $29,402. The benefit phases out gradually for net incomes between $29,402 and $42,404.   

- **Interoperability**: Established by federal-provincial accord as exempt income in Alberta, preventing clawbacks against provincial benefits or student aid living allowances.

### Tax Deduction Structuring: Line 21500 versus METC

To optimize tax filings and lower net income for income-tested benefits, educational and assistive tech expenses should be categorized correctly:

- **Disability Supports Deduction (CRA Line 21500)**: An "above-the-line" deduction directly subtracting eligible support expenses from gross income. Expenses for real-time transcription software, note-taking services, and specialized assistive technology used for education or employment are claimed on Line 21500. This directly reduces Net Income (Line 23600), lowering income thresholds for the CDB, full student grants, and municipal subsidies.

- **Medical Expense Tax Credit (METC - Lines 33099/33199)**: A non-refundable tax credit calculated at the lowest marginal rate (15% federal). Used only for non-deductible medical expenses that do not qualify under Line 21500.

### Alberta Adult Health Benefit (AAHB)

- **Administering Organization**: Government of Alberta (Seniors, Community and Social Services).

- **Financial Value**: Direct coverage for eligible health expenses, including 100% coverage for prescription drugs, dental care, eye exams, eyeglasses, emergency ambulance services, and essential diabetic supplies.

- **Eligibility Triggers & Gating Factors**: Low-income individuals transitioning from provincial support programs or maintaining net income below established thresholds ($16,580 for single individuals, adjusted for inflation and low-income baseline profile).

- **Application Portal & Form Codes**: Submitted via Form ABC 20055 (Application for Alberta Adult Health Benefit). Continuous coverage is maintained through annual CRA income verification.

- **Stacking & Clawback Rules**: Stacks additively alongside University of Alberta Student Union (UASU) supplementary health insurance, acting as first payor for prescription medications and eliminating out-of-pocket medical expenses.

### City of Edmonton Leisure Access Program (LAP)

- **Administering Organization**: City of Edmonton (Community Services).

- **Financial Value**: 100% subsidized unlimited access to all City of Edmonton recreation centres, aquatic facilities, weight rooms, and climbing walls (valued at ~$900/year), plus a 75% subsidy on registered community recreation and fitness courses (up to $100/year).

- **Eligibility Triggers & Gating Factors**: Verified Edmonton residency and income below the Statistics Canada Low-Income Cut-Off (LICO) threshold. The applicant’s earnings (~$25,400) qualify under single-person eligibility criteria.

- **Application Portal & Form Codes**: Applied for online via the City of Edmonton LAP Portal, attaching the prior year's CRA Notice of Assessment.

- **Stacking & Clawback Rules**: Fully non-taxable municipal amenity benefit with zero impact on provincial aid or CRA tax credits.

### Ride Transit Program (City of Edmonton / ETS)

- **Administering Organization**: City of Edmonton in partnership with Edmonton Transit Service (ETS).

- **Financial Value**: Provides a Tier 1 subsidized monthly transit pass for $35 per month (compared to the standard adult pass rate of $100 per month), yielding $780 in annual savings.

- **Eligibility Triggers & Gating Factors**: Edmonton resident with verified low-income status under LICO.

- **Application Portal & Form Codes**: Applied for via the Ride Transit Program online portal or in-person at Edmonton Service Centre locations using the CRA Notice of Assessment.

- **Stacking & Clawback Rules**: Compatible with university enrollment. If the UASU U-Pass fee is assessed on tuition, students with permanent disabilities taking a reduced course load can opt out of the mandatory U-Pass fee through U of A Student Receivables, utilizing the $35/month Ride Transit pass during active study months.

### Utility, Energy Affordability, and Housing Supplements

- **Civida Rent Supplement Program**: Delivers direct housing subsidies ($300 to $500 per month) paid to landlords or low-income tenants in Edmonton spending over 30% of gross income on rent. Application is made through the Civida Housing Portal.

- **Alberta Affordable Housing Network**: Prioritizes social housing allocations and rent support for low-income post-secondary students with verified permanent disabilities.

- **Clean Energy Improvement Program (CEIP) & Low-Income Energy Efficiency Grants**: Offers municipal utility rebates, free installation of low-flow fixtures, LED upgrades, and efficiency rebates for residential utility account holders in low-income brackets.
