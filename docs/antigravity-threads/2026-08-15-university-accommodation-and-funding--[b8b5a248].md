### 3. Recommended "Don't Miss" Events (Best & Most Fun)

During the orientation window (**August 30 – September 4, 2026**), hundreds of events happen across North Campus. Here are the top low-friction, high-value ones worth checking out:

#### 1. **Clubs Fair (Main Quad & SUB)** — *Absolute #1 Must-Visit*
* **When:** September 1–4 (All day in Main Quad and the Students' Union Building).
* **Why:** Over 300+ student organizations, design teams, and hobby clubs set up booths. It’s the easiest way to connect with:
  * **Tech / CS groups:** Undergrad Computer Science Society (UACS), Ada's Team, AI clubs, HackED organizers.
  * **Interests & Hobbies:** Piano/music groups, board game clubs, bouldering/outdoors clubs.
  * Endless free swag, stickers, discounts, and snacks.

#### 2. **Daily Free Pancake Breakfasts (Main Quad)**
* **When:** Every morning during Week of Welcome (usually 8:30 AM – 11:00 AM).
* **Why:** Free hot pancakes and coffee served outdoors in Main Quad by UASU. Great casual atmosphere to grab breakfast on campus before class.

#### 3. **Faculty of Science Welcome & CCIS Tour (Orientation Days)**
* **When:** August 30–31 (via [NSO Registration](https://uab.ca/orientation)).
* **Why:** Takes place inside the **Centennial Centre for Interdisciplinary Science (CCIS)**. You get a direct tour of the CS lecture halls, computing labs, and a chance to meet fellow Computing Science students and profs.

#### 4. **Quad Mainstage Live Music & Lawn Chill**
* **When:** Daily during WOW in Main Quad.
* **Why:** Low-pressure, open-air live acoustic sets and indie bands performing on the outdoor stage. Great place to sit on the grass, listen, and take a breather between exploring campus.

#### 5. **Wilson Climbing Centre / Campus Rec Free Try-It Days**
* **When:** First week of September at the Van Vliet Complex.
* **Why:** Campus Rec usually offers free drop-in climbing/bouldering sessions at the **Wilson Climbing Wall** and open gym tours so you can check out the routes and facilities for the semester.

#### 6. **Outdoor Movie Night in Main Quad**
* **When:** One of the WOW evenings (check [uasuevents.ca](https://uasuevents.ca) for the specific night).
* **Why:** Giant projector screen set up right on the grass in Main Quad with free popcorn. Very relaxed, low-sensory evening hangout under the stars.

### 1. Reassurance: Why This is Completely Standard & Reasonable

It is completely normal to feel that hesitation—impostor syndrome around "invisible" disabilities is something almost every student in your situation experiences. But to put your mind completely at ease:

1. **Your medical justification is objective and undeniable:** 
   - A drop to **95–105 dB at 2 kHz+** is clinically classified as *profound* high-frequency hearing loss. Lecture halls are notoriously echoey, and asking for an FM mic or captioning isn’t an extra perk—it is simply giving your brain the speech frequencies that your ears physically cannot capture.
2. **Advisors see this every single day:**
   - Accessibility advisors do not sit there judging whether someone "looks" disabled enough. Their entire job is to look at medical documentation from a physician and match it to standard tools they have in their catalog.
   - Extra time (1.5×), quiet exam rooms, 40% course loads, and FM systems are the **most routine, standard accommodations** in the entire university system. 
3. **You are working with them, not demanding:**
   - The initial intake is just a friendly conversation where you and an advisor discuss what tools will help you do your best work.
