## Assistant

**Disanalogous** describes a comparison or analogy between two things that are fundamentally different in key, relevant respects—making the analogy invalid, weak, or misleading for the argument being made.

Key Breakdown

**Core Meaning:** Not analogous; lacking sufficient similarity in the areas that matter most to a comparison.

**In Argumentation:** Pointing out that an analogy is *disanalogous* is a common logical counterargument. It highlights that while two items may share surface-level similarities, their underlying differences outweigh those similarities in a relevant way.

Example

**Analogy:** *"Running a national economy is just like managing a household budget; you can't spend more than you take in."*

**Counterargument:** *"That comparison is **disanalogous**. Unlike a household, a national government can print its own currency, issue sovereign debt, and influence macroeconomic factors through taxation and interest rates."*
