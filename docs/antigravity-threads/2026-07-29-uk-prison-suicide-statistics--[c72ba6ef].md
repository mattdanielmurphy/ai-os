## Assistant

Official statistics on suicides in UK prisons are compiled and published quarterly by the Ministry of Justice (MoJ) in the *Safety in Custody* series for England and Wales. In government reporting, prison suicides are classified under **"self-inflicted deaths"**—a category covering confirmed suicides as well as deaths where individuals unintentionally caused their own death during self-harm.

Key Figures & Rates

**Current Rate:** The rate of self-inflicted deaths in prisons has remained broadly stable at approximately **1.0 per 1,000 prisoners** (around 10 to 11 per 10,000) over the past several years.

**Annual Totals (England & Wales):**

**12 months to March 2026:** **67** self-inflicted deaths (down 28% from 93 in the previous 12-month period).

**12 months to September 2025:** **96** self-inflicted deaths.

**2023 Calendar Year:** **93** self-inflicted deaths (rate of 10.8 per 10,000 prisoners).

**Comparison to General Population:** According to Office for National Statistics (ONS) data, the risk of dying by suicide for male prisoners in England and Wales is approximately **3.9 times higher** than for males in the general population.

Demographics and Key Context

**Gender Differences:**

Self-inflicted deaths are predominantly concentrated in the male estate, mirroring the overall prison demographic. Self-inflicted deaths among female prisoners average around 4 per year.

Conversely, non-fatal **self-harm rates are vastly higher in female prisons**—with female establishments recording over 8 to 9 times more self-harm incidents per 1,000 prisoners than male establishments.

**Early Custody Risk:** Parliamentary briefings and HM Inspectorate of Prisons reports highlight that roughly **one-third of all prison suicides occur during the first week of custody**, making early reception, induction, and early remand periods the highest-risk windows.

**Historical Trend:** Prison suicide rates peaked in 2016 (reaching ~15 per 10,000 prisoners) before declining and stabilizing near current levels.
