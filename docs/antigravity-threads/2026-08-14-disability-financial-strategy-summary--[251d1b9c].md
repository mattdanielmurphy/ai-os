You have hit the exact distinction in how U of A ARDS categorizes documentation:

---

### 1. The Hearing Loss Side: Audiology Report is 100% Sufficient
Notice that for hearing loss, the rule says:
> **"Audiologist report OR University of Alberta Verification of Disability Form..."**

Because you have the **official Audiology Report** from a licensed audiologist/clinic showing the audiogram, degree of hearing loss, and tinnitus, **you do NOT need a separate U of A form for your hearing impairment.** That single document unlocks all auditory accommodations (FM systems, transcription software, assistive workstation, and noise-cancelling equipment).

---

### 2. What Category Depression Falls Under at U of A
At U of A Accessibility Resources, Major Depressive Disorder (MDD) is classified under **Mental Health Conditions** (or *Psychiatric / Psychological Disabilities* / *Chronic Health Conditions*).

For mental health conditions:
* ARDS accepts **any detailed medical letter or official government verification form signed by a licensed physician (MD), psychiatrist, or clinical psychologist**.
* If your physician (e.g. Dr. Gera) already signed **Section 2 of your Alberta Student Aid Schedule 4** or provided a clinical letter detailing your diagnosis and cognitive/functional limitations, **ARDS routinely accepts that completed Schedule 4 / medical letter as valid documentation.**

---

### 3. How to Submit Them in the Intake Portal:
When filling out the ARDS intake:
1. Under **Disability Category / Primary Condition**: Select **Hearing / Deaf & Hard of Hearing** $\rightarrow$ Attach your **Audiology Report**.
2. Under **Secondary Condition**: Select **Mental Health Condition** (or *Chronic Health*) $\rightarrow$ Attach your **completed Schedule 4 (Medical Section) / Doctor's note**.

By uploading both, you establish full coverage:
* The **Audiology Report** justifies the live transcription tools, workstation, FM system, and CS peer tutoring.
* The **Schedule 4 / Doctor's verification** justifies the 1.5x exam time, low-distraction room, and 1-on-1 Academic Strategy Coaching.

**No, you do NOT have to pay your doctor \$150 for another form.**

Here is why your existing documents are already sufficient, and how the registration process works:

---

### 1. Why You Don't Need Another Form
Notice the exact policy language on the U of A documentation page:
> *"Qualified healthcare practitioners may complete the University of Alberta Verification of Disability Form. **Alternatively or in addition, healthcare practitioners may provide detailed written reports that fulfill these requirements.**"*

U of A Accessibility Resources **explicitly accepts standard government and diagnostic documentation** instead of their internal template. 

You can simply upload:
1. **Your Official Audiology Report** (from Connect Hearing / clinic) $\rightarrow$ Substantiates the sensorineural hearing loss and chronic tinnitus.
2. **A Copy of Your Completed Alberta Student Aid Schedule 4 (Section 2: Medical Verification)** $\rightarrow$ Substantiates the medical diagnosis and functional barriers (signed by your physician/Dr. Gera).
3. *(Optional)* A copy of your **CRA Form T2201** or clinical assessment notes if you have them.

Because these are licensed medical documents detailing your diagnosis, permanent functional limitation, and physician's signature, **ARDS accepts them as complete third-party verification.**

---

### 2. How the Funding & Sign-Off Flow Works Once You Register

On the second page you linked (*Students Already Approved / Funding & Payment*), U of A outlines how Schedule 4 connects to your registration:

1. **Upload Existing Docs to ARDS Registration Portal**:
   * You submit the online registration and upload your **Audiology Report** and **Schedule 4 / Doctor's Note**.
2. **Intake Appointment**:
   * Your ARDS Accessibility Advisor reviews your existing documentation to set up your classroom accommodations (e.g., 1.5x exam time, testing room).
3. **Schedule 4 Institutional Endorsement**:
   * For the **CSG-DSE grant (services & equipment)**, the advisor fills out and signs **Section 4 (Post-Secondary Institution Recommendation)** of Schedule 4, endorsing your equipment quotes and tutoring/coaching recommendations.
   * You (or ARDS) submit that completed Schedule 4 to **Alberta Student Aid** to unlock the grant disbursement.

---

### 3. Bonus Protection: \$3,500 Medical Fee Reimbursement
Even for any medical fee you *did* already pay to your doctor or audiologist to complete Schedule 4 paperwork:
* **Alberta Student Aid reimburses up to \$3,500 lifetime** for out-of-pocket medical assessment and form completion fees!
* You simply upload the clinic receipt under your Alberta Student Aid portal (under *e-Documents* $\rightarrow$ *Disability Assessment Fee Receipt*) or file a *Request for Reassessment* to receive a direct 100% cash reimbursement.

### The Step-by-Step Breakdown:

### 1. Gather Price Quotes (Before Buying Anything)
* You don't buy yet. You simply go to the **Apple Education Store**, **Best Buy**, or software vendor websites.
* You configure the machine (e.g. MacBook Pro with 36GB RAM, Sony WH-1000XM5 headphones, Glean subscription) and save the carts/quotes as **PDF price estimates**.

* You book your appointment with Accessibility Resources (ARDS).
* You present your medical documents (audiogram + doctor's note) and your equipment quote PDFs.

* Schedule 4 is submitted electronically via your **Alberta Student Aid portal**.

### Alberta Adult Health Benefit (AAHB)

- **Administering Organization**: Government of Alberta (Seniors, Community and Social Services).

- **Financial Value**: Direct coverage for eligible health expenses, including 100% coverage for prescription drugs, dental care, eye exams, eyeglasses, emergency ambulance services, and essential diabetic supplies.

- **Eligibility Triggers & Gating Factors**: Low-income individuals transitioning from provincial support programs or maintaining net income below established thresholds (\$16,580 for single individuals, adjusted for inflation and low-income baseline profile).

- **Application Portal & Form Codes**: Submitted via Form ABC 20055 (Application for Alberta Adult Health Benefit). Continuous coverage is maintained through annual CRA income verification.

- **Stacking & Clawback Rules**: Stacks additively alongside University of Alberta Student Union (UASU) supplementary health insurance, acting as first payor for prescription medications and eliminating out-of-pocket medical expenses.

### City of Edmonton Leisure Access Program (LAP)

- **Administering Organization**: City of Edmonton (Community Services).

- **Financial Value**: 100% subsidized unlimited access to all City of Edmonton recreation centres, aquatic facilities, weight rooms, and climbing walls (valued at ~\$900/year), plus a 75% subsidy on registered community recreation and fitness courses (up to \$100/year).

- **Eligibility Triggers & Gating Factors**: Verified Edmonton residency and income below the Statistics Canada Low-Income Cut-Off (LICO) threshold. The applicant’s earnings (~\$25,400) qualify under single-person eligibility criteria.

- **Application Portal & Form Codes**: Applied for online via the City of Edmonton LAP Portal, attaching the prior year's CRA Notice of Assessment.

- **Stacking & Clawback Rules**: Fully non-taxable municipal amenity benefit with zero impact on provincial aid or CRA tax credits.

### Ride Transit Program (City of Edmonton / ETS)

- **Administering Organization**: City of Edmonton in partnership with Edmonton Transit Service (ETS).

- **Financial Value**: Provides a Tier 1 subsidized monthly transit pass for \$35 per month (compared to the standard adult pass rate of \$100 per month), yielding \$780 in annual savings.

- **Eligibility Triggers & Gating Factors**: Edmonton resident with verified low-income status under LICO.

- **Application Portal & Form Codes**: Applied for via the Ride Transit Program online portal or in-person at Edmonton Service Centre locations using the CRA Notice of Assessment.

- **Stacking & Clawback Rules**: Compatible with university enrollment. If the UASU U-Pass fee is assessed on tuition, students with permanent disabilities taking a reduced course load can opt out of the mandatory U-Pass fee through U of A Student Receivables, utilizing the \$35/month Ride Transit pass during active study months.

### Utility, Energy Affordability, and Housing Supplements

- **Civida Rent Supplement Program**: Delivers direct housing subsidies (\$300 to \$500 per month) paid to landlords or low-income tenants in Edmonton spending over 30% of gross income on rent. Application is made through the Civida Housing Portal.

- **Alberta Affordable Housing Network**: Prioritizes social housing allocations and rent support for low-income post-secondary students with verified permanent disabilities.

- **Clean Energy Improvement Program (CEIP) & Low-Income Energy Efficiency Grants**: Offers municipal utility rebates, free installation of low-flow fixtures, LED upgrades, and efficiency rebates for residential utility account holders in low-income brackets.

## Module 4: Major Private, Foundation & Corporate Scholarships

### Awards for Students with Disabilities in STEM and Computing

Google Lime Scholarship- **Official Name & Administering Organization**: Google Lime Scholarship, administered jointly by Google and Lime Connect.   

- **Financial Value**: \$5,000 CAD (for students studying in Canada) or \$10,000 USD. Includes an all-expenses-paid invitation to the annual Google Scholars Retreat and direct consideration for Google software engineering internships.   

- **Eligibility Triggers & Gating Factors**: Undergraduate or graduate student with a visible or invisible disability enrolled at a Canadian or U.S. university pursuing a degree in Computer Science, Computer Engineering, or a closely related technical field. Requires academic standing and leadership in computing technology.   

- **Application Portal, Deadlines & Form Codes**: Submitted through the Lime Connect online portal. Requires resume, official transcripts, three essay responses, and one letter of recommendation. Application window typically opens in February and closes in late April.   

- **Stacking & Clawback Rules**: Direct scholarship payment made to the recipient. Exempt from tax under Section 56(1)(n) of the *Income Tax Act* for full-time students (including 40% reduced load under disability status). Does not impact student grant calculations.

NEADS National Student Awards & Accessibility Leadership Awards- **Official Name & Administering Organization**: National Educational Association of Disabled Students (NEADS) Award Program.

- **Financial Value**: \$3,000 flat awards (NEADS National Student Award) up to \$10,000 (Holly Bartlett Memorial Award and Accessibility Leadership Award).

- **Eligibility Triggers & Gating Factors**: Canadian citizen or permanent resident with a documented permanent disability enrolled in a post-secondary institution. Evaluated on community involvement, academic commitment, and overcoming disability-related barriers.

- **Application Portal, Deadlines & Form Codes**: Submitted online via the NEADS Funding Portal. Requires proof of disability, academic transcripts, and a personal essay statement. Deadline is late Spring (May).

- **Stacking & Clawback Rules**: Stacks with all federal and provincial student grants.

Canadian Hard of Hearing Association (CHHA) Scholarship Program- **Official Name & Administering Organization**: Canadian Hard of Hearing Association (CHHA) Scholarship Program.

- **Financial Value**: \$1,000 to \$3,000 non-repayable cash awards (e.g., Carisse Annual Scholarship, Frank Algar Memorial Scholarship).

- **Eligibility Triggers & Gating Factors**: Documented hard of hearing, deafened, or high-frequency hearing loss condition. Enrolled in post-secondary studies.

- **Application Portal, Deadlines & Form Codes**: Submitted through the CHHA National Office portal. Requires audiology documentation, transcripts, and personal statement. Deadline: May annually.

AMI Robert Pearson Memorial Scholarship- **Official Name & Administering Organization**: Accessible Media Inc. (AMI).

- **Financial Value**: \$5,000 annual scholarship (two awarded nationally per year).

- **Eligibility Triggers & Gating Factors**: Open to Canadian students with permanent disabilities enrolled full-time in a degree program at a recognized Canadian university.

- **Application Portal, Deadlines & Form Codes**: Submitted via the AMI online scholarship portal. Requires essay submission, reference letters, and transcript. Deadline: Late Spring.

Mattinson Scholarship Program for Students with Disabilities- **Official Name & Administering Organization**: Mattinson Endowment Fund, administered by Scholarship Partners Canada (Universities Canada).   

- **Financial Value**: \$2,000 CAD non-repayable scholarship.   

- **Eligibility Triggers & Gating Factors**: Canadian citizen or permanent resident with a documented permanent disability. Must maintain a minimum cumulative average of 80% (or equivalent) over the last three terms. Under program guidelines, students on a reduced course load under disability accommodations are recognized as full-time students.   

- **Application Portal, Deadlines & Form Codes**: Requires institutional nomination (the University of Alberta nominates one candidate per year). Application code 144MAT. Submitted via the Scholarship Partners Canada portal. Deadline: June 5 annually.   

- **Stacking & Clawback Rules**: Direct award disbursement. Fully tax-exempt and non-interfering with student grant entitlements.

### Corporate STEM, AI & Innovation Awards

- **Keyera Energy - Peter J. Renton Memorial Scholarship**: \$3,000 to \$6,000 award for Alberta residents pursuing undergraduate degrees in engineering, computer science, or physical sciences. Requires academic achievement and community engagement. Applied for via the Keyera Corporate Grants Portal (Deadline: June).

- **RBC Future Launch Scholarships**: \$1,500 to \$10,000 awards for Canadian youth (ages 15–29) pursuing emerging technology, AI, and non-traditional learning pathways. Applications evaluated on a rolling basis via RBC Future Launch portal.

## Module 5: Non-Profit, FOSS & Open-Source Tech Grants & Wage Subsidies

### Non-Profit Wage Subsidies and Youth Employment Capital

Operating an incorporated Canadian non-profit society building public-benefit, open-source software (FOSS) unlocks access to wage subsidies and employment grants:

Canada Summer Jobs (CSJ)- **Official Name & Administering Organization**: Canada Summer Jobs, funded by ESDC.

- **Financial Value**: Covers up to 100% of the provincial minimum wage for non-profit employers, plus mandatory employment related costs (MERC) like CPP and EI, for 30 to 40 hours per week over 8 to 16 weeks (up to ~\$10,000 per position).

- **Eligibility Triggers & Gating Factors**: Employer must be an incorporated non-profit entity. Hired candidates must be youth aged 15 to 30 legally entitled to work in Canada.

- **Application Portal, Deadlines & Form Codes**: Submitted via the Grants and Contributions Online Services (GCOS) portal during the annual application window (December to January).

- **Strategic Execution**: Allows the non-profit society to fund the Founder/Director salary or hire peer software engineers to build public-benefit open-source software.

Student Work Placement Program (SWPP)- **Official Name & Administering Organization**: Student Work Placement Program (SWPP), funded by ESDC and distributed through sector partners like TECHNATION (Career Ready Program), Magnet SWPP, and ICTC WIL Digital.   

- **Financial Value**: Provides wage subsidies up to 50% (capped at \$5,000) for standard hires, or up to 70% (capped at \$7,500) when hiring students from underrepresented groups, including students with disabilities or women in STEM.   

- **Eligibility Triggers & Gating Factors**: The employer must be a registered Canadian business or non-profit. The student must be enrolled in a post-secondary program and the placement must offer work-integrated learning.   

- **Application Portal, Deadlines & Form Codes**: Submitted online via delivery partner portals (e.g., TECHNATION or Magnet SWPP portal) prior to the start of the work term.

Sovereign Tech Fund (STF) & Mozilla Open Source Support (MOSS)- **Financial Allocation**: Direct grants ranging from \$10,000 to \$150,000 for open-source digital infrastructure, security enhancements, and public accessibility tooling.
