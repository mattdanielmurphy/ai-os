## Assistant

**"Don't mention the war" originated with Fawlty Towers itself.** It wasn't referencing an earlier catchphrase; rather, the 1975 episode created the idiom that entered British pop culture.
John Cleese in 'The Germans'. Source: Tellyspotting - KERA

The *Fawlty Towers* Origin

The line comes from Season 1, Episode 6, appropriately titled **"The Germans."**

In the episode, hotel manager Basil Fawlty (John Cleese) suffers a severe concussion during a fire drill. Shortly after escaping the hospital, he returns to the hotel to host a group of German tourists. Terrified of causing offense, he repeatedly warns his staff:

*"Don't mention the war. I mentioned it once, but I think I got away with it all right."*

Because of his concussion—and an overwhelming, awkward obsession with World War II—Basil proceeds to do the exact opposite:

He mishears routine food orders (e.g., turning "prawn cocktail" into "Eva Prawn" and "pickled herring" into "Hermann Göring").

He orders "four Colditz salads" and mentions Goebbels and Ribbentrop.

He ends up doing a goose-step and a finger-mustache Hitler impression right in front of the bewildered guests.

The episode was intended as a satire of post-war British xenophobia and awkwardness around modern Germany.

The *QI* Reference

The running klaxon on *QI* (Series G, Episode 8, titled "Germany") was a direct homage to this scene.

Stephen Fry set "mentioning the war" (or World War II history) as the episode's automatic forfeit. The late Sean Lock famously leaned right into it, repeatedly triggering the klaxon on purpose—culminating in naming author "Evelyn Waugh" just to set the alarm off one last time.
