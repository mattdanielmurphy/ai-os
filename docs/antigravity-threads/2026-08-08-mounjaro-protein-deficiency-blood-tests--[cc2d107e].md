## Assistant

In Alberta, AHCIP only covers lab work that a doctor deems clinically necessary to diagnose or treat a specific condition. However, you can absolutely pay out-of-pocket for "uninsured" or exploratory tests (like total protein, vitamins, or comprehensive hormone panels).

The main hurdle is that you cannot simply walk into a lab and order from a menu yourself. By law, all blood tests processed through the provincial system still require a signed requisition from a licensed practitioner.

Here are the three easiest ways to get those tests ordered and completed:

1. Ask a Doctor for a "Patient Pay" Requisition

You can go to a standard walk-in clinic or your family doctor, explain that you are looking to do some preventative screening, and state explicitly that you are happy to pay out-of-pocket. If the doctor agrees the tests are safe and reasonable, they will write up a standard lab requisition and mark the non-covered tests as "Patient Pay."

**The Process:** When you take that requisition to a community Alberta Precision Laboratories (APL) location in Fort Saskatchewan or Edmonton, tell the phlebotomist at the front desk you have patient-pay tests. They will tally the total, and you pay them directly via debit or credit (APL does not accept cash) before the blood draw.  
Alberta Precision Laboratories

**The Cost:** Prices vary wildly depending on the specific marker. Simple tests often cost between $5 and $20, while highly complex panels can exceed $100. APL will calculate the exact total at the desk.

2. Use a Virtual Telehealth Service

If you want to skip the walk-in waiting room, several Canadian telehealth platforms (such as TelePlus Care or Maple) specialize in providing requisitions for preventative or exploratory bloodwork.  
TelePlus Care

**The Process:** You book a virtual appointment and tell the doctor exactly what markers you want checked. They will email you a digital APL requisition. You print it out, take it to your local APL, pay the out-of-pocket lab fees at the desk, and the results are routed back to the virtual doctor, who will then share them with you.  
TelePlus Care

**The Cost:** You will pay a fee for the virtual consultation itself, plus the out-of-pocket cost of the actual lab tests at APL.  
Maple

3. See a Naturopathic Doctor (ND)

Naturopathic doctors routinely order comprehensive wellness, metabolic, and hormone panels that fall entirely outside standard AHCIP coverage.

**The Process:** You pay for an ND consultation, request the exact exploratory markers you want, and they will provide the requisition. You then take it to APL and pay for the physical tests at the lab counter.

*(Note: Alberta recently introduced legislation allowing residents to pay out-of-pocket for preventative health testing without a doctor's referral, but as of July 2026, this applies specifically to diagnostic imaging like MRIs and CT scans at private clinics, not standard bloodwork.)*  
Government of Alberta

## Assistant

If the goal is strictly a safety net to catch severe deficiencies and prevent long-term issues, the list of worthwhile out-of-pocket tests shrinks considerably.

For basic prevention, **Vitamin D** is the most important out-of-pocket test in Alberta, as a massive percentage of the population becomes severely deficient during the winter, which can lead to immune and bone density issues. Checking **B12** and **Ferritin (Iron)** is also a smart preventative measure to ensure you aren't walking around with baseline anemia or neurological fog.

As for private clinics, they don't quite work the way you described, and they are much more expensive than just paying "a bit extra."

The "Private Clinic" Business Model

Under the *Canada Health Act*, it is strictly illegal for a doctor to charge a patient out-of-pocket for a medically necessary service that is covered by provincial insurance (like AHCIP). You cannot legally buy your way to the front of a public line or pay $50 for a longer doctor's visit.

To get around this law, private clinics in Alberta use a **membership-based concierge model**.

They legally separate the "medical care" from "uninsured health services." The doctors at these clinics still bill Alberta Health for your actual medical checkups. However, the clinic itself charges you a massive, mandatory annual membership fee to walk through the door. This fee legally pays for an "integrated wellness package"—which includes things AHCIP doesn't cover, like 24/7 access to a nurse via text, on-site dietitians, kinesiologists, and 60-to-90-minute appointment slots.

What are the actual fees?

Because they have to bundle all these uninsured services to make the model legal, you cannot pay per visit. You have to buy an annual membership.

In the Edmonton area, clinics like Harrison Healthcare (formerly Copeman) or Enhance Health typically charge:

**An initial onboarding fee:** Often between $1,000 and $1,500 for a massive, multi-hour baseline medical and physical assessment.

**An annual membership fee:** Usually ranges from **$3,000 to $4,500+ per year** for an individual.

What do you actually get for that money?

**Time:** 30 to 60-minute appointments are standard. The doctor actually sits down and talks to you without rushing.

**Access:** Same-day or next-day appointments, plus a private portal or phone number to text the medical team directly.

**Proactive Coordination:** If you need a specialist, their staff aggressively works the phones to find cancellations and get you in faster, rather than just handing you a referral paper.

**Comprehensive Testing:** They will automatically requisition all the preventative bloodwork, metabolic panels, and cardiovascular screenings, and have the time to walk you through exactly what the numbers mean.

If spending $3,500+ a year isn't appealing just to get a doctor who isn't rushed, the hybrid approach of keeping a public GP for emergencies and paying a Naturopathic Doctor a few hundred dollars once a year to run and review comprehensive preventative blood panels is the most common middle ground.
