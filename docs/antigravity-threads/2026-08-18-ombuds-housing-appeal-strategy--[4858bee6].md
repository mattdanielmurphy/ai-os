### 1. Why It’s in the Flowchart (As a Shield, Not a Sword)
In the cheat sheet, notice that `Accessibility Incompatibility` is located strictly under **Branch 3: "Why not just move into residence now?"** and labeled as **`(Secondary)`**:

* **Your primary opening pitch (90% of the conversation):** **Administrative & Temporal Impossibility.** 
  *(The university didn't admit you until August 13 $\rightarrow$ It is chronologically impossible to cancel by July 31 $\rightarrow$ You had to pursue private housing in early August to avoid homelessness).*
* **Why the accessibility point exists:** It is **strictly a defensive countermeasure in reserve**. 

### 2. When (and Only When) to Mention It
You only mention accessibility if the Ombudsperson specifically pushes you with something like:

> *"Residence Services might ask: Now that you are admitted as of August 13, why can't you just take the International House dorm room today and cancel your private apartment search?"*
>

**If (and only if) they ask that question**, you deploy it as objective secondary context:

> *"By the time admission arrived two weeks before classes, I had already committed my living arrangements to private housing. Furthermore, as a registered Schedule 4 student, living off-campus provides the low-sensory environment and accommodations (like my support animals) that an International House dorm simply cannot support."*
>

---

### 3. Summary of Your Game Plan
1. **Opening / Default Case:** Keep it 100% focused on the **admission timeline and administrative catch-22**. Do not mention medical or mental health reasons upfront.
2. **If Pushed on "Why Not Dorm Now?":** First state that you were already deep in the private rental pipeline. If needed, mention accessibility only as reinforcement for why private accommodation was necessary.
3. **Never Say:** *"I signed up on a whim."* or *"I changed my mind."*

Your primary argument is already rock-solid. Keep the accessibility point in your back pocket purely as a contingency.
