In UK policing, **DS** stands for **Detective Sergeant**.

It is a supervisory rank within the CID (Criminal Investigation Department) or other specialized investigative units (like fraud, homicide, or major crime).

Here is how the rank fits into the UK police structure:

**Equivalent Rank:** A Detective Sergeant holds the exact same rank level and pay grade as a uniformed **Sergeant (Sgt)**. The difference is specialization—DSs deal with criminal investigations rather than uniformed operational policing.

**Role & Responsibilities:** A DS typically acts as a field supervisor or team leader, managing a team of Detective Constables (DCs). They oversee active investigations into serious or complex crimes, direct evidence-gathering, write investigation plans, and supervise suspect interviews.

**Hierarchy Position:** It sits directly above **Detective Constable (DC)** and directly below **Detective Inspector (DI)**.
