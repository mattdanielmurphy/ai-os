# Exemplars: the bar for Slub Utils

Indie Mac developers whose work sets the standard for Matt's suite of utilities. Before calling an app,
HUD, website or onboarding flow done, check it against the "Steal this" lines below.

Copy their **habits**, not their **looks**. Our colours come only from the shared palette by role
(`ARCHITECTURE.md` §6), with no orange as a main colour and nothing that reads as stock Claude output.
Researched 2026-10-22 from each developer's own site.

---

## The checklist (what all of them do)

1. **One sentence says what it is.** A name plus one plain, rhythmic tagline. No buzzwords.
2. **Keyboard first, and the keys are shown.** Every shortcut is printed in the UI and on the page.
3. **Honest about limits.** Say what it can't do, why a permission is needed, and what it never touches.
4. **Fast by default, animation with a purpose.** Results as you type; motion only where it explains something.
5. **Smart empty state.** Opening the app shows something useful (recent items), never a blank box.
6. **Trial and pricing are plain.** Free tier or trial with no card, one-time price, a refund line.
7. **Real support docs.** Troubleshooting with exact commands, release notes, a compatibility page.
8. **A voice.** Dry, small humour that people remember; never cutesy, never corporate.

## The Low-Tech Guys: [lowtechguys.com](https://lowtechguys.com)

Two developers in Romania ("guy #1" and "guy #2") with paid apps (rcmd, Clop, Lunar, Cling, Keylume, Pipiri, Crank)
and many tiny free ones (Barglider, Grila, IsThereNet, YellowDot, Music Decoy). Closest match to Slub Utils:
a suite of sharp, single-purpose Mac utilities.

- **Site:** a plain card grid, icon + bold name + two-line tagline, grouped Paid / Free / Extensions.
  Tagline "No-buzzwords driven development"; copy like "copy large, paste small, send fast".
- **[Cling](https://lowtechguys.com/cling):** fuzzy file finder. A one-line floating bar that grows downward
  as results arrive. Opens on recently changed files. Operators (`.pdf`, `in:downloads`, `dm:7d`, `!word`)
  are typed, not clicked. Every action is one chord: `⌘⇧P` preview, `⌘S` stash (pinned across searches),
  `⌘U` private send link, `⌥⏎` drop into the previous app. Ships an MCP server so agents can use it.
  The page even says what it is *not* for.
- **[rcmd](https://lowtechguys.com/rcmd):** app switching with Right Command + a letter. The page demos with
  key sequences instead of video, has a "Focus problems" section admitting macOS limits, explains exactly
  why Screen Recording is needed (titles only, nothing captured), and gives a migration guide for switchers.
- **Pricing:** €15 lifetime, 14-day trial, no card, free tier forever, Pro comparison table, 14-day refund.
- **Support:** licensing help lists the domains to allow, a `curl` check and its expected reply, log commands.

**Steal this:** the bar that grows with results; opening on something useful; a page that admits limits and
explains permissions; one chord per action, all listed; a tiny-free-app tier alongside paid ones.

## Rogue Amoeba: [rogueamoeba.com](https://rogueamoeba.com)

Mac audio utilities: [SoundSource](https://rogueamoeba.com/soundsource/), Loopback, Audio Hijack, Farrago.
Relevant to Matt's audio and HUD work.

- Menu bar is the whole interface: per-app volume, output routing, EQ, pin devices for one-click mute.
- Tagline "Strange name. Great software." Copy is playful but every section shows a concrete use.
- A guided tour of screenshots per feature (effects, devices, AirPlay, groups).
- Download is both trial and full version; a licence key unlocks it.
- Deep support: knowledge base, full manuals, release notes, a live macOS-compatibility status page.

**Steal this:** the menu bar as a complete control surface; one download that is both trial and app;
a manual and a compatibility page for every app.

## Panic: [panic.com](https://panic.com)

Nova, Transmit, Prompt, and the Playdate console.

- Plain confident copy ("We code apps, publish games, and make Playdate") with wry asides
  ("Buy Now_!!_", pixel sprites at the foot of the page).
- Each product has its own strong identity (Playdate: tiny, yellow, a crank) while the house voice stays one.
- "100% native" is a selling point, and their apps share one sync service.

**Steal this:** one house voice with a distinct character per app; small delights in the margins;
shared infrastructure across apps (our kits).

---

## Sindre Sorhus: [sindresorhus.com/apps](https://sindresorhus.com/apps)

One developer, ~60 small native apps (Velja, Plash, Pure Paste, One Thing, Gifski, Command X).

- Tiny focused apps, each doing one job, many in the menu bar, most with Shortcuts actions.
- Site: stats header (apps, years, users, answered support emails), filter pills, cards tagged
  Free / Paid / Open Source and by platform; a "Tiny Apps" section.

**Steal this:** many tiny apps under one roof with a filterable catalogue; Shortcuts support as standard;
showing support effort as a point of pride.

---

## Bjango: [iStat Menus](https://bjango.com/mac/istatmenus/)

- Separate or combined menu bar items; you choose what shows in the bar vs the dropdown, colours and order.
- History graphs, careful typography for live numbers, adopts each new macOS material quickly.
- "Exceptionally efficient", no ads or analytics. 14-day trial.

**Steal this:** settings that let the user decide what the bar shows; live numbers that never jitter
(monospaced digits); efficiency as a feature.

---

## CleanShot X: [cleanshot.com](https://cleanshot.com)

- Every feature (annotate, redact, spotlight, click highlights, keystroke display) gets its own small demo.
- "Truly Mac-native", low memory, battery-friendly; URL-scheme API for automation.
- 30-day money-back guarantee.

**Steal this:** one demo per feature on the page; a URL scheme so other suite apps and agents can drive it.

---

## Cultured Code: [Things](https://culturedcode.com/things/)

- Two Apple Design Awards. Reviewers call out unfolding animations that each have a purpose,
  a super-fast search, and a UI that stays uncluttered as lists grow.
- Full keyboard shortcuts, Shortcuts support, Markdown notes.

**Steal this:** motion that explains where things came from and went; staying calm at scale.

## How agents use this

- Building or reviewing any suite app, HUD, page or onboarding: run the checklist above and name which
  items it misses in the details file.
- When unsure how something should feel, look at how the matching exemplar does it, then make our own version.
- Add a new exemplar here (one section, a "Steal this" line) when Matt points at one he likes.
