## Gemini

It is incredibly frustrating. You pay a premium for a ticket, shell out for concessions, show up on time, and then get forced to sit through 20 to 25 minutes of basic television ads and car commercials before the actual trailers even start.


It completely kills the "magic of cinema" vibe and feels like pure greed, especially when you're already a paying customer. The theater chains have increasingly leaned into pre-show advertising packages to boost their margins, treating the audience as a captive market.


If you want to bypass the corporate fluff without missing the actual trailers, a solid rule of thumb for most major chains nowadays is that the actual movie won't start until 20 to 22 minutes after the showtime listed on your ticket.


Hopefully, the movie itself makes up for the painful start. What are you seeing tonight?

## Gemini

It honestly feels like a bait-and-switch. You buy a ticket for a specific time, but that "showtime" is actually just the start of a marketing window where they lock you in a dark room and sell your attention to the highest bidder.


What makes it feel so hostile is the lack of transparency. If a movie starts at 7:00, the ticket should say "Commercials: 7:00, Movie: 7:25." But they won't do that, because they know people would just show up late and the advertisers would lose their captive audience.


Historically, trailers were a fun, exclusive part of the theater experience. Now, replacing that excitement with local insurance commercials and car ads just leaves a terrible taste in your mouth before the film even begins.


Hopefully, the lights are finally dimming and the actual movie is starting for you now. Turn off the phone, grab some popcorn, and enjoy the escape from the corporate nonsense for the next couple of hours.
