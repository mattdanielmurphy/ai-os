## Gemini
To cleanly transition from the current playing track to your queued track using keyboard shortcuts, you will use a combination of deck loading, crossfading, and volume controls.

Here is the exact step-by-step workflow using the shortcuts from your sheet:

1. Load the Queued Song

Before doing anything, you need to bring your next track from the queue onto the empty deck.

If Deck 1 is playing, load the next track to **Deck 2**: Press **`⌘→`**

If Deck 2 is playing, load the next track to **Deck 1**: Press **`⌘←`**

2. Match the Beats (Optional but Recommended)

To prevent the transition from sounding jarring, sync the tempo of the new track to the playing track:

Press **`2`** (if syncing Deck 1 to Deck 2) or **`9`** (if syncing Deck 2 to Deck 1).

3. Start the Queued Song & Fade Out

You have two main ways to handle the actual fade depending on your mixing style:

Option A: The Smooth Crossfade (Recommended)

This uses djay's auto-transition features to handle the curve perfectly.

Start the new track playing by pressing **`0`** (Deck 2) or **`1`** (Deck 1).

Press **`←`** or **`→`** to trigger a smooth auto-transition toward the new deck.

*Alternative:* If you want a slow, automated cut, you can hold down **`G`** to auto-fade to the other side.

Option B: The Active Line Fader Volume Cut

If your crossfader is centered and you prefer to manage the volume levels directly:

Start the new track playing (**`0`** or **`1`**).

Select the track you want to fade out and press **`⌥Z`** to cut its active line fader instantly, or use the **`-`** key to step its volume down manually.

4. Clean Up the Outgoing Deck

Once the transition is fully complete and the new song is playing solo:

Pause the old deck by pressing **`1`** or **`0`** so it isn't running in the background.

Press **`⌘↑`** to snap your crossfader back to the exact middle, resetting your layout for the next mix.

## Gemini
You actually have a perfect native option for this already built into the ecosystem. Algoriddim djay supports a feature called **djay Remote**, which allows you to link your iPad to your Mac over Wi-Fi or Bluetooth.

You can use the iPad interface as a dedicated MIDI controller to manipulate the crossfader, trigger cues, twist EQ knobs, and launch samples, while your Mac handles the heavy lifting and main screen display.

To set it up:

Download **djay** on your iPad from the App Store if you haven't already.

Ensure both your Mac and iPad are on the same Wi-Fi network (or have Bluetooth enabled).

Open djay on your Mac, go to the settings/djay menu, and look for **djay Remote** (or **Connect iOS Device**).

Open djay on the iPad, and it will prompt you to connect to the running Mac instance.

Once connected, any slider you move or performance pad you tap on the iPad will instantly control the decks on your screen.

If you want to use the iPad to control the Mac app, you have to bypass djay's built-in settings entirely and use standard system tools:
