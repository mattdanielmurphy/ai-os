## Gemini

You're right to be skeptical. There is no historical evidence that Albert Einstein ever said either of those quotes. They are classic examples of "misattribution," where a famous name is attached to a concept to give it more authority.


The Origin of the Quotes
The idea that compound interest is the "eighth wonder of the world" or the "most powerful force" began appearing in advertisements and newspapers in the early 20th century—long before they were linked to Einstein.



* The "Eighth Wonder" Quote: Earliest records point to promotional materials for banks and investment firms in the 1910s and 1920s.

* The "He who understands it..." Quote: This likely evolved from general financial ad copy. Einstein was a theoretical physicist, and while he certainly understood math, his surviving letters and archives contain no mention of financial compound interest.


Why Do People Attribute it to Him?
Einstein’s name has become shorthand for "genius." By attaching his name to a financial principle, it suggests that compound interest isn't just a bank rule, but a fundamental law of the universe.



The Actual Math (Simplified)
While Einstein didn't coin the phrase, the mathematical "force" of compounding is indeed significant. It follows an exponential growth curve rather than a linear one:


The formula for compound interest is:
$$A = P \left(1 + \frac{r}{n}\right)^{nt}$$


Where:



* $A$ = the future value of the investment

* $P$ = the principal investment amount

* $r$ = the annual interest rate (decimal)

* $n$ = the number of times interest is compounded per year

* $t$ = the number of years the money is invested


As a general rule of thumb, it’s always worth double-checking quotes from Einstein, Mark Twain, or Marilyn Monroe—they are the most frequent victims of misattribution.
