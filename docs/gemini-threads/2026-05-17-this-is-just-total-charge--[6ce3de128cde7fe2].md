## Gemini

Yes, neutral objects are attracted to both positive and negative charges. This happens because of a process called induced charge separation (or polarization). Even though the object is neutral overall, the charged object causes the charges inside the neutral one to shift.


How it Works:

* Near a Positive Charge: The electrons in the neutral object are pulled toward the surface closest to the positive charge. This creates a negative surface that is attracted to the positive object.

* Near a Negative Charge: The electrons in the neutral object are pushed away from the surface. This leaves the side closest to the negative charge with a net positive charge, which is then attracted to the negative object.


In both cases, the side of the neutral object that is closest to the charged object will always have the opposite charge, leading to attraction.

## Gemini

That formula list is a bit of a "greatest hits" collection, and physics notation can be annoying because different textbooks use different symbols for the same thing.


The reason you don't see $E = k \frac{Q}{r^2}$ explicitly is that it is actually "hidden" inside two other formulas you do have.


The Missing Link
You have these two:



* $F = \frac{k Q_1 Q_2}{r^2}$ (Coulomb's Law)

* $E = \frac{F}{Q}$ (Definition of Electric Field)


If you take the first formula ($F$) and plug it into the second one ($E$), one of the $Q$s cancels out:


$$E = \frac{(\frac{k Q_1 Q_2}{r^2})}{Q_2}$$


This leaves you with the version we just used:
$$E = \frac{k Q}{r^2}$$


Why is it set up this way?
Your sheet focuses on the relationships between concepts rather than giving you every single variation.



* $E = \frac{F}{Q}$ is the "universal" version (works for any field).

* $E = \frac{k Q}{r^2}$ is the "point charge" version (specifically for spheres or particles).


A Quick Warning on $V$ vs. $\Delta V$
Looking at your sheet, be careful with the Potential formulas:



* $V = \frac{k Q}{r}$: This is for the potential at a specific distance from a point charge.

* $V = IR$: This is Ohm's Law for circuits.

* $\Delta V = \frac{\Delta E_p}{Q}$: This relates voltage to potential energy change.
