No, it is not.


Here is the breakdown of why those two expressions are different:


1. The Left Side: $\frac{\log(2)}{\log(2)}$
Any non-zero number divided by itself equals $1$. Since $\log(2)$ is approximately $0.301$ (base 10) or $0.693$ (natural log), dividing it by itself results in $1$.
$$\frac{\log(2)}{\log(2)} = 1$$


2. The Right Side: $\log(\frac{2}{2})$
First, simplify the fraction inside the parentheses: $\frac{2}{2} = 1$. The logarithm of $1$ in any base is always $0$ (because any base raised to the power of $0$ equals $1$).
$$\log\left(\frac{2}{2}\right) = \log(1) = 0$$


Why the confusion?
It's common to mix up two different logarithmic properties:



* 
The Quotient Rule: This rule states that the log of a quotient is the subtraction of two logs.
$$\log\left(\frac{x}{y}\right) = \log(x) - \log(y)$$
In your case: $\log\left(\frac{2}{2}\right) = \log(2) - \log(2) = 0$.




* 
Change of Base Formula: This rule states that the division of two logs results in a log with a new base.
$$\frac{\log_b(x)}{\log_b(y)} = \log_y(x)$$
In your case: $\frac{\log(2)}{\log(2)} = \log_2(2) = 1$.





Since $1 \neq 0$, the two expressions are not equal.

This is an application of the Change of Base Formula for logarithms.


The formula states that for any logarithmic base $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ (and argument $\boldsymbol{x}$):


$$\log_{\mathbf{b}}(\boldsymbol{x}) = \frac{\log_{\mathbf{a}}(\boldsymbol{x})}{\log_{\mathbf{a}}(\mathbf{b})}$$


How it applies here
In your image, the move works in reverse to simplify the expression:



* Identify the components: You have a fraction where both the numerator and denominator are logarithms of the same (implied) base $\mathbf{10}$.

* The Numerator: $\log(\boldsymbol{x} + \mathbf{2})$ provides the argument.

* The Denominator: $\log \mathbf{3}$ provides the new base.

* The Result: The ratio $\frac{\log(\boldsymbol{x} + \mathbf{2})}{\log \mathbf{3}}$ condenses into a single logarithm with base $\mathbf{3}$:
$$\log_{\mathbf{3}}(\boldsymbol{x} + \mathbf{2}) = \boldsymbol{y} - \mathbf{1}$$


This is a common algebraic tactic used to isolate a variable or to make an equation easier to convert into exponential form.

## Gemini

Logarithm Terminology
A logarithm is the inverse of an exponential expression. It is generally written as:


$$\log_{b}(a) = x$$


Base ($b$)
The number that is being raised to a power. In $\log_{b}(a)$, $b$ is the base.


Argument ($a$)
The value that results from raising the base to the exponent. In $\log_{b}(a)$, $a$ is the argument (sometimes called the power).


Exponent ($x$)
The value the logarithm is equal to. It represents the power to which the base must be raised to produce the argument.



Special Cases
Common Logarithm
A logarithm with a base of 10. It is typically written without a base: $\log(x)$.


Natural Logarithm
A logarithm with a base of $e$ ($e \approx 2.718$). It is written as $\ln(x)$.
