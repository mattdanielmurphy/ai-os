To find the standard form equations for these conics, we need to identify their centers, vertices, and axes from the provided graphs.



Question 34: Hyperbola
This is a horizontal hyperbola because it opens to the left and right.


1. Identify the Center $(h, k)$
The center is the intersection of the two dashed asymptotes. Looking at the grid:



* The center is at $(2, 2)$.


2. Identify the Distance to the Vertices ($a$)
The vertices are the "turning points" of the curves.



* The right vertex is at $(5, 2)$.

* The left vertex is at $(-1, 2)$.

* The distance from the center $(2, 2)$ to a vertex is $5 - 2 = 3$. So, $a = 3$.


3. Identify the Vertical Stretch ($b$)
We can find $b$ by looking at the slope of the asymptotes. Starting from the center $(2, 2)$, the asymptote passes through $(5, 3)$.



* The "rise" is $1$ and the "run" is $3$.

* Since the slope for a horizontal hyperbola is $\pm \frac{b}{a}$, we have $\frac{b}{3} = \frac{1}{3}$. Thus, $b = 1$.


4. Write the Equation
The standard form for a horizontal hyperbola is:
$$\frac{(x-h)^2}{a^2} - \frac{(y-k)^2}{b^2} = 1$$


Plugging in our values:
$$\frac{(x-2)^2}{3^2} - \frac{(y-2)^2}{1^2} = 1$$
Final Equation:
$$\frac{(x-2)^2}{9} - (y-2)^2 = 1$$



Question 35: Ellipse
This is a horizontal ellipse.


1. Identify the Center $(h, k)$
The center is the midpoint between the vertices.



* The horizontal vertices are at $x = -7$ and $x = 3$. The midpoint is $\frac{-7 + 3}{2} = -2$.

* The vertical vertices are at $y = -3$ and $y = -7$. The midpoint is $\frac{-3 + -7}{2} = -5$.

* The center is $(-2, -5)$.


2. Identify the Semi-Major Axis ($a$)
This is the horizontal distance from the center to a vertex.



* From $x = -2$ to $x = 3$ is a distance of $5$. So, $a = 5$ (and $a^2 = 25$).


3. Identify the Semi-Minor Axis ($b$)
This is the vertical distance from the center to a vertex.



* From $y = -5$ to $y = -3$ is a distance of $2$. So, $b = 2$ (and $b^2 = 4$).


4. Write the Equation
The standard form for a horizontal ellipse is:
$$\frac{(x-h)^2}{a^2} + \frac{(y-k)^2}{b^2} = 1$$


Plugging in our values:
$$\frac{(x - (-2))^2}{5^2} + \frac{(y - (-5))^2}{2^2} = 1$$
Final Equation:
$$\frac{(x+2)^2}{25} + \frac{(y+5)^2}{4} = 1$$
