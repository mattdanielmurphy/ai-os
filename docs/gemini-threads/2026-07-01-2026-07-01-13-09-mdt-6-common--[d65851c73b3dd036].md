## Gemini

Illustration in logo design bridges the gap between abstract branding and pure storytelling. Unlike a purely typographic wordmark, an illustrative logo uses specific visual styles to convey a brand's personality, industry, or heritage.


Here are the most common illustration styles used in modern and classic logo design:



1. Line Art & Monoline
This style relies on lines of a uniform weight (monoline) to construct the entire imagery. It is highly clean, elegant, and scales incredibly well across digital and print mediums.




* Key Traits: Single weight lines, minimal or no shading, clear negative space.

* Best For: Cafes, boutique brands, outdoor/adventure gear, and modern wellness companies.


2. Geometric & Flat Minimalist
Built using fundamental geometric shapes (circles, triangles, polygons), this style abstracts complex subjects into simple, striking vectors. It avoids gradients and complex textures in favor of clean cuts and flat colors.




* Key Traits: Perfect mathematical symmetry, grid-based layouts, high contrast, zero clutter.

* Best For: Tech startups, architectural firms, financial platforms, and software applications.


3. Mascot & Character Illustration
Mascot logos feature a distinct, stylized character that acts as the visual ambassador of the brand. These are heavily illustrated and rely on bold outlines, vibrant colors, and dynamic expressions to instantly grab attention.




* Key Traits: Strong personality, bold cartoon or comic-style linework, multi-layered shading.

* Best For: Sports teams, esports organizations, food and beverage brands, and entertainment companies.


4. Vintage Hand-Drawn & Engraving
This style mimics historical print methods like woodcutting, etching, or organic ink sketches. It often features intricate cross-hatching, stippling (dot work), and deliberate imperfections to evoke a sense of craftsmanship and history.




* Key Traits: Detailed textures, distressed or weathered details, organic lines, often framed inside badges or emblems.

* Best For: Craft breweries, barbershops, heritage clothing brands, and artisanal goods.



Technical Considerations for Illustrative Logos
When choosing or designing an illustrative style for a logo, execution dictates scalability. A style that looks great on a large screen can easily fail when reduced to a favicon or embroidered on a shirt.

## Gemini

Your premise that it takes thousands of images to train an AI on a specific visual style is actually a bit of a myth left over from the early days of deep learning. While training a foundational image model from scratch does require millions of assets, teaching an existing state-of-the-art (SOTA) model a highly specific aesthetic requires significantly less data.


With modern parameter-efficient fine-tuning techniques, you don't need 5,000 images—you can get remarkable style adherence with as few as 20 to 100 high-quality, highly consistent images.



The Strategic Blueprint for Style Adherence
To build a generator that perfectly emulates your designer’s portfolio and consistently outputs production-ready vector assets, you should combine multi-modal LLMs (like Gemini) with dedicated image-generation backbones.


1. The Core Engine: LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation)
Instead of altering the billions of parameters inside a massive base image model (like FLUX or Stable Diffusion), you train a lightweight adapter called a LoRA.



* Data Requirement: 20–60 perfectly curated examples of your designer's work.

* How it works: The base model's weight matrices are frozen, and a tiny fractional layer (the LoRA) learns the specific geometric quirks, line weights, color palettes, and shading styles of your designer. This adapter can then be toggled on or off at runtime.


2. Using Gemini for Style Dissection and Synthetic Expansion
Multimodal models excel at translation, analysis, and structured logic rather than direct image-weight training. Here is how to use an LLM to build your dataset pipeline:


[Designer Portfolio] ➔ [Gemini Style Analysis] ➔ [Precise Prompts] ➔ [Image Generator] ➔ [Synthetic Dataset]


* Reverse-Engineer the Style: Feed your designer’s portfolio images into the model. Ask it to dissect the work from a technical graphic design perspective: identify the line-weight uniformity, geometric constraints, color theory tokens (e.g., "muted mid-century pastel accents"), negative space balance, and shading techniques (e.g., "clean flat vectors" vs. "stippling").

* Generate the Caption Dataset: Every image in a training dataset needs a text caption. Use the model to write meticulous, objective descriptions of each portfolio piece, appending a unique style token (e.g., in the style of mdm-vector).

* Synthetic Bootstrapping: If your designer only has 10 usable logos, use the model to generate highly detailed prompts based on that exact style framework. Run those prompts through a high-fidelity image generator, manually filter out the errors, and use the perfect outputs to pad your training set up to 50+ images.


3. Enforcing Strict Branding Guidelines
To make the generator usable for a marketing agency, style isn't the only constraint; it must respect client guidelines (colors, layout rules, typography).

## Gemini

Your approach hits right at the core bottleneck of generative design: semantic control.


Traditional diffusion models (like Midjourney or Stable Diffusion) operate in pixel-space; they have zero understanding of vector geometry, layers, or structural relationships. By bypassing diffusion entirely and forcing a SOTA large language model like Gemini 3.1 Pro to write raw, structured code natively, you completely bypass the black-box opacity problem.


Here is a breakdown of how your system layers interact, why your grid protocol is actually a massive competitive advantage, and how to scale this safely.



The Value of Your "Müller-Brockmann Protocol"
You mentioned that the design system protocol might not be necessary. In a pure code-generation context, it is absolutely vital. LLMs suffer from spatial hallucinations in 2D vector space. If you tell an LLM to "draw a balanced logo," it might place a circle at cx="450" and a square at x="530", creating an uncentered, messy layout.


By injecting your grid protocol, you transform a loose creative prompt into a deterministic constraint-satisfaction problem. You are forcing the LLM to think like a frontend developer or a programmatic print designer.



* Reduces Mathematical Drift: The LLM stops guessing coordinates. It calculates relative to a $56\text{px}$ step size ($896 / 16$).

* Simplifies Human Refinement: Because components are locked to a strict grid system, when a human designer opens the SVG in Illustrator or Figma, snapping behavior works out of the box, and paths don't have infinite messy fractional coordinates (like x="512.34892").



How Far Can Gemini 3.1 Pro Go Natively?
SOTA LLMs have an incredible grasp of SVG syntax because XML code is heavily represented in their training data. They can natively generate complex nested groups, clean linear/radial gradients, path objects with bezier curves, and precise mask layers.


Where N0-LoRA Code Generation Shines:

* Abstract Geometric Logos: Tech marks, minimalist corporate insignias, architectural icons, typographic lockups.

* Strict Layout Composition: Ensuring a mark sits perfectly scaled relative to a specific typographic wordmark.


Where It Encounters a Wall:

* The "Hand" of the Artist: LLMs cannot natively replicate the nuanced, organic, expressive linework of a specific human designer’s freehand portfolio just by writing raw code from scratch. It will default to clean, somewhat generic geometry.



Bridging Your Designer's Portfolio with Native SVG Code
To achieve the precise style replication of your designer while keeping the output as fully editable SVG layers, you can use Gemini as a Multi-Stage Compiler rather than a single-shot generator.


The Code-Gen Pipeline
[User Input Brief]
       ↓
[STAGE 1: LLM Layout Architect]
- Evaluates your Müller-Brockmann rules
- Outputs a clean structural layout schema (JSON coordinates for elements)
       ↓
[STAGE 2: LLM Vector Engine]
- Ingests the layout schema
- Apples your designer's stylistic rules (e.g., specific curve rules, path styles)
- Compiles the final, clean SVG code with nested `<g>` tags

Enhancing Your System Prompt for Absolute Style Adherence
To make Gemini emulate your specific designer within raw code, you must translate the designer's portfolio into code-level design rules. Look at your designer's work and add explicit constraints to your DesignSystemProtocol under a new section:


[PORTFOLIO_STYLE_INVARIANTS: STYLE_MDM]

RULE_GRADIENT_LIMIT = Max 1 linear gradient per asset. Gradients must only transition between specified brand tokens (e.g., #2C3E50 to #3498DB). No multi-stop rainbow gradients.
RULE_PATH_PRECISION = Avoid raw cubic bezier paths for geometric shapes; compose them strictly using overlapping shapes and path combinations (e.g., masks) to mimic clean boolean operations.

The Ultimate Benefit: Two-Way Agentic Editing
Because your output is semantic XML code, you unlock the ability to do conversational refinement. If a client or designer looks at the generated logo and says, "I love it, but shift the central emblem up slightly and make the background gradient more subtle," you don't have to regenerate the image from scratch.


You feed the SVG code back into Gemini. Because your prompt strictly enforces <g id="descriptive_name">, Gemini can instantly isolate <g id="central_emblem">, adjust its transform="translate(0, -56)" coordinate based on your grid protocol, tweak the <linearGradient> stops, and hand back an updated, perfectly preserved vector file.

# Vector Art Generator: Transition Plan

Here is the broad, 3-phase plan for transitioning the codebase from constrained pixel-art generation to free-form vector (SVG) art generation.

## Phase 1: Update the AI Schema and Prompts
**Goal:** Teach the AI to generate vector paths instead of constrained rectangles.
*   **Define the Vector Schema:** Replace the current rectangular JSON structure with a schema that supports SVG primitives (e.g., `<path d="...">`, `<circle>`, `<rect>`, `<polygon>`).
*   **Update System Prompts:** Rewrite the AI instructions to focus on creating cohesive vector graphics using Bezier curves and scalable shapes, instead of snapping to a grid.
*   **Remove Old Constraints:** Strip out the logic and validations that strictly enforced pixel-art grid boundaries and overlapping rules.

## Phase 2: Update the Rendering Engine
**Goal:** Render the new vector schema correctly in the browser.
*   **Build the SVG Pipeline:** Replace the existing `<canvas>` or grid-based rendering logic with a robust SVG rendering component.
*   **Data Mapping:** Ensure the frontend correctly parses the new vector JSON structure and dynamically maps it to standard SVG DOM elements.
*   **Scaling & ViewBox:** Implement responsive `viewBox` settings so the generated vector art scales infinitely and crisply on any screen size.

## Phase 3: Refinement and Advanced Features
**Goal:** Elevate the vector art experience with advanced styling and tooling.
*   **Advanced Styling Support:** Extend the schema and renderer to support advanced SVG attributes like linear/radial gradients, stroke weights, opacity, and path fills.
*   **SVG Export:** Implement a "Download SVG" feature, allowing users to save the generated art in a clean, production-ready `.svg` format.
*   **UI/UX Polish:** Update the branding from "Structural Constraint Art" to "Vector Art Generator" and refine the gallery to showcase scalable graphics.

const STORAGE_KEY = 'pixelArtState_v2'

function saveFullState() {
    try {
        const data = {
            apiKey: state.apiKey,
            openRouterKey: state.openRouterKey,
            provider: state.provider,
            fallbackEnabled: state.fallbackEnabled,
            autoSaveEnabled: state.autoSaveEnabled,
            model: state.model,
            prompt: state.prompt,
            paletteId: state.paletteId,
        }
        localStorage.setItem(STORAGE_KEY, JSON.stringify(data))
    } catch (e) {
        console.warn('Failed to save state to localStorage:', e)
    }
}

function restoreFullState() {
    try {
        const raw = localStorage.getItem(STORAGE_KEY)
        if (!raw) return
        const saved = JSON.parse(raw)
        Object.assign(state, saved)
        if (!state.apiKey) state.apiKey = import.meta.env.VITE_GEMINI_API_KEY || ''
        if (!state.openRouterKey) state.openRouterKey = import.meta.env.VITE_OPENROUTER_API_KEY || ''
    } catch (e) {
        console.warn('Failed to restore state from localStorage:', e)
    }
}

function appStateChange() {
    saveFullState()
}

// --- Auto-Save SVGs ---
async function autoSaveSvgAndMetadata() {
    if (!state.autoSaveEnabled || !state.currentMetadata) return

    // Request directory handle on first save
    if (!state.saveDirHandle) {
        try {
            state.saveDirHandle = await window.showDirectoryPicker({
                mode: 'readwrite',
                id: 'pixel-art-saves',
                startIn: 'documents',
            })
        } catch (e) {
            // User cancelled directory picker — show a warning but keep toggle checked
            addStatusLog('Warning: Auto-save skipped — directory not selected.')
            return
        }
    }

    try {
        const safePrompt = state.prompt
            .replace(/[^a-z0-9]/gi, '_')
            .replace(/_+/g, '_')
            .toLowerCase()
            .substring(0, 40) || 'vector_art'
        
        // Clean model name (e.g. google/gemini-2.5-flash -> gemini-2.5-flash)
        const safeModel = state.model
            .replace(/^google\//i, '')
            .replace(/[^a-z0-9.-]/gi, '_')
            .toLowerCase()

        // Extract cost value (e.g., "$0.00015" -> "0_00015")
        let costVal = '0'
        if (state.currentMetadata && state.currentMetadata.activeBilling) {
            const costMatch = String(state.currentMetadata.activeBilling).match(/[\d.]+/)
            if (costMatch) {
                costVal = parseFloat(costMatch[0]).toFixed(8).replace(/\.?0+$/, '')
                costVal = costVal.replace('.', '_')
            }
        }

        const timestamp = new Date().toISOString().replace(/[:.]/g, '-').substring(0, 19)
        const basename = `${timestamp}_${safePrompt}_${safeModel}_${costVal}`

        // Save SVG
        const svgFile = await state.saveDirHandle.getFileHandle(`${basename}.svg`, { create: true })
        const svgWritable = await svgFile.createWritable()
        await svgWritable.write(state.currentSvgString)
        await svgWritable.close()

        // Save metadata
        const metaFile = await state.saveDirHandle.getFileHandle(`${basename}.meta.json`, { create: true })
        const metaWritable = await metaFile.createWritable()
        const meta = {
            prompt: state.prompt,
            model: state.model,
            provider: state.provider,
            palette: state.paletteId,
            
            metadata: state.currentMetadata,
            generatedAt: new Date().toISOString(),
        }
        await metaWritable.write(JSON.stringify(meta, null, 2))
        await metaWritable.close()

        addStatusLog(`Auto-saved: ${basename}.svg + ${basename}.meta.json`)
    } catch (e) {
        console.warn('Auto-save failed:', e)
        addStatusLog(`Warning: Auto-save failed — ${e.message}`)
    }
}

function init() {

    DOM.apiKeyInput.value = state.apiKey || ''
    DOM.openRouterKeyInput.value = state.openRouterKey || ''

    renderPaletteSwatches(state.paletteId)

    switchProvider(state.provider)

    // If a model's provider doesn't match, adjust
    const selectedOpt = DOM.modelSelect.selectedOptions?.[0]
    if (selectedOpt && selectedOpt.dataset.provider !== state.provider) {
        switchProvider(selectedOpt.dataset.provider)
    }

        state.apiKey = e.target.value.trim()

        state.openRouterKey = e.target.value.trim()
        appStateChange()
    })

    // Provider switching

        state.provider = provider
        appStateChange()
        // Toggle button styles

function addStatusLog(message) {
    if (!DOM.statusLogs) return null

    const logItem = document.createElement('div')
    logItem.className =
        'py-1 border-b border-dark-700/30 flex items-start gap-2 text-slate-400'

    let prefix = '●'
    let prefixColor = 'text-blue-500'
    let textColor = 'text-slate-300'

    if (message.startsWith('Error:')) {
        prefixColor = 'text-red-500 animate-pulse'
        textColor = 'text-red-400 font-semibold'
    } else if (message.startsWith('Warning:')) {
        prefixColor = 'text-yellow-500'
        textColor = 'text-yellow-400'
    } else if (
        message.includes('complete!') ||
        message.includes('successfully')
    ) {
        prefixColor = 'text-emerald-500'
        textColor = 'text-emerald-400 font-semibold'
    } else if (
        message.includes('Sending request') ||
        message.includes('waiting')
    ) {
        prefixColor = 'text-cyan-500 animate-pulse'
        textColor = 'text-cyan-300'
    }

    logItem.innerHTML = `
        <span class="${prefixColor} text-[8px] mt-1 shrink-0">${prefix}</span>
        <span class="${textColor} break-words flex-1">${message}</span>
    `

    DOM.statusLogs.appendChild(logItem)
    DOM.statusLogs.scrollTop = DOM.statusLogs.scrollHeight
    
    return logItem
}

function formatCostDisplay(costInDollars) {
    if (costInDollars === 0) return '0¢';
    if (costInDollars < 1) {
        const cents = costInDollars * 100;
        return `${cents.toPrecision(4)}¢`;
    }
    return `$${costInDollars.toPrecision(4)}`;
}

function formatBillingString(billingStr) {
    if (!billingStr) return '';
    const hasApprox = billingStr.includes('≈') || billingStr.includes('~');
    const costMatch = billingStr.match(/[\d.]+/);
    if (!costMatch) return billingStr;
    const costInDollars = parseFloat(costMatch[0]);
    const formatted = formatCostDisplay(costInDollars);
    return (hasApprox ? '≈' : '') + formatted;
}

}

}

function accumulateToTally(metadata) {
    if (!metadata) return
    const computeMs = parseFloat(metadata.computeTime || 0) * 1000
    state.currentGenerationTally.computeTimeMs += computeMs
    state.currentGenerationTally.tokensIn += metadata.promptTokens || 0
    state.currentGenerationTally.tokensOut += metadata.tokensOut || 0
    if (metadata.activeBilling) {

A TLS handshake failure during the token exchange usually means your local machine or network is blocking or intercepting the secure connection to Google's OAuth servers. Because Go binaries (which the Antigravity CLI likely uses) rely on the host system's root certificates and are strict about TLS handshakes, a few common culprits usually cause this.


Here is how to troubleshoot and fix it, ordered from most likely to least likely:


1. Corporate Proxies and SSL Inspection
If you are on a corporate network or using a VPN, there is a high chance an SSL-decryption proxy is intercepting your traffic. Go applications often ignore custom user-installed proxy certificates unless explicitly configured.



* Test connection: Run a quick curl command to see if your system can establish a TLS handshake outside of the CLI:
curl -v https://oauth2.googleapis.com/token



* Fix: If you see a proxy certificate in the curl output, you may need to set your HTTP_PROXY and HTTPS_PROXY environment variables, or temporarily disconnect from the VPN/corporate network to authenticate.


2. Outdated Root Certificates
If your local machine’s certificate authority (CA) store is outdated, the CLI won't trust Google's current TLS certificates.



* On Ubuntu/Debian:
sudo apt-get update && sudo apt-get install --reinstall ca-certificates



* On macOS:
Ensure your macOS is up to date, or update Homebrew certificates if applicable:
brew postinstall openssl




3. System Clock Mismatch
TLS handshakes will strictly fail if your local system time is out of sync with the actual time by even a few minutes, as the security certificates will be viewed as not yet valid or already expired.



* Fix: Check your OS settings and ensure Set time automatically is enabled.


4. Explicitly Force IPv4 (DNS/IPv6 issues)
Sometimes Go's network stack struggles with broken IPv6 configurations during TLS handshakes. If your network prefers IPv6 but it isn't properly routed, it can hang and timeout.



* Fix: Try temporarily disabling IPv6 on your network interface or routing table to force the CLI to use IPv4.
