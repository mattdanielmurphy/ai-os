## Gemini

To find the expected number of flips required to see a specific sequence like 13 heads in a row, we use a classic result from probability theory regarding "waiting times" for Bernoulli trials.


For a fair coin ($p = 0.5$), the expected number of flips $E$ to get a run of $n$ successes is given by the formula:


$$E = \frac{1 - p^n}{p^n(1 - p)}$$


The Calculation
Plugging in your values where $n = 13$ and $p = 0.5$:



* Calculate $p^{13}$: $$(0.5)^{13} = \frac{1}{8,192}$$

* Apply the formula:
$$E = \frac{1 - \frac{1}{8,192}}{\frac{1}{8,192}(0.5)}$$

* Simplify:
$$E = \frac{\frac{8,191}{8,192}}{\frac{1}{16,384}}$$
$$E = \frac{8,191}{8,192} \times 16,384$$
$$E = 8,191 \times 2$$
$$E = 16,382$$


The Result
You would expect to flip the coin 16,382 times on average to see a streak of 13 heads.



Why isn't it just 8,192?
It’s a common intuition trap to think the expected time is just the reciprocal of the probability ($1/P$). However, the "waiting time" for a sequence is slightly different because the flips are overlapping.



* If you fail on the 13th flip (getting a Tail), you don't just lose that one flip; you've lost the entire "progress" of that 12-head streak and have to start the count from zero.

* The formula effectively accounts for all those "near-miss" resets.


If you were to try this in a script—perhaps in TypeScript to test the "Fast" model's logic—you'd find the mean of many simulations consistently hovering right around that 16,382 mark.
