## Gemini

Fair point. It is pretty vague and sounds like a generic textbook filler sentence. Let’s strip out the fluff and make the physics explicit.


Instead of a generic phrase, you can point directly to the two distinct laws of electromagnetism that govern how they function:



* The Similarity: Both rely on the interaction between a magnetic field and moving charges in a conductor.

* The Distinction: They just run that interaction in opposite directions:

* Motor (Lorentz Force / Motor Effect): You push a current through a wire inside a magnetic field $\vec{B}$, which exerts a magnetic force $\vec{F}$ on the charges, forcing the wire to move.

* Generator (Faraday's Law of Induction): You physically force the wire to move through a magnetic field, changing the magnetic flux $\Phi_B$ over time, which induces an electromotive force (EMF) and drives a current.








A Cleaner Way to Frame the Answer
If you want to keep it concise and punchy without the vague "linking electricity and magnetism" phrasing, you can structure it like this:



Similarity: Both share identical physical construction, utilizing a conductive coil of wire placed within a magnetic field.


Difference: > * An electric motor inputs current to produce mechanical rotation (utilizing the magnetic force on a current-carrying wire).



* An electric generator inputs mechanical rotation to produce current (utilizing electromagnetic induction via changing magnetic flux).
