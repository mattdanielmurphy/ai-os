## Gemini

It isn't necessarily "worse," but it's more aggressive on your system. Think of it like the difference between a slow-release watering system for a plant versus dumping a bucket of water on it all at once.


1. The "Concentration Spike"
When you eat a banana or a potato, the potassium is trapped inside fiber and complex carbohydrates. Your body has to break that food down, which releases the potassium slowly into your bloodstream over several hours.


With a powder, the potassium is "free" and hits your stomach all at once. Because you are on Ozempic, that concentrated "pool" of potassium sits in your stomach longer. This can cause:



* Gastric Irritation: Potassium is a salt; in high concentrations, it can irritate the stomach lining, leading to nausea or a "burning" sensation.

* The Kidney Spike: Your kidneys have to work harder to process a sudden surge of potassium compared to the slow drip from a meal.


2. The Bioavailability Myth
People often think powders are "better" because they absorb faster, but your body is actually incredibly efficient at extracting potassium from food. You usually absorb about 90% of the potassium from a banana. The powder doesn't really give you "better" potassium; it just gives it to you faster.


3. The "Missing Partners"
Potassium in food comes with "friends" like Vitamin C, Vitamin B6, and fiber, which all help your body use the mineral more effectively. A powder is just the mineral.



When to use the Powder vs. Food

* Use the Powder: When you've had a high-sodium meal (like a steak or homemade pizza) or when you're in the middle of a caffeine taper headache. It acts like a "rescue" to balance your electrolytes quickly.

* Use Food: For your daily baseline. It’s safer for your heart and much easier on your stomach.


The "Dark Circle" Connection
