## Gemini

If you already have a secured card, an unsecured card, and flawless utilization habits, the short answer is: KOHO rent reporting will do very little to actually move your credit score right now.


While rent reporting services are heavily marketed as credit-boosting powerhouses, their true value depends entirely on the composition of your existing credit file. Here is a breakdown of why it won't do much for your current situation, and what it actually changes on your report.



The Reality of Rent Reporting
Credit scoring models (like the ones Equifax Canada uses) weigh different factors to calculate your score. Rent reporting primarily targets two buckets: Payment History (35%) and Credit Mix (10%).

Three Massive Caveats to Keep in Mind
If you are thinking about paying for KOHO's rent reporting (which is typically tied to their paid subscription tiers), keep these structural limitations in mind:



* It only reports to Equifax. As of right now, Canadian rent reporting services only feed data into Equifax Canada. TransUnion Canada does not currently include rental tradelines in its consumer scoring models. If a future lender decides to pull your TransUnion report, your rent history won't even exist there.

* Lenders look past the three-digit number. When you apply for major credit down the road (like a car loan or a mortgage), a computer algorithm looks at your score, but a human underwriter looks at your actual history. Underwriters want to see how you handle borrowed money. Seeing that you pay your rent on time is nice, but it doesn't carry the same weight as seeing you responsibly manage a larger, unsecured line of credit over several years.

* The "Double-Edged Sword" Risk. If you sign up for a rent reporting service and an automated payment glitch, a roommate dispute, or a bank delay causes your rent to register as late, that negative mark gets reported to Equifax and will actively tank the solid score you’ve built.


What Actually Moves the Needle For You?
Because you are already doing the heavy lifting by maintaining zero-to-low utilization on two cards, your score is already on the right track. The only two things that will significantly increase your score from here are:



* Time: The age of your oldest account accounts for roughly 15% of your score. There is no shortcut for this; you just need to let those two cards mature.

* Higher Limits: Eventually requesting a limit increase on your unsecured card (without a hard credit check, if possible) will naturally keep your utilization microscopic even if you spend a bit more in a given month.


The Verdict: Save your money. Rent reporting is a fantastic tool for someone who has absolutely zero credit history or is recovering from bankruptcy and can't get approved for a basic card. For someone who already has a clean, multi-card setup with perfect utilization, paying for rent reporting yields an incredibly low return on investment.

What do you think is a health range of fat intake that would basically ensure I can reliably give plasma? One time before (I think I was on keto; in the least my diet was much fattier than it is now) my plasma was rejected for too much fat

---

## Gemini

That rejection happens because of a condition called lipemia, where an abundance of chylomicrons (the large lipoprotein particles that transport dietary fat) turns the plasma from its normal clear, straw-colored liquid into a cloudy, milky substance. Because donation centers rely on visual and optical tests to screen for infectious diseases, highly cloudy plasma cannot be safely processed.



When you are in deep ketosis, your body relies heavily on circulating free fatty acids and triglycerides for fuel, making lipemic plasma much more likely—especially if a high-fat meal was consumed close to the appointment.


To reliably clear the screening process while maintaining excellent health, targeting the standard nutritional guidelines is your best bet, paired with strategic timing around your donation window.



1. The Daily Macronutrient Target
According to Health Canada and the Acceptable Macronutrient Distribution Ranges (AMDR), a healthy daily fat intake for adults is 20% to 35% of total daily calories.


For a standard 2,000 to 2,500 calorie baseline, that translates to roughly 45 to 95 grams of total fat per day. Keeping your baseline within this standard range keeps your resting serum triglycerides at a manageable level.


2. The Critical 24-Hour Protocol
Your baseline daily intake matters less than what you eat in the 4 to 24 hours immediately leading up to your donation. Chylomicrons peak in your bloodstream roughly 3 to 5 hours after eating a fat-rich meal.


To ensure clear plasma on donation day, follow this preparation timeline:



* The Night Before: Eat a low-fat, high-protein dinner. Avoid heavy saturated fats like fatty steaks, deep-fried foods, heavy creams, or large amounts of cheese.

* The Day Of (Pre-Donation Meal): Eat a meal that is virtually fat-free but rich in complex carbohydrates and lean protein (e.g., oatmeal with fruit, a turkey breast sandwich on whole wheat with no mayo, or chicken breast with rice).

* Hydration: Drink roughly 500 mL to 1 Liter of water in the 3 hours leading up to the appointment. Excellent hydration expands your blood volume, which optimizes blood flow and slightly dilutes the concentration of lipids in the sample.


Staying within the 20% to 35% daily range provides plenty of healthy fats for cellular and hormonal function, while moving away from a high-fat ketogenic baseline and timing your pre-donation meals will stop lipemia from derailing your next appointment.

## User

Would it help if I fasted or might it actually be harmful because I’ll enter ketosis

---

## Gemini

Fasting for a short window before your appointment can actually be counterproductive and increase the risk of another rejection.


While a brief fast (like missing a single meal) won't instantly throw a non-keto body into deep ketosis, it changes how your body mobilizes energy in a way that directly impacts blood plasma.


Here is why fasting isn't the best strategy for plasma donation, along with the specific risks involved:


1. Elevated Free Fatty Acids
When you fast for several hours, your insulin levels drop and your body begins breaking down stored body fat to use for energy via lipolysis. This process releases a steady stream of free fatty acids (FFAs) directly into your bloodstream.


While these aren't the exact same chylomicrons that turn plasma milky after a greasy meal, a high concentration of circulating fats from fasting can still cause visible turbidity (cloudiness) in the plasma, potentially triggering another lipemic rejection.


2. The Hydration and Blood Volume Issue
Fasting frequently goes hand-in-hand with mild dehydration. When you aren't consuming food, you lose the ambient hydration that comes from solid meals, and your body naturally excretes more water and sodium as glycogen stores deplete.


A lower blood volume makes your veins harder to access, slows down the apheresis machine cycle, and significantly increases the likelihood of a vasovagal reaction—causing you to feel lightheaded, dizzy, or nauseous during or immediately after the collection.


3. Post-Donation Recovery
Plasma donation removes a significant amount of water, proteins, and electrolytes from your system. If you go into the appointment with flatlined blood sugar and no recent nutrient intake, your body will struggle to stabilize after the draw, leaving you completely drained for the rest of the day.




The Ideal Compromise: Instead of fasting, aim for a "zero-fat" or "ultra-low-fat" window rather than a "zero-food" window.



Eating a meal consisting purely of lean protein and complex carbohydrates (like plain oatmeal, cream of wheat, toast with a bit of jam, or a dry turkey breast sandwich) 2 to 3 hours before you donate gives you the necessary blood sugar baseline and hydration benefits, keeps your energy up, and keeps dietary fats completely out of your bloodstream during the screening.
