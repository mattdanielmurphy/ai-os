The multiple prices you see in image.png for "Happier" (10% Happier) are a common, if confusing, quirk of how the Apple App Store displays data.


Apple requires developers to list every price point that is currently or has ever been offered for an app. When you look at that list in the App Store "Information" section, you are seeing a graveyard of past promotions alongside the current ones.


Why there are so many prices:

* Historical Data: Many of those lower prices (like the $77.99 or $92.99 annual options) might be from past holiday sales or "limited-time" offers that have since expired.

* Testing: Developers often run A/B tests to see if people are more likely to subscribe at $99.99 versus $95.99.

* Introductory Offers: Some of these prices are likely "introductory" rates meant only for the first year, after which they renew at the standard price.

* Grandfathered Rates: Existing users might be locked into an older, cheaper price that is no longer available to new users like your dad.


Which one is the real price?
The App Store list is not a menu you can choose from; it is just a record of what the developer has "registered" with Apple.
