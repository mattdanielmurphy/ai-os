For a horizontal compression to occur, the graph needs to shrink toward the y-axis, which happens when the horizontal scale factor is less than 1. This means:


$$\frac{1}{|q|} < 1 \implies |q| > 1$$


Evaluating the Options

* $p$ variables: These options are irrelevant because $p$ controls vertical stretch and compression.

## Gemini

It’s easy to feel like the sign shouldn't matter for the "squeeze," and in terms of the width of the waves, you’re right! But mathematically, the distinction between $q < -1$ and $-1 < q < 1$ is what separates a compression from a stretch.


Here is why that shift happens and why the "size" of the number is the key:


1. The Compression (The "Squeeze")
The horizontal compression is determined by the absolute value of $q$.



* If $|q| > 1$ (like $4$ or $-4$), the graph compresses.

* If $|q| < 1$ (like $0.5$ or $-0.5$), the graph stretches.


In your example, both $4$ and $-4$ have an absolute value of $4$, so they both compress the graph by the same amount.


2. The "Tiny Shift" (Phase Shift)
The reason the graph shifts when you swap $-4x$ for $4x$ is because of how the horizontal shift (phase shift) is calculated. The shift isn't just the number $r$; it’s actually:
$$\text{Phase Shift} = -\frac{r}{q}$$



* With $-4x + 3$: $\text{Shift} = -\frac{3}{-4} = +0.75$ (Shifts right)

* With $4x + 3$: $\text{Shift} = -\frac{3}{4} = -0.75$ (Shifts left)


When you changed the sign of $q$, you accidentally flipped the direction of the shift!


3. Why the choice is $q < -1$
In the context of your U5-Lessons 4/5/6 Quiz, the question asks which condition results in a compression.



* $-1 < q < 1$: This represents decimals and fractions like $0.5$ or $-0.2$. These make the period longer, which is a stretch.

* $q < -1$: This represents numbers like $-2, -3, -4$. Since their absolute value is greater than 1, they compress the graph.
