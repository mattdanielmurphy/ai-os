## Gemini

That trance-like, locked-in state is precisely what the pioneers of spiritual jazz were chasing. When a track stretches past the 15- or 30-minute mark, the traditional structure of jazz dissolves. The music stops being about a series of clever chord changes and becomes about a sustained modal drone, a repetitive rhythmic pulse, and a slow, collective climb toward catharsis.


If you want to lock into that specific wavelength while playing or listening, here is a breakdown of the definitive, long-form spiritual jazz tracks that master the art of the hypnotic groove.



The Architects of the Infinite Groove
Pharoah Sanders – "The Creator Has a Master Plan" (Karma, 1969)

* Runtime: ~32 minutes

* The Vibe: The ultimate blueprint for long-form spiritual ascension.

* The Trance Mechanism: Built entirely around a warm, looping two-note bassline played by Reggie Workman. Because the harmonic foundation never moves, the percussion layers (maracas, bells, shakers) can shift like sand while Sanders uses his saxophone to travel from peaceful, breathy meditations to ecstatic, screaming overtones.


Alice Coltrane – "Journey in Satchidananda" (Journey in Satchidananda, 1971)

* Runtime: ~6 minutes (though the album functions as one continuous suite)

* The Vibe: Earthbound, meditative, and deeply fluid.

* The Trance Mechanism: Driven by Majid Shabazz on the tamboura (an Indian drone instrument) and Cecil McBee’s looping, bluesy bassline. Alice’s harp plucks cascade over the top like water. It forces you to slow your internal clock down to match the pace of the drone.


Miles Davis – "Shhh/Peaceful" (In a Silent Way, 1969)

* Runtime: ~18 minutes

* The Vibe: Space-age, ambient, and hyper-focused.

* The Trance Mechanism: Tony Williams keeps a relentless, clockwork sizzle going on the hi-hat while the three electric pianos (Herbie Hancock, Chick Corea, and Josef Zawinul) create a shimmering, impressionistic fog. It’s an exercise in absolute restraint that locks the brain into a steady alpha-wave state.



Modern Continuity & Hypnotic Minimalism
If you are looking for contemporary projects that take this exact ethos and stretch it even further into pure minimalism, these are essential:


The Necks – Sex (1989) or Drive By (2003)

* Runtime: ~45 to 60 minutes (single-track albums)

* The Vibe: A mesmerizing, slow-burn spiral.

* The Trance Mechanism: This Australian trio doesn't play traditional spiritual jazz, but they are the masters of the long-form trance. They start with a simple, repeating piano chord and a walking bassline, staying on it for an hour. The changes are so microscopic and gradual that you don’t realize how intense the music has become until you are completely submerged in it.


Natural Information Society – Descension (Out of Our Constrictions) (2021)

* Runtime: ~75 minutes

* The Vibe: Psychedelic, rhythmic, and communal.

* The Trance Mechanism: Led by Joshua Abrams playing the guimbri (a Moroccan three-stringed lute), this group builds entire albums around traditional African hypnotic rhythms mixed with jazz horns. The continuous, cyclical thrum of the guimbri acts as a physical anchor, making it incredibly easy to lose track of time while playing along.

## Gemini

Yes, a hard-boiled egg is significantly more resilient than a raw egg. The difference comes down to basic physics and chemistry, changing how the egg responds to force, spinning, and impacts.


1. Structural Reinforcement (The Inside Matters)
While the calcium carbonate shell remains exactly the same on both eggs, the state of the interior completely changes the structural integrity.



* Raw Egg: The inside is a liquid. When a raw egg drops or hits an object, the shell takes the entirety of the localized impact force. Because there is no solid internal support, the shell easily flexes inward and cracks.

* Hard-Boiled Egg: Cooking denatures the proteins in the egg white (albumen) and yolk, cross-linking them into a tight, solid, elastomeric matrix. When a boiled egg experiences an impact, this solid interior acts as a shock absorber. It supports the shell from the inside, distributing the force of the blow throughout the entire volume of the egg rather than letting it concentrate at the point of impact.


2. Rotational Stability and Fluid Dynamics
If you spin both eggs on a table, the hard-boiled egg is much more resilient against losing its balance or stopping.



* Raw Egg: The liquid inside obeys fluid dynamics. When you try to spin it, the shell moves first, but the liquid inside lags behind due to inertia. This internal sloshing creates friction and destabilizes the spin, causing the egg to wobble and quickly come to a halt. If you tap a spinning raw egg to stop it for a second and let go, the internal liquid keeps rotating, causing the egg to start spinning again.

* Hard-Boiled Egg: Because it is a solid mass, it rotates as a single rigid body. It spins smoothly, efficiently, and can even stand up on its tip due to the gyroscopic effect (the dynamic lifting force).


3. The "Drop" Reality
Don't expect a hard-boiled egg to bounce like a rubber ball. If you drop it from a significant height onto a hard surface, the shell will still crack and shatter because the shell itself is brittle. However, unlike a raw egg which results in an immediate, catastrophic fluid spill, a hard-boiled egg will retain its shape and keep its contents entirely contained.
