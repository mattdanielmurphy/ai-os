The Momentum Factor
While kinetic energy is often lost in collisions (turning into heat or sound), linear momentum is always conserved in a closed system, regardless of whether the collision is elastic or inelastic.


Since you know the initial and final states of the first block, and the initial state of the second block, you actually have enough information to find that "missing" velocity.


Think about the total momentum before the impact:
$$p_{initial} = m_1v_{1i} + m_2v_{2i}$$

$$p_{final} = m_1v_{1f} + m_2v_{2f}$$

Is energy lost?
You mentioned feeling like no energy is lost. In physics, a collision where no kinetic energy is lost is called a perfectly elastic collision. Most real-world "head-on" collisions are actually inelastic, meaning some kinetic energy is converted into other forms.


To find out for sure, you’ll want to:



* Solve for the final velocity ($v_{2f}$) of the 8.60 kg block using the conservation of momentum.

* Calculate the total kinetic energy ($K = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$) of both blocks before the collision.

Because kinetic energy uses $v^2$, the math doesn't allow you to combine velocities before squaring. Mathematically:
