Getting a clean, high-quality version of *Not Going Out* Season 1 (the 2006 run featuring Megan Dodds as Kate) is tricky, especially if you are watching from Canada.  
Wikipedia

Here is what you need to know about the quality limitations and the best ways to get your hands on it.

The Quality Reality: No "True" HD Exists

Before buying or signing up for anything, it’s worth noting that **Season 1 was shot and mastered in Standard Definition (SD)** back in 2006.

It was originally broadcast in PAL 576i.  
Blu-ray.com

Because of this, **there is no true High Definition (HD) master** in existence. Any digital storefront claiming to sell it in "HD" is simply offering an upscaled version of the standard-definition file.

Option 1: Digital Purchase (Best Local Digital Option)

While subscription services are spotty, you can buy the digital files directly. These are cleanly compressed and save you the hassle of physical media:

**Apple TV & Google Play (Canada):** Both stores offer Season 1 for digital purchase. While still SD/upscaled, these are the cleanest, highest-bitrate digital files officially distributed in North America.

Option 2: UK Streaming Services (Best Streamed Quality)

If you already use a VPN to bypass geo-restrictions, you can stream it directly from the UK where the masters are kept in the best possible digital shape:

**U (formerly UKTV Play):** The UK broadcaster's free ad-supported platform has Series 1 streaming. Because they serve the local UK market, their encoder settings for older SD content are usually highly optimized compared to North American ports.

**BBC iPlayer:** Occasionally rotates the earlier seasons back into its active box sets.

Option 3: Physical Media (The Purist Route)

If you want to avoid compression artifacts and get the absolute best, uncompressed presentation of the original broadcast, the original DVD is the way to go:

You can find the **Series 1-7 DVD Boxset** or the standalone **Series 1 DVD** on eBay or imported via Amazon UK.

**The Catch:** These are UK imports, which means they are **Region 2 PAL discs**. To play them in Canada, you will need a region-free DVD player, or a standard computer disc drive using free media software like **VLC**, which bypasses region handshakes.  
Blu-ray.com

**Why not BritBox?**
If you've looked on BritBox Canada, you've likely noticed Season 1 is completely missing. Due to licensing oddities, their catalog for *Not Going Out* frustratingly starts at Season 2 (when Sally Bretton's character, Lucy, is introduced).

## Gemini
No, you can definitely do better than that.

While the show is stuck in Standard Definition, the file you have is heavily compressed and represents a noticeable drop even from SD broadcast/DVD quality.

Here is why your current file is lacking and what the actual ceiling looks like for this season.

Why Your Current File is Subpar

**The Resolution:** `640x352` is a heavily downscaled 16:9 widescreen resolution. The native PAL broadcast and DVD master is **`720x576`** (anamorphic 16:9). By watching at `640x352`, you are throwing away roughly 45% of the native vertical detail. On a modern screen or TV, this will look incredibly blurry and muddy.

**The Bitrate:** `~817 kbps` is very low. Even with efficient modern codecs, a sub-1000 kbps bitrate on a fast-paced live-action sitcom will result in noticeable compression artifacts, macroblocking (blocky patterns in dark scenes), and a "smeary" look whenever there is movement.

The Absolute Ceiling: What You *Should* Look For

If you want the best possible presentation of Season 1, you want to get as close to the **original DVD/broadcast master** as possible. You should look for files with the following specs:

Specification	Your Current File	What You Want (DVD Rip / WEB-DL)
**Resolution**	`640x352`	**`720x576`** (PAL Native) or **`1024x576`** (Square-pixel equivalent)
**Bitrate**	`~817 kbps`	**`2,000 to 5,000 kbps`**
**Format / Container**	Likely `.avi` or highly compressed `.mp4`	**`.mkv`** (specifically a lossless **DVD Remux** or a high-quality `H.264 / x264` rip)
How to Find the "Decent" Copy

If you are searching torrent indexes or digital sharing spaces, keep an eye out for these specific keywords in the release titles:

**`DVDRip` or `DVD.Remux`:** A "Remux" is a 1:1 bit-for-bit copy of the video data directly off the retail UK DVD disc. This will give you the maximum bitrate (~4,000 to 7,000 kbps MPEG-2) and the absolute cleanest native standard-definition image possible, free of any additional compression.

**`iP.DL` or `WEB-DL`:** These are direct digital rips downloaded from streaming platforms like BBC iPlayer or U (UKTV Play). While compressed using modern H.264, they are encoded at a much higher bitrate than your current file and will easily be in native `576p` widescreen.

**`x264` / `H.264` over `XviD` / `DivX`:** Older torrents from the late 2000s were often compressed using the outdated XviD codec to squeeze episodes into 175MB or 350MB files (which explains your 817 kbps file). Modern H.264 encodes of the DVD will look vastly superior even at similar file sizes.

If you can find a **DVD Remux (.mkv)** or a **576p WEB-DL**, the difference in clarity, color, and lack of blocky digital noise will be night and day compared to what you currently have.
