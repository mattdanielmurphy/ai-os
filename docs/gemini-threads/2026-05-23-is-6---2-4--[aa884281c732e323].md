No, they are not equal. We can prove this by simplifying the left side of the equation to match the denominator of the right side.


Step 1: Simplify the left side
Starting with the left side:
$$ \frac{\sqrt{6} - \sqrt{2}}{4} $$


We can extract a common factor of $\sqrt{2}$ from the numerator:
$$ \sqrt{6} = \sqrt{2}\sqrt{3} $$


Substitute this back into the expression and factor out the $\sqrt{2}$:
$$ \frac{\sqrt{2}\sqrt{3} - \sqrt{2}(1)}{4} $$
$$ \frac{\sqrt{2}(\sqrt{3} - 1)}{4} $$


Next, simplify the fraction. Since $4 = 2 \cdot 2$, and $2 = \sqrt{2} \cdot \sqrt{2}$, we can rewrite the denominator as $2\sqrt{2}\sqrt{2}$:
$$ \frac{\sqrt{2}(\sqrt{3} - 1)}{2\sqrt{2}\sqrt{2}} $$


Cancel out the common $\sqrt{2}$ from the numerator and denominator:
$$ \frac{\sqrt{3} - 1}{2\sqrt{2}} $$


Step 2: Compare the two sides
Now we can compare our simplified left side to your original right side:



* Left side: $$ \frac{\sqrt{3} - 1}{2\sqrt{2}} $$

* Right side: $$ \frac{-\sqrt{3} - 1}{2\sqrt{2}} $$


Because the numerators are different ($ \sqrt{3} - 1 $ evaluates to a positive number, while $ -\sqrt{3} - 1 $ evaluates to a negative number), the two expressions are not equal.

Alternative 2: An Interactive yt-dlp Bash Function
If you prefer sourcing from web streaming, you can use a quick bash/zsh function that wraps yt-dlp. This bypasses python-dependent tools like spotdl by using yt-dlp's built-in search parameters combined with a quick result readout.


Add this clean function to your ~/.zshrc or ~/.bashrc:


dl-album() {
    local query="$1"
    if [ -z "$query" ]; then
        echo "Usage: dl-album \"artist album name\""
        return 1
    fi

    echo "Searching top album/playlist results for: $query..."
    
    # 1. Search for playlists/albums specifically and list top 3 matches
    yt-dlp "ytsearch3:type=playlist $query" \
        --print "%(index)d. [%(uploader)s] %(title)s - %(url)s" \
        --flat-playlist

    echo -n "Enter the URL of the best match to download: "
    read -r target_url

    if [ -n "$target_url" ]; then
        # 2. Pull down the chosen album, extract audio, and match metadata
        yt-dlp -x --audio-format mp3 --audio-quality 0 \
            --yes-playlist \
            -o "%(playlist_title)s/%(playlist_index)s - %(title)s.%(ext)s" \
            "$target_url"
    fi
}

Why this route works smoothly:

* Zero Node/Web Dependencies: It doesn't need a local server or a browser to load.

* Explicit Filtering: By forcing type=playlist in the search query, it ignores isolated track videos and specifically hunts for grouped albums.

* Structured Output: The -o flag automatically creates a folder named after the album and prefixes the tracks numerically (01 - Track Title.mp3) so your media player doesn't scramble the track order.

If you want to pull directly from the Soulseek network completely over the command line, there are two distinct ways to do it.


You can use a dedicated Node-based CLI client that runs directly in your terminal shell, or you can run a popular self-hosted background daemon called slskd and interact with it purely using an API wrapper or custom shell aliases.



Alternative 1: soulseek-cli (Pure Terminal Client)
There is a standalone tool called soulseek-cli built explicitly for this. It handles authentication, system keychain storage, searches, and down-filtering straight from the terminal prompt.


1. Installation
Since it's built on Node, you install it globally via npm:


npm install -g soulseek-cli

(Note: If you run into keychain compilation errors on a headless machine, make sure libsecret or its equivalent is installed via your package manager).

You only need to authenticate once. It will securely store your credentials in your native OS system keychain:


soulseek login

Alternatively, you can skip the keychain prompt by setting runtime environment variables:


export SOULSEEK_ACCOUNT="your_username"
export SOULSEEK_PASSWORD="your_password"

* To download high-quality MP3 albums:

## Gemini

Making a low-sodium version of Swiss Chalet sauce is highly effective because standard recipes rely heavily on commercial chicken bouillon cubes and salt, which load it up with sodium.


The signature profile of Chalet sauce relies on an intricate mix of warm spices (ginger, allspice/cloves), sharp poultry herbs (sage, thyme, savory), and a distinct vinegar/citrus tang against a light tomato-chicken background.


By swapping commercial bouillon for a homemade or zero-sodium stock and modifying the hidden sodium contributors like Worcestershire, you can cut the sodium content down drastically while retaining that authentic herby, tangy bite.


Low-Sodium Swiss Chalet Sauce

* Prep time: 5 minutes

* Cook time: 10 minutes

* Yield: Approximately 2.5 cups


Ingredients
The Base & Acids



* 2 cups no-salt-added chicken broth (or water mixed with zero-sodium chicken bouillon packets)

* 1/4 cup no-salt-added tomato juice (or 2 tsp no-salt tomato paste mixed into 1/4 cup water)

* 2 tbsp white vinegar (adjust up slightly if you like it extra tangy)

* 1 tsp fresh lemon juice


The Warm Spices & Aromatics



* 1 1/2 tsp paprika (gives it that classic orange-red hue)

* 1/2 tsp onion powder

* 1/4 tsp garlic powder

* 1/4 tsp ground ginger

* 1/4 tsp ground allspice (or a tiny pinch of ground cloves)

* 1/2 tsp sugar (or honey, to balance the acids)


The Herbs



* 1 bay leaf

* 1/2 tsp dried sage

* 1/2 tsp dried summer savory (or poultry seasoning if savory is unavailable)

* 1/4 tsp dried thyme

* 1/4 tsp dried basil


The Condiments & Thickener



* 1/2 tsp Worcestershire sauce (Note: Contains ~50mg sodium per tsp, but spread over 2.5 cups, it adds negligible sodium while providing critical umami. Omit if strictly zero-sodium is required).

* 6 drops Tabasco or Frank’s RedHot (adds a subtle vinegary kick without pushing up the salt)

* 2 tbsp cornstarch mixed into 2 tbsp cold water (slurry)

* 1 tbsp unsalted butter (whisked in at the end to give it that authentic, silky mouthfeel)



The Method



Chef's Tip for Umami: Because standard Swiss Chalet sauce depends heavily on MSG and high-sodium bouillon for its savory depth, omitting the salt can sometimes make herbal sauces taste a bit flat. If you find the finished sauce lacks a punch, add a tiny splash of apple cider vinegar or an extra drop of lemon juice; brightness often mimics the sharpness of salt on the palate. Enjoy it hot alongside a rotisserie chicken and fries.
