## User

I’ve got cool ranch Doritos and ghost pepper powder. I would like to know if there a way I can make the chips spicy but relatively evenly spiced with the ghost pepper powder

---

## Gemini

To get an even coating of ghost pepper powder on Cool Ranch Doritos without ending up with a "minefield" of spice, you need to solve the surface-tension problem. Standard chips are dry, so powder usually just falls to the bottom of the bag.


Here is the most effective method to ensure the powder adheres evenly:


The "Aerosol" Technique
The goal is to create a microscopic layer of moisture for the powder to grab onto without making the chips soggy.



* The Mist: Place your chips in a large stainless steel bowl or a heavy-duty gallon freezer bag. Give them one or two very light mists of neutral oil spray (like avocado or canola oil). If you don't have a spray, a tiny amount of melted butter flicked from a spoon works, but it's harder to keep even.

* The Dilution: Ghost pepper powder is incredibly potent. To avoid hot spots, mix your desired amount of ghost pepper with a "carrier." Since these are Cool Ranch, mixing the ghost pepper with a little bit of buttermilk powder or even a pinch of fine salt helps increase the volume of the spice, making it easier to distribute.

* The Shake:

* Sift the spice mixture over the chips using a fine-mesh strainer if you have one.

* Toss the chips gently in the bowl or inflate the bag with air, seal it, and shake vigorously for 30 seconds.




* The Set: Spread the chips out on a baking sheet for 5 minutes. This allows the oil to stabilize and the powder to "set" into the existing ranch seasoning.


Pro-Tips for Ghost Peppers

* Heat Sensitivity: Ghost pepper heat builds over time. Start with a much smaller amount of powder than you think you need—you can always add more, but you can't subtract it once it's bonded to the ranch dust.

* Safety: Wear gloves if you have them, or use tongs to eat them. Ghost pepper dust on the fingers is a one-way ticket to a very painful eye-rubbing incident later.


Do you usually prefer a "slow burn" that builds up, or are you looking for that immediate punch of heat?

## Gemini

Fair enough. If the aerosol method is too much work, you can go the "low-effort, high-velocity" route. It’s less precise, but it gets the job done in seconds.


The "Lazy Bag" Method

* The Air Gap: Open your bag of Doritos, but don't eat any yet. You need that extra "headspace" in the bag.

* The Drop: Dump your ghost pepper powder straight into the bag.

* The Trap: Pinch the top of the bag shut, trapping as much air inside as possible so the bag is puffed up like a balloon.

* The Shake: Shake the bag violently for about 20 seconds. Rotate it, flip it, and give it some real force.


Why this works: The chips act as their own "tumbler," and the existing oil/dust on the Cool Ranch seasoning will grab some of the ghost pepper.


The "Dip" Alternative
If shaking the bag sounds like it'll result in too much "spice dust" in the air (which can make you cough), just put a small pile of the ghost pepper powder on a side plate. Lick a chip, dab it into the powder, and proceed. It’s 100% even because you control the dose per bite.


Just a heads-up: if you go the bag-shake route, try not to inhale immediately after opening it—that ghost pepper dust will be airborne.
