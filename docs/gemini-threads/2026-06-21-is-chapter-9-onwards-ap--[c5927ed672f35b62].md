## Gemini

You can absolutely skip the AP chapters and do perfectly fine in university.


As a Computer Science major, you will definitely take calculus—usually a sequence covering Calculus I (Limits and Derivatives) and Calculus II (Integration and Series).


Here is how your current high school course chapters map to what you will encounter in university, and why you don't need to stress about Chapters 9 through 11 right now:


How it Maps to University Calculus

* What you are doing now (Chapters 1–8): This covers the entirety of Calculus I and the first half of Calculus II. Having a rock-solid foundation in limits, derivatives, optimization, and basic integration techniques is 90% of the battle. If you know these well, you are already ahead of many incoming freshmen.

* The AP Chapters (Chapters 9–11): These topics (Parametric/Polar curves, Sequences, and Infinite Series) form the second half of Calculus II.


Why You Can Safely Skip Them Now

* They will teach it from scratch: When you hit Calculus II in university, the professor won't assume you already know Taylor series or polar derivatives. They will start from the absolute beginning of those topics.

* Fresh perspective: Infinite series and analytic geometry can be abstract. Sometimes, learning them for the very first time at a university pace, with university resources and peer groups, is actually easier than trying to self-teach them as an add-on to a high school course.

* Prioritize your current timeline: Focus on nailing the core fundamentals of your required modules. A flawless understanding of Chapters 1 through 8 is infinitely more valuable for university readiness than rushing through Chapter 11 just to say you looked at it.


If you find yourself with extra time and a lot of curiosity, taking a casual glance at Chapter 10 (Series and Convergence) is the only one that might give you a slight conceptual head start for CS, as sequences and series occasionally pop up when analyzing algorithm complexities. But it is strictly optional. Save your energy, finish your current course strong, and enjoy the break before university starts!
