## Gemini

Actually, it is exactly the opposite—decaf usually needs a finer grind than caffeinated beans to get the same extraction time.


It feels counterintuitive, but the decaffeination process (whether water, $CO_2$, or Ethyl Acetate) alters the physical structure of the bean before it ever hits the roaster. It pre-cooks and expands the bean's internal cellular matrix, making the finished coffee much more brittle, porous, and fragile.


When you grind decaf, two things happen that mess with a pour-over:



* The shatter effect: Because the beans are so brittle, they shatter easily in the grinder, creating a massive amount of "fines" (microscopic dust-like coffee particles).

* High porosity: Water passes through the porous structure of individual decaf grounds incredibly fast.


If you use your standard caffeinated grind size for decaf, the water will often channel right through, resulting in a fast, sour, and hollow brew. Conversely, if your grinder produces too many fines, they can migrate to the bottom of the paper filter and stall your drawdown completely, leaving you with a bitter, over-extracted mess.


How to Adjust Your Grinder
To hit the sweet spot for a medium-roast pour-over, use these adjustments as your starting point:
