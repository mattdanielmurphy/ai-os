Isolate the terms with $z$:

$\frac{7}{z} - \frac{1}{z} = 2$

*

$6 = 2z$

To solve the equation $\frac{5}{x} = \frac{x-5}{x-2} + \frac{1}{x}$ algebraically, follow these steps:


1. Identify the Lowest Common Denominator (LCD)
The denominators are $x$ and $x-2$. Therefore, the LCD is $x(x-2)$.
Note: $x \neq 0$ and $x \neq 2$ because these values would make the denominators zero.


2. Multiply by the LCD
Multiply every term in the equation by $x(x-2)$ to clear the fractions:
$$x(x-2) \cdot \frac{5}{x} = x(x-2) \cdot \frac{x-5}{x-2} + x(x-2) \cdot \frac{1}{x}$$


This simplifies to:
$$5(x-2) = x(x-5) + 1(x-2)$$


3. Expand and Simplify
Expand both sides:
$$5x - 10 = x^2 - 5x + x - 2$$
$$5x - 10 = x^2 - 4x - 2$$


4. Solve the Quadratic Equation
Move all terms to one side to set the equation to zero:
$$0 = x^2 - 4x - 5x - 2 + 10$$
$$0 = x^2 - 9x + 8$$


Factor the quadratic:
$$(x-8)(x-1) = 0$$


This gives the solutions:
$x = 8$ and $x = 1$


Since neither of these values violates the initial restrictions ($x \neq 0, x \neq 2$), both are valid solutions. The option $x=8, x=1$ is correct.
