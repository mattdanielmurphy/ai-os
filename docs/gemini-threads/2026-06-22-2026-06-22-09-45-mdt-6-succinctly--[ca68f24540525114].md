## Gemini

By convention, no—but physically, yes. It depends on whether you are talking about conventional current or electron flow.



1. Conventional Current (High to Low)
In physics and engineering, conventional current is defined as the movement of positive charge.



* Positive charges are naturally repelled by high potential (positive terminals) and attracted to low potential (negative terminals).

* Therefore, conventional current always flows from high potential to low potential.


2. Electron Flow (Low to High)
In actual metallic wires, the charges that physically move are electrons, which carry a negative charge.



* Because they are negative, electrons are attracted to high potential and repelled by low potential.

* Therefore, the actual flow of electrons moves from low potential to high potential.






Summary: If you are analyzing a standard circuit diagram, assume current flows from high to low potential unless specified otherwise.
