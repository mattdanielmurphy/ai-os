## Gemini

It’s incredibly frustrating when Universal Control (which is what those "Link to Mac or iPad" toggles govern) decides to reset itself. Since you're on macOS, this is usually less about a deliberate setting change and more about a corrupted configuration file or a handshake failure between iCloud devices.


Here are the most likely reasons this keeps toggling off:


1. Bluetooth and Wi-Fi Handshake Issues
Universal Control requires a constant, stable "handshake" via Bluetooth to detect proximity and Wi-Fi for the data transfer.



* The Fix: Toggle Bluetooth and Wi-Fi off and then back on for both devices. If you use a VPN or a firewall (like Little Snitch or LuLu), ensure they aren't blocking local network discovery services like sharingd.


2. Corrupted Handoff Preference Files
If the settings won't "stick," the property list file (plist) that stores these preferences might be corrupted.



* The Fix: You can force macOS to regenerate these files. Open Terminal and run:
defaults delete com.apple.universalcontrol
Then, restart your Mac and re-enable the settings in the menu you have open in the screenshot.


3. iCloud Session "Staleness"
Because these settings are tied to your iCloud account, a sync error can cause the Mac to think it's no longer authorized to share the pointer.



* The Fix: Sign out of iCloud on the Mac, restart, and sign back in. This is a pain, but it often resolves persistent "Link to Mac" toggling issues.


4. Sleep/Wake Dynamics
Sometimes when a Mac enters a deep sleep (Hibernation mode), it drops the Universal Control state and fails to re-initialize it upon waking.



* The Check: If you notice this happening specifically after your Mac has been asleep for a long time, try disabling "Put hard disks to sleep when possible" in Settings > Energy Saver (or Battery).


Quick Troubleshooting Checklist:

* Proximity: Ensure the devices are within 30 feet (10 meters) of each other.

* Apple ID: Double-check that both devices are signed into the exact same Apple ID and have Two-Factor Authentication enabled.

* iPad Side: If you're linking to an iPad, ensure Settings > General > AirPlay & Handoff > Cursor and Keyboard is also toggled ON there.

## Gemini

Yes, you can absolutely do this. The trick is to "hide" the page from your standard view first, then explicitly "enable" it within your specific Focus mode.


Step 1: Hide the Page from your Main View
First, you need to create the page and then hide it so it doesn't show up during "normal" phone use.



* Create the page by dragging apps or widgets to a new blank screen.

* Long-press any empty area on your Home Screen until the apps start to jiggle.

* Tap the horizontal row of dots (the page indicator) at the bottom above the dock.

* You will see a grid of all your Home Screen pages. Uncheck the circle under the specific page you want to hide.

* Tap Done in the top right.


Now, that page is invisible during your regular daily use, but it still exists in your system.


Step 2: Link the Hidden Page to a Focus Mode
Next, you tell iOS to bring that hidden page "to the front" only when your Focus is active.



* Go to Settings > Focus.

* Select the Focus mode you want to use (e.g., Work, Fitness, or a Custom one).

* Scroll down to the Customize Screens section.

* Tap Choose (it's the middle preview image that looks like a Home Screen).

* You will see your Home Screen pages again. Select the page you previously hid (the one you unchecked in Step 1).

* Tap Done.


How it Works in Practice

* When Focus is OFF: That specific page is hidden. You can swipe through your regular pages, and it simply won't be there.

* When Focus is ON: iOS overrides your default settings and inserts that page into your Home Screen rotation.



Pro Tip: If you want to be really strict, you can select only that specific page in the Focus settings. When the Focus is active, your iPhone will literally only show that one page, preventing you from swiping over to distracting apps on your other screens.

While many new adapters are marketed as 140W PD 3.1, this refers to the input they can accept. In reality, most high-end hubs reserve about 15W for internal operations, meaning the maximum output delivered to your laptop is typically 125W.
