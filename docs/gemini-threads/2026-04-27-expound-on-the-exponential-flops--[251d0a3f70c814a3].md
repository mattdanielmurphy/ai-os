The graph featured in The Social Dilemma illustrates a fundamental divergence in "processing power." It typically plots two lines: the exponential growth of computing power (measured in FLOPS, or Floating-point Operations Per Second) against the relatively flat, linear line of human biological evolution.


The Core Premise: Brain vs. Silicon
The documentary uses this visualization to argue that while human biology—our instincts, dopamine response systems, and cognitive processing speeds—has remained largely unchanged for tens of thousands of years, the technology designed to capture our attention has grown in complexity at an staggering rate.



* Human Baseline: Our brains evolve on a timescale of millennia. Our ability to process information, resist social validation, or identify manipulation is essentially the same as it was for our ancestors.

* Technological Acceleration: Silicon-based processing has followed various iterations of Moore’s Law, doubling in performance roughly every two years for decades.



Has the Trend Continued?
The short answer is yes, but the nature of the growth has shifted. Since the documentary’s release in 2020, we have moved from the era of "General Computing" into the era of "Massive AI Scaling."


1. The Shift to AI-Specific FLOPS
Traditional CPU growth has slowed due to physical limits in transistor shrinking. However, the industry pivoted to GPUs (Graphics Processing Units) and TPUs (Tensor Processing Units). These chips are specialized for the parallel math required for neural networks.



* Between 2012 and 2022, the amount of compute used to train the largest AI models increased by a factor of roughly 100 million.

* This growth is significantly faster than the historical Moore’s Law trend, doubling roughly every six months rather than every two years.


2. The GPT Era
When The Social Dilemma was filmed, Large Language Models (LLMs) were still relatively niche. Since then, the deployment of models like GPT-4 and Claude has pushed the demand for FLOPS to an industrial scale. We are no longer just looking at a graph of "raw speed," but a graph of "algorithmic intelligence."


3. Hardware Specialization
Current hardware, such as the NVIDIA H100 or Blackwell architectures, utilizes "Tensor Cores" specifically designed for matrix multiplication. This has allowed the "FLOPS" line on that graph to kick upward even more steeply than predicted in 2020.

## Gemini

It is indeed. Jo Brand's prize submission is a stylized, modern take on a phrenology bust, a tool once used to promote one of the most influential and damaging pseudosciences of the 19th century.


What was Phrenology?
Developed by German physician Franz Joseph Gall in the late 1790s, phrenology was based on the idea that the brain is a collection of distinct "organs," each responsible for a specific personality trait or mental faculty (such as "cautiousness," "wit," or "veneration").


Phrenologists believed that:



* The more developed a faculty was, the larger that part of the brain would grow.

* The skull would then bulge to accommodate this growth.

* By feeling the bumps and indentations on a person's head—a process called cranioscopy—a practitioner could supposedly determine their character and intelligence.



Why It Is Considered Pseudoscience
While phrenology correctly anticipated the idea of localization of function (that different parts of the brain do different things), it failed as a science for several reasons:



* Anatomical Falsehood: The shape of the skull does not actually mirror the shape of the brain.

* Lack of Evidence: There was no empirical data to support the link between skull bumps and specific behaviors.

* Confirmation Bias: Practitioners would ignore evidence that contradicted their findings and emphasize any details that seemed to fit.


The Link to Racism
The most harmful aspect of phrenology was its role in scientific racism. In the 1800s, it was used to provide a "biological" justification for European superiority and the oppression of others.



* Justifying Slavery and Colonialism: Phrenologists would measure the skulls of different ethnic groups and claim that Western Europeans had "superior" cranial developments in areas related to intellect and morality, while other groups were "innately" suited for servitude or lacked higher reasoning.

* Social Control: It was used to "diagnose" criminals or the "insane," often suggesting that their behavior was an unchangeable result of their biology rather than social or environmental factors.


Modern Context
Today, phrenology is used in pop culture—like the ceramic head in the image—primarily as a kitsch decorative item or a symbol of Victorian eccentricity. However, the term is still frequently invoked in modern discussions as a warning against "neo-phrenology," where people attempt to use modern technologies like facial recognition or AI to inaccurately "predict" personality or criminality based on physical appearance.

## Gemini

Structured outputs are a way to force an AI model to respond in a very specific, predictable format—typically JSON—rather than in plain, conversational text.


Think of it like giving the AI a form to fill out instead of a blank piece of paper. This is essential for developers who want to plug AI responses directly into an app, a database, or a piece of code without worrying about the AI adding "here is your info" or changing the way it labels data.



How it Works
When you use structured outputs, you provide a Schema (a blueprint). This blueprint tells the model exactly which "keys" or categories it must use and what type of data goes into them.



* Standard Output: "The weather in London is 15 degrees Celsius and it's currently raining."

* Structured Output:
{
  "location": "London",
  "temperature": 15,
  "unit": "celsius",
  "condition": "rainy"
}




Why it Matters

* Reliability: In the past, models might occasionally forget a bracket or change "temperature" to "temp," which would break a computer program. Structured outputs guarantee the model follows the rules 100% of the time.

* Ease of Integration: Since the output is organized, another program can instantly read the data and, for example, update a weather icon on a website or log a price in a spreadsheet.

* Complex Data Extraction: You can give a model a massive wall of text and ask it to "extract all dates, names, and prices into a table format." Structured outputs ensure that table is perfectly formatted every time.


Where You See It
In tools like Google AI Studio (which you are currently looking at), the "Structured Outputs" menu allows you to define this blueprint using JSON Schema or a visual editor. This ensures that when you hit "Run," the model doesn't just talk to you—it gives you data you can actually use in a build.
