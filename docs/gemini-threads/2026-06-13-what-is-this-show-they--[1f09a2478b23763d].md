The premise of Love Is Blind is a massive social experiment designed to see if people can fall in love and get engaged without ever actually seeing each other.


Here is exactly how the insanity unfolds:


Phase 1: The Pods

* Blind Dates: Single men and women live in separate quarters and go on speed dates in purpose-built "pods."

* The Setup: They can talk, laugh, and cry together through a speaker in the wall, but a completely opaque glass barrier keeps them from seeing what the other person looks like.

* The Proposal: Over a few weeks, people narrow down their choices. If they decide they want to spend the rest of their lives together, they have to propose—still entirely sight unseen. Only after the proposal do they finally meet face-to-face for the first time.


Phase 2: The Real World

* The Honeymoon: The newly engaged couples are whisked away to a luxury resort to test their physical chemistry and see if their emotional connection holds up.

* Moving In Together: They return to the real world, move into a shared apartment complex, and are forced to integrate into each other's actual lives. This means meeting the parents, dealing with finances, discovering each other's living habits, and dealing with any lingering drama from other people they dated in the pods.


Phase 3: The Wedding

* The Ultimate Ultimatum: Within just a matter of weeks from first meeting, the couples walk down the aisle at a real wedding in front of their friends and family.

* The Twist: Neither person knows for sure if the other is going to say "I do" or "I do not" until they are standing at the altar.


It is a recipe for absolute chaos, which explains why Greg Davies found himself trapped on his couch binge-watching it for three days straight!
