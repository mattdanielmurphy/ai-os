To scale up to a 250g total mass using your preferred 17g coffee dose, your brewing ratio shifts slightly to a $1:14.7$ total ratio.


To adapt this for an iced pour-over, we keep the exact same $40%$ ice to $60%$ hot water split rule to ensure the melting ice dilutes the concentrated brew perfectly without washing it out.

## Gemini

On the J-Max, dropping down from 2.8 is exactly the right move. Because the J-Max has a massive 90 clicks per rotation—with each individual click moving the burrs by a tiny 8.8 microns—you have an incredibly precise resolution.


A setting of 2.8 (2 full rotations + 8 numbers) sits at the upper, coarser boundary for a standard V60. Since your drawdown was way too fast and weak, you need to step down into the medium-fine territory to force the water to slow down and interact with the grounds.


The Targeted Step Down
Since you are brewing an iced pour-over with a reduced hot water volume (150g), your contact time is naturally abbreviated. You want to compress the grind size significantly to make up for that lost time.



* For the Hot Recipe: Try dropping down to 2.2 (2 full rotations and 2 numbers).

* For the Iced Recipe: Drop even further down to 1.8 (1 full rotation and 8 numbers).


The Math & Data Points
By jumping from 2.8 down to 2.2 or 1.8, you are dropping the physical gap between your burrs by roughly 350 to 500 microns total.


This massive increase in surface area will provide the required fluid resistance to slow down your 10-second concentric pours. Keep your 90°C kettle temp and 17g mass completely constant, and watch the clock during your next brew:



* If your total drawdown lands between 2:15 and 2:30 for the iced version, your J-Max has successfully restricted the flow enough to pull out the core sweetness without choking.


Let me know how the drawdown time responds to tightening the dial on this next run!
