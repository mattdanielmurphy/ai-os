## Gemini

Ah, the classic circular dictionary trap. Defining a word using the exact root word you’re trying to look up is incredibly annoying.


"Interstitial" basically just means the spaces in between things.


If you look at a brick wall, the bricks aren't touching perfectly; there are tiny gaps filled with mortar. Those gaps are interstitial spaces.


Here is how it's actually used in the wild:



* Biology: Interstitial fluid is the liquid sitting in the microscopic spaces between your body's cells.

* Tech/Web: An interstitial ad is that annoying full-screen popup that appears in between clicking a link and the next page loading.

* Materials Science: An interstitial defect is when a stray atom wedges itself into the gaps between the regular grid of atoms in a crystal.



Dictionaries That Don't Suck
Most standard dictionaries (like standard Google search results or Oxford) prioritize linguistic precision and brevity over actual readability. If you want definitions written for humans, check these out:



* Collins COBUILD Dictionary: Instead of fragmented phrases, it explains words in full, natural sentences (e.g., "Interstitial means relating to spaces between things"). It was originally designed for language learners, but it's brilliant for anyone who wants a straightforward answer.

* Wiktionary: Because it’s crowdsourced, it tends to skip the academic preen and jump straight to practical usage, synonyms, and context.

* American Heritage Dictionary: Often includes "Usage Notes" and clear examples that show the word in a real sentence, which usually does a better job of explaining the concept than the definition itself.
