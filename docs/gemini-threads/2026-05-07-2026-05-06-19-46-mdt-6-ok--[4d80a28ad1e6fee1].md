## Gemini

The short answer is that iMessage is end-to-end encrypted, but there is a major catch involving how you back up your phone.


How iMessage Encryption Works
When you send an iMessage to another Apple user, the content is encrypted so that only the sender and recipient can read it. Not even Apple can see the text or photos while they are in transit.


However, if you use iCloud Backup (which most people do), a copy of your encryption key is often stored within that backup. If Apple is served a legal warrant, they can technically access your backup and, by extension, your messages.


To make iMessage truly "private," you have to enable Advanced Data Protection in your iCloud settings. This moves the encryption keys to your device only, meaning Apple no longer has a way to decrypt the data.



Is Telegram Encrypted?
Telegram is often misunderstood. By default, it is less secure than iMessage or WhatsApp:



* Cloud Chats (Default): Standard Telegram chats are encrypted between your device and the Telegram server, but not end-to-end. Telegram holds the keys. This allows you to access your history on multiple devices, but it means Telegram employees or governments could theoretically access your data.

* Secret Chats: You must manually start a "Secret Chat" to get end-to-end encryption. These chats are device-specific and won't show up on your computer if you started them on your phone.



What is Actually Encrypted?

---

## Gemini

Yes, images (along with videos and other attachments) are fully end-to-end encrypted in iMessage, just like your text messages.


When you send a photo:



* Your phone generates a separate, random encryption key specifically for that image.

* The image is encrypted and uploaded to Apple’s servers.

* The encryption key for that image is then encrypted using the recipient's public key and sent as part of the message.

* Only the recipient's device can decrypt that key to download and view the image.


Apple technically hosts the "blob" of data on their servers while it's in transit, but because they don't have the key, it’s just scrambled gibberish to them.


Important 2026 Context: iPhone to Android
As of May 2026, the situation for "green bubble" chats has changed significantly:



* Legacy SMS/MMS: If you send a photo via standard MMS to an Android user, it is not encrypted.

* New Encrypted RCS: With the release of iOS 26.5 this month, Apple has finally enabled end-to-end encrypted RCS. If you and the Android user both have the latest updates, photos sent between you will now be encrypted, marked by a small lock icon in the chat.


The "iCloud" Loophole (Again)
The same rule applies to photos as it does to text: if you use standard iCloud Backup without Advanced Data Protection enabled, a copy of the keys required to unlock those photos is stored in your backup. If someone gets access to that backup, they can see the photos. Enabling Advanced Data Protection is the only way to ensure the keys never leave your physical devices.
