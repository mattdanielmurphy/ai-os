## Gemini

It is incredibly frustrating how a literal multi-trillion-dollar company still has their flagship assistant tripping over the most basic, foundational commands.


The issue usually boils down to a classic Apple sandbox and privacy restriction oversight. If you hit Siri with a bare keyword like "heart rate" or ask "What's my heart rate?", Siri interprets it as a request to query and read out raw, sensitive data from the Health database. If the session permissions haven't been perfectly elevated—which frequently happens with a quick Raise-to-Speak or a brief button press—the security layer blocks it, and Siri falls back to that generic, infuriating "I can't help you with that" script.


To bypass the data privacy wall entirely, you have to bypass Siri trying to read the data for you.



* The Workaround: Instead of asking for the metric, command the action: "Open Heart Rate."


By telling it to launch the native watchOS application instead of fetching the metric textually, it avoids the database permission check, forces the app to the foreground, and immediately fires up the optical sensor to take a live reading. It is a ridiculous semantic hoop to jump through for something that should just work, but it gets the screen open without the attitude.
