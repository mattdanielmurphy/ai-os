## Gemini

Eli Lilly's experimental obesity drug retatrutide represents a major shift in the metabolic landscape, acting as a "triple agonist." While current blockbusters like Wegovy target one hormone receptor (GLP-1) and Zepbound targets two (GLP-1 and GIP), retatrutide targets three: GLP-1, GIP, and glucagon.


The ongoing TRIUMPH Phase 3 clinical trial program has recently posted historic results, positioning the drug as the most potent weight-loss medication evaluated to date.



Pivotal Phase 3 Readouts
TRIUMPH-1 (Obesity & Overweight)
Topline results released in May 2026 from this massive 80-week trial of 2,339 adults (without diabetes) showed unprecedented weight reduction:



* The 12 mg Dose: Participants lost an average of 70.3 lbs (28.3%) of their body weight at 80 weeks.

* 104-Week Extension: For a subset of participants with severe obesity (baseline BMI ≥35) followed for two years, the average weight loss reached 85.0 lbs (30.3%).

* The 4 mg Dose: Even on the lowest dose—achieved with just a single escalation step—participants lost an average of 19% of their body weight, offering a highly effective option with fewer side effects.

* Obesity Reversal: 65.3% of participants on the highest dose fell below the clinical threshold for obesity (BMI <30) by the end of the trial.


TRIUMPH-4 (Obesity & Knee Osteoarthritis)
Data released from this 68-week trial focused on adults with obesity and knee osteoarthritis:



* Participants achieved an average weight loss of 28.7% on the 12 mg dose.

* Crucially, it drastically reduced physical pain, with up to 14% of patients reporting they were completely free of knee pain by the end of the study.



The Triple-Hormone Mechanism
Retatrutide’s exceptional efficacy stems from its three-pronged approach to metabolic pathways:


                  ┌───► GLP-1 ────► Suppresses appetite & delays gastric emptying
                  │
Retatrutide ──────┼───► GIP ─────► Enhances insulin secretion & stabilizes energy storage
                  │
                  └───► Glucagon ─► Increases energy expenditure & targets liver fat

The addition of the glucagon receptor agonist is the key differentiator. It boosts the body's energy expenditure (metabolic rate) and directly targets lipid metabolism in the liver. Early research also suggests this triple-hormone profile may help preserve lean muscle mass more effectively than older GLP-1 single-agonists.



Safety, Side Effects, and Tolerability
The side effect profile is largely consistent with existing incretin-based therapies, primarily manifesting as gastrointestinal distress during the dose-escalation phases:



* Nausea & Vomiting: Nausea affected 42.4% of patients on the 12 mg dose (vs. 14.8% on placebo). Vomiting occurred in about 25% of patients at the highest dose.

* Diarrhea & Constipation: Reported by roughly 32% and 26% of high-dose participants, respectively.

* Discontinuation: Due to these GI side effects, the treatment discontinuation rate for the 12 mg cohort was 11.3% in TRIUMPH-1.

* Dysesthesia: Concerns from Phase 2 regarding skin sensitivity/tingling diminished, with fewer instances reported in the larger Phase 3 trials.


What’s Next for the TRIUMPH Program?
Eli Lilly is tracking several other specialized Phase 3 trials within the TRIUMPH framework, with further readouts expected later this year:



* TRIUMPH-2: Evaluating efficacy in adults with type 2 diabetes.

* TRIUMPH-3: Assessing outcomes for individuals with established cardiovascular disease.

* TRIUMPH-7: Investigating impact on chronic low back pain.


Lilly plans to submit retatrutide for FDA approval by the end of 2026, targeting a commercial launch in 2027.

If you look into a Canadian site for a retatrutide trial, there are a few country-specific realities to keep in mind:

However, because these trials require frequent, mandatory in-person clinic visits (often every 2 to 4 weeks for lab work and vitals), traveling long distances usually makes this impractical unless you live close to the border of another province.

* The Provincial Health Care Connection: While the trial sponsor (Eli Lilly) fully pays for the experimental drug, the blood panels, and the specialized clinical care, you still need a valid provincial healthcare card (like AHCIP in Alberta) for standard medical screenings and administrative intake.

Do you get paid for any studies?

---

## Gemini

Yes, you do get paid, but the structure depends entirely on the type of study. Clinical trials break this down into two distinct categories: reimbursement (covering what you spend out-of-pocket) and compensation (paying you for your time and inconvenience).


Because retatrutide trials (like the TRIUMPH program) are Phase 3 trials testing a drug that offers a direct potential health benefit, the financial setup looks very different from a Phase 1 "healthy volunteer" trial.



1. Out-of-Pocket Reimbursement (Standard for TRIUMPH)
For Phase 3 outpatient trials, the primary financial goal of the sponsor (Eli Lilly) is to ensure that participating costs you $0. They will directly cover or reimburse you for:



* Travel and Mileage: Kilometres driven to and from the clinic site, public transit fares, or taxi/rideshare costs.

* Parking: Hospital or clinic parking vouchers or receipt reimbursements for every mandatory visit.

* Meals: A per diem or meal allowance if your visit requires fasting or takes several hours for extensive blood panels and metabolic tracking.


2. Direct Compensation (The "Stipend")
You will generally receive a stipend for your time and the inconvenience of the study's demands.



* The Phase 3 Rate: In late-stage outpatient trials like TRIUMPH, this typically ranges from $50 to $150+ per clinic visit. It is meant to account for the hours you spend getting weighed, having blood drawn, doing ECGs, and meeting with the research staff.

* Prorated Payments: By Canadian research ethics rules, you are paid incrementally as you go (e.g., loaded onto a pre-paid reloadable visa card after each visit). A study cannot withhold your money until the very end to force you to stay if you want to drop out due to side effects.



Contrast with Phase 1 Trials: The massive $3,000 to $10,000 payouts you hear about online are reserved for Phase 1 trials. Those require healthy volunteers to live inside a locked clinic for days or weeks at a time, undergoing intense, frequent testing with zero medical benefit to themselves. Because retatrutide is already in Phase 3, those high-paying cloistered studies are long over.




The Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) Factor
If you participate in a trial in Canada, any compensation for your time (the stipends, not the parking/mileage receipts) is legally considered income.



* If your total stipend payouts from the research facility exceed $500 in a calendar year, the institution running the site will ask for your Social Insurance Number (SIN) and issue you a T4A slip.

* You are required to declare this on your annual Canadian tax return.


When you speak with a study coordinator during a screening call, one of the documents they are legally mandated to hand you is the Informed Consent Form. This document will have a specific section explicitly outlining the exact dollar amount you will receive per visit, the mileage rate, and how the funds are distributed.

## Gemini

The idea of hanging out in a high-tech facility for a couple of weeks and walking away with a massive cheque is exactly why Phase 1 trials have an almost mythical status among students, freelancers, and people with flexible schedules.


But there is a very direct relationship here: the payout matches the restriction and the underlying risk.



The Risk: "First-in-Human"
When a drug enters Phase 1, it has typically only been tested in lab dishes and animal models. Phase 1 is the very first time it touches a human bloodstream.


The primary goal isn't to see if the drug works; it is purely to find out how much of it a human body can take before it becomes toxic.


While serious catastrophes are incredibly rare due to ultra-strict modern protocols, the risk is real. The testing process involves:



* Dose Escalation: They start with a tiny dose in the first group of volunteers. If nobody has a severe reaction, the next group gets a higher dose, and so on, until they hit the limit of tolerability.

* Predictable Misery: Even when things go "well," volunteers frequently experience intense side effects—severe nausea, pounding headaches, extreme fatigue, skin rashes, or wildly fluctuating blood pressure—because the researchers are actively pushing the limits of the compound.



The Reality of In-Patient Life
The money isn't just for the physical risk; it’s compensation for a total loss of freedom.



* The Lockdown: You are confined to a specialized clinical ward (like the Clinical Investigation Unit at the University of Alberta Hospital) for anywhere from 3 to 14+ consecutive days. You cannot leave to go for a walk, step outside for fresh air, or grab food.

* Total Control: Every single variable is strictly engineered. Your exact meal portions are weighed and timed. Your fluid intake is tracked.

* Constant Testing: You are essentially a human pincushion for the duration of the stay. It is common to have a matching set of intravenous lines or an indwelling cannula to pull blood samples every hour on the dot—including through the night while you are trying to sleep. You'll also be hooked up to continuous ECG monitors and blood pressure cuffs.



Are They Hard to Get Into?
Yes, but not for the reasons you’d think. They aren't hard because of high intelligence or skill requirements; they are hard because the entry criteria require you to be a flawless "baseline" human.


To qualify as a "Healthy Volunteer," your screening process is incredibly rigorous:

In clinical trial terminology, you fit a distinct, highly sought-after category: Otherwise Healthy Adults with Obesity.


When pharma companies finish their very first Phase 1 trials on healthy, lean volunteers to make sure a new metabolic drug doesn't do anything immediately dangerous, they immediately move to what is called a Phase 1b or Phase 2a trial.


For these specific studies, they want people who have a high BMI but are otherwise an absolute "clean slate."



Why You're a "Unicorn" for Metabolic Research
Finding someone who has a high BMI but doesn't have a cascading list of other metabolic complications is actually pretty tough for recruiters. Researchers love a profile like yours because:



* No "Confounding Variables": If someone is taking blood pressure medication, metformin for diabetes, or cholesterol pills, it muddies the water. The researchers can't tell if a side effect or a positive result is from the new drug or an interaction with an existing medication.

* Zero Substance Interference: Your lack of alcohol, drugs, and caffeine is a massive asset. Clinical trials have to control for everything—caffeine alone alters heart rate, blood pressure, and metabolic output, which can mess with delicate Phase 1 data panels.



How Phase 1b / 2a Trials for Obesity Work
If you enroll in an early-stage study specifically designed for obesity, it blends the high-payout structure of Phase 1 with the target audience of Phase 3:



* The In-Patient Stay: These often still involve spending a few days locked in a metabolic ward. Researchers might use specialized equipment like a metabolic chamber (a sealed room that measures exactly how much oxygen you breathe in and carbon dioxide you breathe out) to see if the new drug is actively shifting your resting metabolic rate.

* The Payout: Because they are still asking you to give up your freedom, stay in a facility, and undergo intensive hourly blood draws and ECGs, these early-stage target-population trials still offer those significant four-figure payouts.



Where to Look in Alberta
Since you are a clean slate mechanically but meet the BMI threshold, you wouldn't look at general "healthy volunteer" registries. Yes, .gov is a top-level domain restricted exclusively to the United States government.


However, even though it is a US government website, ClinicalTrials.gov is the undisputed global gold standard registry for clinical trials.


International medical journals and regulatory bodies (including Health Canada) require pharmaceutical companies to register their trials on this specific site before they even begin. Because of that, almost every legitimate clinical trial happening in Canada, Europe, or anywhere else in the world is listed there.


When you use it, you just use their location filters to find the Canadian sites.



The Canadian Equivalents
If you want to use registries that are strictly Canadian and won't show you US locations by default, you have a few solid options:


1. Health Canada's Clinical Trials Database

* What it is: The official registry maintained by the Canadian federal government.

* The Catch: It only lists Phase 1, 2, and 3 trials that are actively importing or manufacturing investigational drugs for human use in Canada. It's highly accurate for safety, but the interface is a bit clunky compared to the US site.

* Where to find it: Search for the Health Canada Clinical Trials Database via Canada.ca.


2. Canada Clinical Trials (Canadian Institutes of Health Research)

* What it is: A cleaner, more public-facing search engine funded by the Canadian government to help patients find active studies across the country.

* Where to find it: canadaclinicaltrials.ca


3. Alberta-Specific Portals
Since you are in Alberta, you can skip the national noise entirely and look at the registries managed right in your backyard by the universities and Alberta Health Services (AHS):



* BeTheCure.ca: Run by the University of Alberta, the University of Calgary, and AHS. This is the absolute cleanest tool for finding local metabolic and health studies looking for participants in Edmonton and Calgary.


If you are looking for global cutting-edge drugs (like the next wave of Lilly or Novo Nordisk compounds), the US .gov registry will still usually have the most detailed, up-to-the-minute contact info for the study coordinators running the local sites in Edmonton.

## Gemini

By default, yt-dlp already intelligently selects the best quality available. It uses the bv*+ba/b format selector, which attempts to download the best video-only track (that has a corresponding audio track) and the best audio-only track, then merges them.


If you want to customize your "best" format, here are the most common parameters.


1. The Standard "Best Quality" Command
If you just want to let the software handle the quality selection (which is recommended), you don't actually need to provide a format parameter. However, to explicitly ensure the best quality:


yt-dlp -f "bv*+ba/b" [URL]


* bv*: Best video (including those without audio).

* +ba: Plus best audio.

* /b: Fallback to the best single file (if a combined video+audio is better or the only option).



2. Best Quality with MP4 Compatibility
Many users prefer MP4 over the default WebM/MKV containers for better compatibility with hardware players or editing software. Use this to force an MP4 output:


yt-dlp -f "bv*[ext=mp4]+ba[ext=m4a]/b[ext=mp4]/b" [URL]


* This tells yt-dlp to prioritize MP4 video and M4A audio. If that combination isn't available, it falls back to the best single-file MP4, and finally the best available format of any kind.



3. Best Quality with Format Sorting (-S)
Instead of forcing a specific extension, you can use the -S (sort) flag to define your own priority. This is the modern, preferred way to control quality.


Example: Best video, prioritizing H.264 codec and 1080p resolution


yt-dlp -S "vcodec:h264,res:1080" [URL]


* vcodec:h264: Prefers H.264 video.

* res:1080: Prefers 1080p resolution.



4. How to See What is Available
If you aren't sure which format ID or quality is "best" for a specific video, you can list all available formats before downloading:


yt-dlp -F [URL]

This will print a table showing the ID, EXT, RESOLUTION, and CODEC. You can then pick the ID you want and download it directly:


yt-dlp -f [ID] [URL]

Important Tips:

* Install FFmpeg: To merge separate video and audio streams (which is how yt-dlp achieves the highest quality), you must have FFmpeg installed and in your system's PATH.

* Recoding: If you download a format that isn't your preferred container (like downloading a WebM but wanting an MP4), you can add --recode mp4 to your command to have yt-dlp automatically convert it after the download.
