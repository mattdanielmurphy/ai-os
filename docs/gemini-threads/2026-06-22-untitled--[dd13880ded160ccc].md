## Gemini

The issue with the current calculation is that it overlooks how pushing down on the broom handle changes the forces at play.


Two key things need to be adjusted:



* The Normal Force ($F_N$) is higher than gravity ($mg$): Because Barney is pushing downward and forward along the handle, he is pressing the broom harder into the floor.

* Components matter: Only the horizontal component of his push overcomes friction, while the vertical component increases the normal force (and therefore increases the friction itself).


Here is how to set up the problem correctly using a Free Body Diagram approach.



1. Identify the Components of the Applied Force ($F_a$)
Barney pushes with force $F_a$ at an angle of $41^\circ$ relative to the horizontal:



* Horizontal component (forward): $F_{ax} = F_a \cos(41^\circ)$

* Vertical component (downward): $F_{ay} = F_a \sin(41^\circ)$



2. Set Up the Force Balance Equations
Because the broom moves at a constant speed, the acceleration in both the horizontal and vertical directions is zero.


Vertical Direction ($\Sigma F_y = 0$)
The upward forces must equal the downward forces:
$$F_N = mg + F_{ay}$$
$$F_N = mg + F_a \sin(41^\circ)$$


Horizontal Direction ($\Sigma F_x = 0$)
The forward force must equal the backward friction force ($F_f$):
$$F_{ax} = F_f$$
$$F_a \cos(41^\circ) = \mu F_N$$



3. Substitute and Solve for $F_a$
Substitute the expression for $F_N$ into the horizontal equation:
$$F_a \cos(41^\circ) = \mu \big(mg + F_a \sin(41^\circ)\big)$$


Now, expand and rearrange the equation to isolate $F_a$:
$$F_a \cos(41^\circ) = \mu mg + \mu F_a \sin(41^\circ)$$
$$F_a \cos(41^\circ) - \mu F_a \sin(41^\circ) = \mu mg$$
$$F_a \big(\cos(41^\circ) - \mu \sin(41^\circ)\big) = \mu mg$$


$$F_a = \frac{\mu mg}{\cos(41^\circ) - \mu \sin(41^\circ)}$$



4. Plug in the Values

* $m = 1.1 \text{ kg}$

* $g = 9.8 \text{ m/s}^2$

* $\mu = 0.45$


$$F_a = \frac{0.45 \times 1.1 \times 9.8}{\cos(41^\circ) - 0.45 \sin(41^\circ)}$$


$$\mu mg = 4.851$$
$$\cos(41^\circ) \approx 0.7547$$
$$\sin(41^\circ) \approx 0.6561 \implies 0.45 \times 0.6561 \approx 0.2952$$


$$F_a = \frac{4.851}{0.7547 - 0.2952} = \frac{4.851}{0.4595} \approx 10.56\text{ N}$$


Rounded to three significant figures, the required pushing force is $10.6\text{ N}$.
