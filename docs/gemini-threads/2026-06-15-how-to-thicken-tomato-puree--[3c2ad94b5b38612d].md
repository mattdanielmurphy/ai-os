## Gemini

To thicken tomato puree, you essentially need to remove excess water or add body without changing the core flavor. The best approach depends on whether you have time to simmer it or need an immediate fix.


1. Reduction (The Best Method for Flavor)
If you aren't in a hurry, this is the superior way to thicken. Simmering concentrates the sugars and acidity, intensifying the tomato flavor.



* How: Keep the puree on low heat, uncovered. Stir occasionally to prevent sticking.

* Result: A deep, rich, and naturally thick sauce.


2. The Slurry Method (Fastest)
If you need immediate thickness without boiling it down further, use a starch.



* How: Mix 1 part cornstarch with 1 part cold water (or broth) until smooth. Whisk this mixture into your hot puree.

* Result: Thickens almost instantly. Keep in mind that too much can dull the vibrant tomato color or give it a slightly glossy texture.


3. Tomato Paste
This adds thickness and a concentrated "umami" punch.



* How: Whisk in a tablespoon or two of tomato paste.

* Result: Improves body and adds a deeper savory note. This is common in Italian-style ragus.


4. Vegetable Puree
For a healthier body that doesn't rely on starch.



* How: Puree cooked carrots, cauliflower, or roasted bell peppers and stir them into the tomato puree.

* Result: Adds sweetness (carrots) or creaminess (cauliflower) and significantly increases volume and thickness.



Which one to choose?

* For pasta sauces: Use Reduction or Tomato Paste. They maintain the authentic mouthfeel.

* For soups or stews: Use Vegetable Puree or Cornstarch. They are more forgiving in larger batches.

* For a quick fix: Use a Cornstarch Slurry. Just add it slowly so you don't over-thicken.


Pro-tip: If you are simmering, avoid high heat. Rapid boiling can cause the puree to splatter everywhere and potentially "burn" the sugars, leading to a bitter aftertaste. Keep it at a gentle bubble.

Question: The function $y = Ce^{-3t} - 7$ solves the differential equation $y'' + 4y' = -3y - 21$.


Options:



* false

* true


Work:
$y = Ce^{-3t} - 7$
$y' = -3Ce^{-3t}$
$y'' = 9Ce^{-3t}$


Substitute into the differential equation:
$\text{LHS} = 9Ce^{-3t} + 4(-3Ce^{-3t}) = -3Ce^{-3t}$
$\text{RHS} = -3(Ce^{-3t} - 7) - 21 = -3Ce^{-3t} + 21 - 21 = -3Ce^{-3t}$


$\text{LHS} = \text{RHS}$


Correct Answer: true (2nd choice)

Question: Consider the differential equation $\frac{1}{\cos x} \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{e^{\sin x}}{2y}$ with initial condition $(0, 0)$. First solve this to find $y(x)$, and then use it to answer this question: What is $y(\pi)$?


Options:



* $\pm 1$

* $0$

* $\pm 2$

* $\pm 3$


Work:
$2y , dy = e^{\sin x} \cos x , dx$
$\int 2y , dy = \int e^{\sin x} \cos x , dx$
$y^2 = e^{\sin x} + C$


Using $(0,0)$:
$0^2 = e^{\sin 0} + C \implies 0 = 1 + C \implies C = -1$
$y^2 = e^{\sin x} - 1$


At $x = \pi$:
$y^2 = e^{\sin \pi} - 1 = e^0 - 1 = 1 - 1 = 0$
$y = 0$


Correct Answer: 0 (2nd choice)

Question: A certain wolf population, $p(t)$, is governed by the differential equation: $\frac{dp}{dt} = 0.4p(1 - p)$, where $p$ is the population (in thousands), and $t$ is the time in years. Options:



* 922

* 884

* 905

* 936


Work:
$\frac{dp}{dt} = 0.4p(1-p), \quad t_0 = 0, \quad p_0 = 0.8, \quad \Delta t = 0.5$


Step 1 ($t = 0.5$):
$p_1 = 0.8 + 0.4(0.8)(1 - 0.8)(0.5) = 0.8 + 0.032 = 0.832$

$p_2 = 0.832 + 0.4(0.832)(1 - 0.832)(0.5) = 0.832 + 0.027955 = 0.859955$


Step 3 ($t = 1.5$):
$p_3 = 0.859955 + 0.4(0.859955)(1 - 0.859955)(0.5) = 0.859955 + 0.024086 = 0.884041$


Step 4 ($t = 2.0$):
$p_4 = 0.884041 + 0.4(0.884041)(1 - 0.884041)(0.5) = 0.884041 + 0.020502 = 0.904543$

Question 6

* 0.2689

* -0.6394

* 0.1967

* 0.1962


Work:
$f(x) = 5x - \cos(x)$
$f'(x) = 5 + \sin(x)$
$x_{n+1} = x_n - \frac{5x_n - \cos(x_n)}{5 + \sin(x_n)}$


$x_0 = -4$
$x_1 = -4 - \frac{5(-4) - \cos(-4)}{5 + \sin(-4)} \approx -4 - \frac{-20.65364}{4.24320} \approx 0.8674$
$x_2 = 0.8674 - \frac{5(0.8674) - \cos(0.8674)}{5 + \sin(0.8674)} \approx 0.8674 - \frac{3.69033}{5.76274} \approx 0.2270$
$x_3 = 0.2270 - \frac{5(0.2270) - \cos(0.2270)}{5 + \sin(0.2270)} \approx 0.2270 - \frac{0.16067}{5.22507} \approx 0.1962$


Correct Answer: 0.1962 (4th choice)



Question 7
Question: If the local Linear approximation of $f(x) = 3\sin x + e^{3x}$ at $x = 2$ is used to find the approximation for $f(1.9)$, then the % error of this approximation is


Options:



* Greater than 15%

* Between 0% and 5%

* Between 5% and 10%

* Between 11% and 15%


Work:
$f(x) = 3\sin x + e^{3x} \implies f(2) = 3\sin(2) + e^6 \approx 406.157$
$f'(x) = 3\cos x + 3e^{3x} \implies f'(2) = 3\cos(2) + 3e^6 \approx 1209.166$


Linear approximation $L(x) = f(2) + f'(2)(x - 2)$:
$L(1.9) = 406.157 + 1209.166(1.9 - 2) = 406.157 - 120.917 = 285.240$


Actual value:
$f(1.9) = 3\sin(1.9) + e^{5.7} \approx 2.839 + 298.867 = 301.706$


Percentage Error:
$% \text{ error} = \left| \frac{301.706 - 285.240}{301.706} \right| \times 100% \approx \left| \frac{16.466}{301.706} \right| \times 100% \approx 5.46%$


Correct Answer: Between 5% and 10% (3rd choice)
