## Gemini

It's a really common belief that loading up on calcium-rich foods like milk will kick your nail growth into overdrive, but the short answer is no, drinking more milk won't make your fingernails grow faster.


Here is what's actually happening under the surface.


The Role of Calcium
Your nails are made of a tough, layered protein called keratin, not calcium. While calcium is present in tiny amounts in the nail plate to help keep it rigid, adding extra calcium to your diet won't act as a growth accelerator. The classic white spots people often spot on their nails (which many assume is a calcium deficiency from not drinking enough milk) are usually just minor matrix injuries—essentially the nail equivalent of a small bruise from bumping your finger.


Why They Might Actually Be Growing Faster
If you aren't imagining it, there are a few scientifically backed reasons your nails might be picking up the pace right now:



* The Season: Nails grow measurably faster in the summer than in the winter. The increased circulation from warmer weather delivers more nutrients to the nail matrix (the root under your cuticle where growth happens).

* Daylight and Circulation: More daytime light exposure and general physical activity during this time of year naturally boost blood flow, which feeds the nail bed.

* Overall Nutrition: If drinking more milk is part of a general upgrade to your overall protein and calorie intake, that can support healthier growth. Keratin requires amino acids, so a well-nourished body simply has more raw materials to work with.


On average, fingernails grow about 3.5 millimeters a month, and it takes around six months to replace a whole nail from root to tip. You're likely just seeing the natural summer growth spurt in action.
