## Gemini

It is incredibly frustrating trying to find those 3-inch Dremel Saw-Max discs in person. Because of that proprietary arbor size, most big-box stores just don't carry them on the shelves, and you usually have to resort to ordering them online.


The good news is that switching to your larger grinder is a much easier fix. That missing "end screw on thing" is officially called an angle grinder flange nut (or lock nut).


Since you are already at the store, you can grab a replacement right now:



* The Size You Need: If it's a standard 4.5-inch or 5-inch angle grinder, the spindle uses a universal 5/8"-11 thread size.

* Where to Look: Head to the power tool accessories aisle (right by the grinding and cutting wheels).

* What to Buy: Look for a "Universal Grinder Flange Nut Kit" or a brand-specific replacement. Stores like Canadian Tire sell standard replacement nuts (like the Mastercraft 5/8"-11 lock nut), and Home Depot stocks options like the Makita or Milwaukee flange nut kits. They usually run between $5 and $18.



Tip: Make sure you have both the inner flange (the bottom piece that sits on the grinder before the disc goes on) and the outer lock nut (the top piece that screws down). If you are missing both parts, look specifically for a "kit" that includes both pieces so your disc sits flat and safe.



If you can't spot them on the shelf, just ask an associate for a replacement angle grinder lock nut, and they should be able to point you right to them.

Ok so what I’m doing is grinding off some bolts. Cutting them and grinding them down. What do I want here?

## Gemini

Do not buy any of the discs hanging on that red board. Those specific Diablo discs are X-LOCK discs. You can tell by the weird, star-shaped pattern in the center instead of a round hole. They are designed for specialized quick-change grinders and will not fit a standard threaded spindle like your Jobmate.


Since you are cutting bolts off and then grinding the remaining stub flat, you actually need two different types of wheels (or one specific hybrid option). Look on the nearby shelves for standard discs with a completely round 7/8-inch center hole and grab these:


1. For Cutting the Bolts Off: A 4.5" Cut-Off Wheel

* What to look for: Look for packaging that says "Cut-Off" or "Trimming". The disc will be very thin—usually 1/16-inch or 0.045-inch thick.

* Why: These slice through metal like butter, but they are fragile. Only use them straight-on to slice the bolt head or shank off. Never push on the side of a thin cutting wheel, or it can shatter.


2. For Grinding the Leftovers Flush: A 4.5" Flap Disc OR Grinding Wheel
Once the bolt is cut, you need to switch wheels to smooth the rest down to the surface. You have two choices here:



* Option A: Flap Disc (Recommended): Look for a disc covered in overlapping pieces of sandpaper (usually 40, 60, or 80 grit). Flap discs grind quickly, are much easier to control for a flush finish, and leave a cleaner surface without gouging the surrounding metal.

* Option B: Thick Grinding Wheel: Look for a rigid wheel labeled "Grinding" that is 1/4-inch thick. These are tough and meant for heavy material removal using the face/side of the wheel.


Quick Checklist Before You Pay:

* Ensure the center hole is perfectly round (7/8" diameter), not star-shaped.

* Ensure the label says 4-1/2" to match your Jobmate guard.

* Grab your 5/8"-11 flange nut kit from the hardware section so you can actually mount them!

I can’t get one disc for both parts?

---

## Gemini

You technically can get one disc to do both, but you have to buy a specific type labeled as a "Combination" (Cut & Grind) disc, or a thick grinding wheel (usually 1/8" to 1/4" thick).


If you see a disc labeled "2-in-1" or "Combination" with a regular 7/8-inch round hole, grab that. You can use it straight-on to cut the bolt off, and then tilt it slightly to grind the stub flush using the face of the wheel.


What to avoid for doing both tasks:



* Do not use a standard thin "Cut-Off" wheel (1/16" or 0.045") to grind. They are strictly for straight slicing. Pushing on the flat side of a thin cutting wheel puts lateral pressure on it, which can cause the disc to instantly shatter.


If they don't have a specific "Combination" disc on the shelf, your safest single-disc workaround is to buy a standard 1/8-inch or 1/4-inch thick metal grinding wheel. It will be a bit slower and make a wider, messy cut when you chop the bolt off, but it is heavily reinforced so you can safely use it for both slicing and grinding flat.

Ok just getting flap and cut off discs. What grit?

---

## Gemini

For the cut-off discs, thickness is what matters rather than grit. Grab a pack of 1/16-inch or 0.045-inch thin discs (often labeled as "Metal/Stainless").


For the flap discs, you want a coarse or medium grit because you are doing heavy stock removal to flatten those bolt stubs down:



* 60 Grit (Highly Recommended): This is the best all-around option for what you are doing. It will grind the remaining metal down quickly while still leaving a relatively smooth, clean finish without gouging deep scratches into the surrounding surface.

* 40 Grit: If the bolts are massive or you have a ton of them to do, grab 40 grit instead. It is ultra-coarse and aggressively tears through metal fast, though it will leave the metal looking a bit more scratched up.


The ideal plan: Slice the bolt heads or shanks off straight-on with your thin cut-off wheel. Then, swap to a 60 grit flap disc, hold the grinder at a slight angle (around 15 degrees), and pass back and forth over the stub until it is completely flush with the surface.


Make sure the packaging for both explicitly says it fits a 7/8" arbor and matches your 4-1/2" grinder size!
