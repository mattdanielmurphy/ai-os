## Gemini

To understand this solution, it helps to look at the trailer as a lever system where the wheels act as a pivot point (the fulcrum). The scale measures the upward normal force required to keep the trailer level at the hitch.


When the boat moves backward, it shifts the center of gravity closer to the wheels, which reduces the "downward" torque on the hitch side, causing the scale reading to drop.



1. Establish the Torque Equilibrium
In both positions, the system is in static equilibrium, meaning the clockwise torque ($\tau_c$) must equal the counter-clockwise torque ($\tau_{cc}$).


Using the wheels as the pivot:



* Scale Force ($F_N$): Pushes up at the hitch, $6.0\text{ m}$ away from the wheels.

* Weight ($F_g$): Pulls down at the center of gravity, at an unknown distance $d_1$ from the wheels.


The general equation is:
$$F_N \cdot (6.0) = F_g \cdot d_1$$


2. Compare the Two Positions
The solution sets up two specific equations based on the scale readings (converting mass to Newtons using $g = 9.80\text{ m/s}^2$):



* Position 1: Scale reads $48\text{ kg} \rightarrow F_{N1} = 48 \cdot 9.8 = 470.4\text{ N}$
$$6.0 \cdot F_{N1} = F_g \cdot d_1 \quad \text{--- (Equation 1)}$$

* Position 2: The boat moves $0.15\text{ m}$ back. Scale reads $37\text{ kg} \rightarrow F_{N2} = 37 \cdot 9.8 = 362.6\text{ N}$. The new distance is $d_1 - 0.15$.
$$6.0 \cdot F_{N2} = F_g \cdot (d_1 - 0.15) \quad \text{--- (Equation 2)}$$


3. Solve by Subtraction
To find the mass, we need $F_g$. The easiest way is to subtract Equation 2 from Equation 1. This cancels out the unknown distance $d_1$:


$$(6.0 \cdot F_{N1}) - (6.0 \cdot F_{N2}) = (F_g \cdot d_1) - (F_g \cdot d_1 - 0.15 \cdot F_g)$$
$$6.0 \cdot (F_{N1} - F_{N2}) = 0.15 \cdot F_g$$


4. Final Calculation
Now, isolate $F_g$ (the total weight of the boat):
$$F_g = \frac{6.0 \cdot (470.4 - 362.6)}{0.15} = 4312\text{ N}$$


To get the mass ($m$), divide the weight by gravity:
$$m = \frac{F_g}{g} = \frac{4312}{9.8} = 440\text{ kg}$$


Answer: A ($440\text{ kg}$).
