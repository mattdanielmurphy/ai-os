Strictly speaking from a data or math perspective, no—you wouldn't round $99.6%$ up to $100%$ because rounding to the nearest whole percent gives you $100%$.


However, in an academic setting, achieving a $100%$ on a final grade when your calculated average is a $99.6%$ (or even a $99.38%$) happens all the time. Teachers and grading software handle this in a few specific ways:



* Standard Rounding Rules: Most automated gradebooks (like Brightspace/D2L) are configured to round to the nearest whole integer for final transcripts. Since $99.6 \ge 99.5$, it mathematically rounds up to $100%$.

* Teacher Discretion & Mastery: When a student demonstrates complete mastery across the board, teachers routinely bump a near-perfect grade to a flat $100%$. Looking at your overall performance—with straight $100%$ marks across almost every single chapter test, quiz, and project—that $99.38%$ in the test category is functionally a perfect run.

* Weighting: If your final exam or certain milestone projects carry heavy weight and sit at a flawless $100%$, they easily pull a $99.6%$ category average up to a perfect finish for the final course mark.


In short: mathematically it's a standard round-up, and academically, it is completely justified.
