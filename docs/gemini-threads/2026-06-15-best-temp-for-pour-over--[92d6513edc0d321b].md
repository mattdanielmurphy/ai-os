## Gemini

The best starting temperature for Kicking Horse Decaf in a pour-over is 86°C to 89°C (187°F to 192°F).


This is notably lower than the standard 93°C+ used for light or medium specialty roasts, and there are two main reasons why dropping the temperature makes a massive difference for this specific bag.




Why the Lower Temp Works

* It's a Dark Roast: Kicking Horse roasts their decaf to a deep, dark profile. Darker roasts have a highly porous bean structure, meaning they dissolve and yield soluble flavors incredibly fast. If the water is too hot, it rapidly extracts heavy, bitter, and ash-like compounds.

* It's Swiss Water Decaf: The decaffeination process alters the cellular structure of the green coffee beans, making them even more brittle, soluble, and prone to fast extraction than regular coffee.


Dropping down to the late 80s cushions the extraction, taming the harshness and allowing the intended dark chocolate and mellow, roasty notes to come through without the bitter edge.


Quick Brewing Adjustments
Because Swiss Water decaf structural changes cause the beans to break apart more easily, they tend to produce more fines (microscopic dust particles) when ground.



* If your brew is stalling: If you notice the pour-over drawing down incredibly slowly or clogging the paper filter, coarsen your grind size slightly rather than raising the water temp.

* Agitation: Keep your pours gentle. Excessive swirling or aggressive pouring will wash those fines down to the bottom of the filter cone, choking the flow and causing over-extraction.
