## Gemini

Whatever you do, do not put Drano (or any chemical drain cleaner) in your toilet. It is a common mistake, but Drano is not designed for toilets. The chemical reaction generates significant heat which can crack the porcelain of the bowl or melt PVC pipes. Furthermore, if the clog doesn't clear, you’re left with a basin full of toxic, caustic water that makes it dangerous for you or a plumber to finish the job manually.


The "slow flush" followed by a delayed self-drain suggests you have a partial blockage further down the trap or the drain line. Here is the step-by-step approach to fix this:


1. The Dish Soap & Hot Water Trick
Since the water eventually goes down, you have enough room to try this before reaching for tools.



* Squirt a generous amount of dish soap (about $1/4$ cup) into the bowl. It acts as a lubricant.

* Let it sit for 10 minutes.

* Pour a bucket of hot tap water (not boiling—boiling water can crack the porcelain) into the bowl from about waist height. The pressure and the soap often slide the obstruction through.


2. The Flange Plunger
If the soap doesn't work, use a plunger. Ensure you are using a flange plunger (the one with the extra rubber sleeve tucked inside the bottom) rather than a flat sink plunger.



* Make sure there is enough water in the bowl to cover the rubber head.

* Get a tight seal and push/pull vigorously. The "pull" is often more important for breaking up the clog than the "push."


3. Check the Tank (The "Lever" Issue)
You mentioned having to hold the lever down. While the slow drain is a clog issue, the lever struggle might be a separate mechanical problem in the tank:



* Open the lid and check the flapper chain. If there is too much slack, the flapper won't lift high enough to let out a full "gulp" of water.

* Adjust the clip so there is only about $1/2$ inch of slack.


4. The Toilet Auger (The Pros' Secret)
If a plunger fails, skip the chemicals and get a toilet auger (also called a closet auger). It’s a cable inside a protective tube that won't scratch your porcelain. It is specifically designed to navigate the "S" curve of a toilet and is much more effective than a standard snake.


When to call a plumber:

## Gemini

65°C is perfectly safe for your toilet.


The danger zone begins when you use water that is near boiling (above 80°C or 90°C). Porcelain is a ceramic material that is very strong but brittle; it handles gradual heat well, but thermal shock—the rapid expansion caused by an extreme temperature jump—is what causes it to crack.


Since your bathroom is likely around 20°C, a jump to 65°C is well within the tolerance levels of standard plumbing. Most residential water heaters are set between 49°C and 60°C, so using the hottest water from your tap is a reliable and safe "sweet spot."


Best Practices for the Pour:

* Avoid the "Boiling Kettle" Temptation: If you’re heating water on the stove, pull it off long before it starts to bubble. If it's too hot to touch comfortably, it's plenty hot for the clog.

* The Depth Factor: Pouring from waist height provides a bit of mechanical force (head pressure) that helps push the lubricant and heat into the trap where the obstruction is likely sitting.

* Watch the Level: Only pour if the water level in the bowl has subsided enough to handle the extra volume. You don't want to overflow a bowl that already has dish soap in it.


If the water goes down but the flush still feels "weak" or "lazy" afterward, you might want to check the small holes under the rim of the bowl (the jets) to see if they are clogged with mineral deposits, as that can also prevent a forceful flush.

Since you're using npm link, here is the most likely hierarchy of what went wrong and how to fix it:


1. The Execution Bit was Stripped
If you moved files, changed branches, or did a fresh build that overwrote the entry point file (usually specified in your package.json "bin" field), the filesystem might have lost the "executable" permission.


The Fix:
Run this in your project root:


chmod +x ./path/to/your/bin-file.js

(Replace ./path/to/your/bin-file.js with whatever file your package.json points to in the bin section.)


2. The Link got "Dirty"
Sometimes when you modify the bin structure or the package.json while a link is active, the symlink in your global node_modules becomes a "dead" link or points to a folder instead of the file.


The Fix:
Force a refresh of the link:


npm unlink -g proxima
npm link

3. The "Node" Shebang Issue
If you edited the entry point file, check the very first line. It must have the shebang, or zsh will try to run it as a shell script instead of a Node.js script, which often results in a permission error.


The Fix:
Ensure line 1 of your bin file is exactly:


#!/usr/bin/env node

4. Ghost Processes
If you are on macOS/Linux and a previous instance of the CLI is hung or "stuck" in a weird state (especially if you're using Electron or a persistent engine), the OS might lock the file.


The Quick Check:
Try running the file directly bypassing the link:


node ./path/to/bin-file.js


* If this works: The issue is definitely the npm link symlink.

* If this fails: You likely have a syntax error or a file-system lock on the project folder itself.


Which change did you just implement? If you changed the package.json "bin" name or moved the entry file to a different folder, npm link won't auto-update; you have to run npm link again to re-map the symlink.
