## Gemini

Microsoft's AutoUpdate (MAU) is notoriously persistent on macOS because it runs as a background daemon that restarts itself even if you force-quit the application. Since you prefer command-line control and deep automation, you can disable it by revoking its permissions or removing the launch agents entirely.


Here are the most effective ways to stop the cycle:


Method 1: The "Permissions Lockdown" (Recommended)
This is the cleanest method because it leaves the files in place (so apps don't break) but prevents the updater from actually executing.


Open Terminal and run:


sudo chmod 000 /Library/Application\ Support/Microsoft/MAU2.0/Microsoft\ AutoUpdate.app

This sets the permissions to 000, meaning no user or system process has the right to open or execute the updater. If you ever want to update manually, change 000 back to 775.



Method 2: Delete the Launch Daemons
MAU stays alive because of plist files in your Library folders that tell macOS to keep the process running. You can delete these to stop the "zombie" behavior.



* Unload and remove the services:
sudo launchctl unload -w /Library/LaunchDaemons/com.microsoft.autoupdate.helper.plist
launchctl unload -w ~/Library/LaunchAgents/com.microsoft.update.agent.plist



* Delete the files:


    sudo rm /Library/LaunchDaemons/com.microsoft.autoupdate.helper.plist
    sudo rm /Library/LaunchAgents/com.microsoft.update.agent.plist
    sudo rm /Library/PrivilegedHelperTools/com.microsoft.autoupdate.helper
    ```

## Gemini

Determining if you have perfect pitch (absolute pitch) involves assessing your ability to identify or produce a specific musical note without any reference tone.


How to Identify Perfect Pitch
Most people with perfect pitch discover it early in childhood, often when they begin musical training. You likely have it if you can:



* Identify individual notes played on an instrument or found in environmental sounds (like a car horn or a doorbell) by name ($C$, $F\sharp$, etc.).

* Sing a specific note on command without hearing a starting pitch.

* Identify the key of a musical composition immediately upon hearing it.

* Name multiple notes played simultaneously in a complex chord.


It is distinct from relative pitch, which is the ability to identify notes by their distance from a known starting point. Most professional musicians rely on highly developed relative pitch rather than absolute pitch.



Is It Learnable?
The consensus in cognitive psychology and neuroscience is that perfect pitch is generally not learnable in adulthood.



* The Critical Period: Research suggests there is a "critical period" for acquiring perfect pitch, usually ending around age six or seven. This is similar to how children acquire native fluency in a language; the brain is uniquely plastic and able to form permanent associations between sound frequencies and linguistic labels (note names).

* Genetics and Environment: It appears to be a combination of a genetic predisposition and early musical exposure. For example, speakers of tonal languages (like Mandarin or Vietnamese), where pitch changes the meaning of words, show significantly higher rates of perfect pitch.

* Adult Limitations: While adults can significantly improve their pitch recognition through "pitch memory" or "chroma" training, they rarely achieve the effortless, instantaneous recognition characteristic of true absolute pitch.


Relative Pitch: The Practical Alternative
While you likely cannot "learn" perfect pitch as an adult, you can master relative pitch. This involves training your ear to recognize intervals (the distance between two notes).


By learning that the jump from $C$ to $G$ is a "Perfect Fifth," you can navigate any piece of music as long as you have one reference note. In many professional settings, relative pitch is considered more useful because it allows a musician to understand the structural relationship between notes, rather than just identifying them in isolation.

## Gemini

It is exceptionally rare. In Western populations, the estimated prevalence of perfect pitch is roughly 1 in 10,000 people.


Several factors contribute to why those numbers are so low and why they fluctuate across different groups:



* Musical Training Timing: Even if someone has the genetic predisposition, they usually need to begin formal musical training before age 6 to "lock in" the ability. If that window closes, the talent often remains dormant or never develops.

* Linguistic Influence: The rarity changes based on native language. In cultures that speak tonal languages (where the pitch of a word changes its meaning), the rate of perfect pitch is significantly higher. Some studies of music conservatory students in East Asia have shown prevalence rates closer to 40% or 50%, as the brain is already primed to treat pitch as a primary carrier of information.

* The "Curse" of Rarity: Because it is so uncommon, it’s often mythologized as a sign of genius. However, many legendary musicians (including Wagner and Stravinsky) did not have it. In some cases, it can even be a hindrance—musicians with perfect pitch often struggle when a piano is slightly out of tune or when they have to transpose music to a different key, as their brain "screams" that the notes they are hearing don't match the notes they are playing.


For the vast majority of the world, the "superpower" isn't identifying a $B\flat$ in a vacuum, but rather understanding how that $B\flat$ feels in relation to the notes around it.

Go with Traditional. It forces the restaurant to rely on the quality of the chicken itself rather than the seasoning of a flour coating. If you want the best of both worlds, ask if they "hand-dust" their wings—that usually signals a kitchen that cares about the process.
