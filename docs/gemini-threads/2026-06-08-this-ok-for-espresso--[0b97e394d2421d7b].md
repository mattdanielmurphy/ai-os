This is actually a transformer question, not a motor question, so there is no "back EMF" subtracting from a supply voltage here.


Why $34.3\text{ V ac}$ is Correct
Transformers work on the principle of mutual induction between two separate coils (the primary and the secondary). The relationship between the voltages and the number of turns in the coils is a direct ratio:


$$\frac{V_s}{V_p} = \frac{N_s}{N_p}$$


Where:



* $V_p = 124\text{ V}$ (Primary voltage)

* $N_p = 524$ (Primary turns)

* $N_s = 145$ (Secondary turns)

* $V_s = \text{emf induced in the secondary}$


The Calculation
Rearranging the formula to solve for the secondary voltage ($V_s$):


$$V_s = V_p \times \left(\frac{N_s}{N_p}\right)$$


$$V_s = 124\text{ V} \times \left(\frac{145}{524}\right)$$


$$V_s \approx 124\text{ V} \times 0.2767 \approx 34.3\text{ V}$$


Because the secondary coil has fewer turns than the primary coil, this is a step-down transformer, meaning the induced voltage on the output side is simply a smaller fraction of the input voltage. No subtraction required—you nailed the math!
