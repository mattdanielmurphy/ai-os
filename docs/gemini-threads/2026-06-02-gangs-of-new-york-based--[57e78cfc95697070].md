# Gangs of New York based on a true story?

> [!NOTE]

## User

Gangs of New York based on a true story?

---

## Gemini

Yes and no. Martin Scorsese’s Gangs of New York (2002) is based on a 1927 non-fiction book by Herbert Asbury, but the film treats history more like a canvas than a strict script.


The backdrop, the environment, and the overarching political tensions are highly accurate. However, the central characters and the specific plot are heavily fictionalized.



What the Movie Got Right
1. The Setting: Five Points
The movie accurately portrays Five Points in Lower Manhattan as one of the most dangerous, impoverished, and densely populated slums in the world during the 19th century.



2. The Real Gangs
The names of the gangs featured in the film—like the Bowery Boys, the Dead Rabbits, the Plug Uglies, and the Shirt Tails—were completely real. They frequently clashed over territory, ethnicity, and political influence.


3. The New York Draft Riots of 1863
The climax of the film accurately captures the horror of the Civil War Draft Riots. In July 1863, working-class New Yorkers (mostly Irish immigrants) rioted over a new conscription law that allowed wealthy citizens to pay $300 to avoid the draft. The riot quickly turned into a horrific racial massacre, targeting Black citizens, and federal troops really were called in straight from the Battle of Gettysburg to shell parts of the city and restore order.



What the Movie Fictionalized
1. William "Bill the Butcher" Cutting
Daniel Day-Lewis’s iconic character is based on a real person named William Poole.

2. Amsterdam Vallon and the Plot
Leonardo DiCaprio’s character, Amsterdam Vallon, is entirely fictional, as is his father, Priest Vallon (Liam Neeson). The central revenge plot—and the massive, organized pitched battle in Paradise Square that opens the film—were created for Hollywood. While the real gangs had plenty of brutal street fights and riots, they rarely fought in massive, formal gladiatorial battles in the middle of the square.


3. Timeline Compression
The movie blends events from different eras. The real gang wars peaked in the 1840s and 1850s (such as the Dead Rabbits Riot of 1857). By compressing these events into the 1863 Draft Riots, the film makes it look like the old gang rivalries were settled by the Union Army's artillery, whereas in reality, those specific gang dynamics had already shifted by the time of the riots.



The Verdict: If you look at it as a historical document of the vibe, politics, and atmosphere of 1860s New York, it is remarkably well-researched. If you look at individual characters and the specific narrative arc, it's historical fiction.
