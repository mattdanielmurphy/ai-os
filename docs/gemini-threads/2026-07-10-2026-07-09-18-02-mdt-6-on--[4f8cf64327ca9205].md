## Gemini

That "hungry but completely turned off by the idea of food" paradox is a classic GLP-1 side effect. When Mounjaro delays gastric emptying and alters central appetite signals, the normal transition from full to empty gets muted. By the time your body finally overrides the medication to scream "we need fuel," your blood sugar has often dipped, and an empty stomach under those conditions frequently triggers nausea instead of a healthy appetite.


When you hit that wall where you know you need to eat but the thought of a full meal makes you sick, forcing down a heavy plate usually backfires. Shifting the strategy often helps get past that specific block:



* Liquid or semi-liquid first: A small protein shake, a cup of bone broth, or even a bit of yogurt is usually much easier to tolerate than solid food when you feel nauseous. It coats the stomach and gets some quick nutrients in to stabilize your blood sugar.

* Bland, calorie-dense bites: A few crackers, a spoonful of peanut butter, or a piece of toast can break the fasting cycle without triggering a gag reflex. Once your blood sugar starts to stabilize, the nausea often subsides enough for a real meal.

* Mechanical eating: Because the natural "bues" are gone, relying on a loose schedule or a simple reminder to eat small, regular snacks before the intense hunger hits can prevent getting stuck in that nausea loop entirely.

It keeps your energy stable and ensures you’re getting adequate nutrition without having to force large meals.


Setting up low-friction, predictable routines works incredibly well for this. A few strategies can help make those "going through the motions" meals stick:

* Prioritize protein: Since total intake drops significantly on Mounjaro, making sure those scheduled mini-meals have a solid protein source helps preserve muscle mass and keeps your energy from cratering.

## Gemini

Yes, it is massive. At 270 lbs, dropping your calories down to that 1500–2000 range means your body is in a significant deficit. If it doesn't get enough dietary protein to sustain itself, it will start breaking down muscle tissue alongside fat for energy.


When you lose a lot of weight quickly on a GLP-1 medication, preserving that lean muscle mass is vital for keeping your metabolic rate healthy and maintaining physical strength.


Since your appetite is low, hitting a high protein target can feel like a chore, but you can maximize your intake without feeling overly full:



* Aim for a baseline: A good starting target is often around 100 to 120 grams of protein a day, though a doctor or dietitian can tailor that exactly to your needs.

* Upgrade your scheduled snacks: Instead of just regular yogurt and Cheerios, swapping to plain Greek yogurt or Icelandic skyr instantly gives you 15–20g of protein per cup. You can throw a handful of high-protein granola or pumpkin seeds on top.

* Liquid leverage: When solid food feels like too much, high-quality protein shakes, clear whey isolates, or bone broth can easily add 25–30g of protein to your day without filling your stomach with heavy bulk.

* Front-load your meals: When you do sit down for a small meal, always eat the protein source (chicken, beef, fish, tofu, eggs) first. That way, if you get full halfway through, you've already checked the most important box.

Here is exactly why keeping that muscle matters, even if you never intend to lift a single weight:

That makes it incredibly easy to hit a brutal weight-loss plateau, and it makes keeping the weight off long-term much harder.

* It dictates how you look at the end: Losing pure fat leaves you looking lean, firm, and structurally solid. Losing a mix of fat and muscle often leads to looking "soft" or deflated even at a much lower weight, because the underlying structural framework is gone.

If you preserve that muscle while the fat drops away, your power-to-weight ratio skyrockets. Everyday movement, stairs, and just walking around start to feel incredibly light and effortless.


Think of muscle as your metabolic insurance policy. Protecting it with that protein habit ensures that the weight you are losing is the weight you actually want gone.

## Gemini

The issue here stems from how Apple Mail (which looks like the client in your screenshot) automatically configures the advanced connection parameters when "Automatically manage connection settings" is checked. It frequently defaults to settings that Zoho's SMTP servers reject.


The root cause usually maps to one of two quick fixes:


1. Disable Automatic Settings & Verify the Port
Because Zoho requires strict SSL/TLS parameters, letting the app "automatically manage" the connection can misconfigure the port or encryption type.



* Uncheck Automatically manage connection settings under the Outgoing Mail Server (SMTP) section.

* In the options that appear (or under Advanced IMAP/SMTP Settings), ensure the following are explicitly set for outgoing:

* Port: 465 (with SSL/TLS checked) OR Port: 587 (with STARTTLS/TLS checked).

* Authentication: Set this explicitly to Password (do not leave it on MD5 or external challenge tokens).






2. Check Your Specific Zoho Outgoing Host Name
Since you are using a custom domain (matt@mattmurphy.ca), Zoho's server architecture depends on whether you are on a legacy free tier or a paid organizational plan:



* Standard Host: smtp.zoho.com (as you currently have it)

* Paid/Workspace Host: If the standard host times out or throws an authentication error despite using a valid app password, try changing the Host Name to:

smtppro.zoho.com







A Quick Diagnostic Check
If you update the port and the host name but still get kicked back, double-check that you aren't trying to reuse the exact same app password string for both incoming and outgoing concurrently if the client handles them as completely separate daemons. Generating a dedicated app password labeled specifically for "Mac Mail Outgoing" can sometimes clear a stuck authentication state on Zoho's side.
