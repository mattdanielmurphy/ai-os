Domestic flight at 6:50. Boarding at 6:10? When does boarding end probably?

---

## Gemini

For a domestic flight departing at 6:50 AM, the boarding gate will almost certainly close 15 minutes before departure, which would be 6:35 AM.


While individual airline policies can vary slightly (some budget carriers in the US or Canada may push this to 20 minutes), the standard "hard" cutoff for domestic travel is usually 15 minutes.


Why the 6:35 AM cutoff matters:

* Gate Deadlines: Most airlines (like Air Canada, WestJet, or United) explicitly state that passengers must be at the gate and ready to board at least 15 minutes prior to takeoff. After this point, they may release your seat to standby passengers or offload your bags.

* The "Last Call": If boarding begins at 6:10 AM, the "final call" usually happens around 6:30 AM.

* Security Buffer: Early morning (6:00 AM – 8:00 AM) is a peak travel window. If you aren't already at the airport, keep in mind that security lines can be surprisingly long during this "business traveler" rush.


Quick Tip: If you have a checked bag, remember that the cutoff to drop it off is typically 45 to 60 minutes before departure (so between 5:50 AM and 6:05 AM). If you miss that window, they won't let you check the bag even if the plane hasn't boarded yet.

## Gemini

"Duty-free" shopping essentially allows you to buy goods without paying the local government's import duties or sales taxes (like GST/HST).


However, since you’re on a domestic flight, there is a major catch:


1. It’s strictly for International Travel
You can only purchase duty-free items if you are leaving the country. In most Canadian airports, the duty-free shops are physically located in the international or transborder (USA) departures areas. If you are flying between Canadian cities (e.g., Edmonton to Vancouver), you won't even have access to these stores.


2. How it works (when you do fly international)

* The "Duty": Normally, when a store imports high-end scotch or French perfume, they pay a "duty" (tax) to the government, which is passed on to you. Duty-free shops are treated as being "outside" the country's tax jurisdiction.

* The Savings: You generally save anywhere from 10% to 30% depending on the item. It’s most worth it for alcohol and tobacco because those have the highest government taxes.

* The Limits: You can’t just buy a truckload. Every country has "allowances." For example, when returning to Canada after 48 hours, you can bring back roughly $800 worth of goods, including 1.14 liters of spirits or 1.5 liters of wine, without paying tax.


3. Can you buy anything at the airport then?
Yes, you can still shop at the regular "retail" stores in the domestic terminal (like Hudson News, tech shops, or clothing stores). You will just pay the standard Alberta 5% GST on those items.


Summary for your 6:50 flight: Since you're flying domestic, duty-free isn't an option this morning. If you need a snack or a last-minute gift, stick to the standard shops in your terminal!
