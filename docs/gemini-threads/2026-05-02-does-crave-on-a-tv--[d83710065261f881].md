Does crave on a tv do 4k hbo?

## Gemini

Yes, but there is a major catch: you have to pay for their top-tier plan.


Crave treats 4K the same way Max does—as a premium upsell rather than a standard feature. Here is how the logic breaks down:


1. The Plan Requirement
You specifically need the Crave Premium plan (usually around $22/month).



* Crave Standard with Ads is capped at 1080p.

* Even if you are watching a massive HBO release like House of the Dragon, it will downscale to HD unless you're on that top tier.


2. The Device Requirement
Even with the right plan, 4K only works on dedicated TV apps. Most major platforms support it now:



* Apple TV 4K and Roku (4K models).

* Amazon Fire TV (4K Stick/Cube).

* Android TV / Google TV (Shield, Chromecast with Google TV, etc.).

* Smart TV Apps: Samsung (2018+), LG (2018+), and Sony/TCL/Hisense models running Google TV.

* Consoles: PS5 and Xbox Series X/S.


3. The "HBO" Quality Gap
Just because it's an HBO show doesn't mean it’s automatically 4K on Crave. While HBO produces almost all their new tentpole series in 4K/HDR, Crave's library of 4K content is notoriously smaller than the native Max library in the US.


To check if a specific show is actually going to hit 4K:



* Look for the "4K" or "UHD" badge on the show's landing page in the app.

* Tip: You can literally type "4K" into the Crave search bar on your TV to see exactly what they are actually surfacing in that resolution.


Short version: If you have the $22 Premium plan and a 4K streaming box/Smart TV, yes. If you’re using the cheaper plan or a browser, you're stuck in 1080p-land.

## Gemini

It depends on your specific Optik TV package, but for most people, it's either included as a "Premium" choice or it’s a paid add-on.


Here is how Telus handles it:


1. The "Premium" Slot (Most Common)
Most modern Optik TV bundles (like the "4 Theme Packs + 1 Premium" or "Core + 1 Premium") give you a "Premium" credit. You can use that credit to pick one of the following:



* Crave (which includes HBO and Max Originals)

* Netflix

* Disney+

* Sportsnet/TSN


If you chose Netflix or Disney+ as your premium, then yes, Crave would be extra (usually around $20–$22/month). If you haven't picked a premium yet, you can swap into Crave at no additional cost via your My TELUS account.


2. Stream+ (The New Bundle)
If you have Stream+ (the bundle Telus pushes that includes Netflix, Disney+, and Amazon Prime), Crave is not usually part of that base price. You have to add it on top for about $19.80/month (there's often a small discount for bundling).


3. Legacy Packages
If you're on a much older legacy plan, Crave might be a standalone add-on. If you see channels 400–401 (HBO) or 406–409 (Crave) in your guide and they aren't locked, you already have it.



How to check if you have it (and get it in 4K):

* Check your guide: Go to channel 400. If it works, you have it.

* The 4K Catch: If you get Crave through Telus, you usually get the "Premium" version of Crave. This means you can watch in 4K, but you have to use the Crave app on a 4K device (like a 4K Apple TV, Chromecast, or your Smart TV's native app).

* Note: The actual Telus Optik box is often limited to 1080p for Crave content; the app is your best bet for the high-res HBO stuff.





Pro-tip: If you find you're paying for Crave separately, check your My TELUS "Manage TV" section. You might be able to swap a theme pack or premium you don't use to get it for "free."

## Gemini

Using Crave through Prime Video is actually the "power user" move in Canada, but it changes the rules for 4K.


The Good News: Better Quality
Amazon’s infrastructure is significantly better than Bell’s. When you watch Crave content inside the Prime Video app, you are usually getting a higher bitrate and a more stable stream than you would using the actual Crave app. Prime’s player is much better at handling high-bandwidth video without stuttering.


The Bad News: The 4K/HDR "Missing Link"
Even though Prime Video supports 4K, Crave’s "Channel" on Prime is notoriously stingy with it.



* The 1080p Cap: Most HBO content on the Prime-Crave channel is capped at 1080p SDR. Even if House of the Dragon is available in 4K HDR on the standalone Crave app, Amazon often only receives the 1080p assets from Bell for the Prime Channel.

* No Dolby Vision/Atmos: You will almost never get Dolby Vision or Atmos through the Prime Video version of Crave. It’s usually standard HDR (if you’re lucky) or just high-bitrate SDR.


Can you use the Crave App with your Prime login?
No. This is the biggest catch.



* If you subscribe through Telus or directly, you can log into the Crave app on your TV.

* If you subscribe via Prime Video Channels, your sub is "trapped" inside the Prime Video interface. You cannot use those credentials to log into the standalone Crave app to hunt for the 4K versions of shows.


Summary for your setup:

* On the Web: You are still going to be capped at 1080p (or 720p depending on the browser) because of the DRM issues I mentioned before.

* On your TV: Use the Prime Video app. You’ll get the most stable, highest-bitrate 1080p image possible, which often looks better than a "fake" 4K stream anyway.


If you're a real stickler for 4K/HDR for something like The Last of Us or Dune, you’d unfortunately have to cancel the Prime channel and subscribe directly through Crave (or Telus) to gain access to the actual Crave TV app’s 4K library.

Even if you pay for the top tier, the Prime Video interface often doesn't surface the 4K version of HBO shows. Amazon receives a specific feed from Bell, and historically, that feed has been 1080p SDR for the vast majority of the library.


3. The "Secret" Fix (Linking Your Account)
I have some better news than before: you actually can escape the Prime interface now. You can link your Prime subscription to a Bell Media account to use the actual Crave app, which is much more reliable for 4K.

* Go to the Crave.ca sign-in page.

* Select "Activate Subscription Through Provider."

* Choose Prime Video Channels from the list.

* Follow the prompts to link it to a Crave account.


Once you do this, stop using the Prime app for HBO stuff. Download the standalone Crave app on your Smart TV or Apple TV/Fire Stick. If you have the Premium tier, you’ll finally see the "4K" badges on shows like The Last of Us or Euphoria.



The Bottom Line

* Web Browser: Still 1080p (or 720p) because of the DRM "logic" we talked about. No way around this.

* Prime App on TV: Usually 1080p, even if you pay for the expensive plan.

It’s an exhausting amount of hoop-jumping just to get the resolution you’re actually paying for.

## Gemini

You’re right—it’s not under the "TV Provider" button. Because Amazon is technically a "3rd party partner" rather than a cable company, the login flow is tucked away in a different spot.


To get your Prime subscription working inside the actual Crave app (so you can get that 4K/HDR), you have to do the "Activate" flow instead of the "Sign In" flow.


The Fix

* Go to crave.ca/link on your phone or computer.

* Look for a button that says "Activate Subscription Through Provider" (it is usually separate from the standard "Sign In" button).

* Scroll down to the bottom of the list. You should see a section for "3rd Party Partners"—Amazon Prime Video should be listed there.

* Log in with your Amazon credentials.

* It will then ask you to create (or sign into) a Bell Media account. This "links" your Prime payment to a Crave email login.


Once Linked:

* On your TV: Open the Crave app, and now use the Standard Sign-In (email and password) that you just created.

* Resolution: If you are paying for the $22 Premium tier through Prime, the 4K content will now actually show up with the 4K/HDR badges in the app.


Why they make it this hard:
Bell (who owns Crave) wants your data directly. By making you "Activate through a provider" and create a Bell Media account, they get your email and usage habits, while Amazon handles the billing. It’s a classic corporate tug-of-war where the user ends up doing the manual labor.
