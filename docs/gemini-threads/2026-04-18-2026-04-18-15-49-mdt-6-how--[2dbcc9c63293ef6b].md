To embolden LaTeX within a Markdown string, you need to use LaTeX-specific bolding commands inside the $ delimiters, as standard Markdown ** formatting usually doesn't affect the math renderer.


For the most consistent results across symbols and fractions, \boldsymbol{...} is the most reliable choice.


Updated String
**it feels only about $\boldsymbol{59\%}$ ($\boldsymbol{\frac{0.16}{0.27}}$) of the "pull" that his feet feel**


Which command should you use?

* \mathbf{...}: Best for standard Latin letters and numbers (e.g., $\mathbf{59}$). However, it often fails to bold symbols like % or mathematical operators.

* \boldsymbol{...}: Designed to bold the entire expression, including symbols, Greek letters, and complex structures like fractions.


If you only wanted to bold the numbers and leave the symbols at normal weight, you could use:
$\mathbf{59}\%$ and $(\frac{\mathbf{0.16}}{\mathbf{0.27}})$


But for a uniform "bold" look that matches the surrounding Markdown text, \boldsymbol is the way to go.

However, there are two small caveats to keep in mind if you use it for everything:



* Roman/Upright Text: If you want your bold text to be upright (non-italicized) like standard text, \boldsymbol won't do that—it will just give you bold italics. In those cases, you’d want \mathbf{...}.

* Performance/Readability: In very long, complex equations, \boldsymbol can occasionally make symbols look "muddy" or crowded because it thickens every line of the character.

The issue is likely that your terminal is interpreting Ctrl+z as a SIGSTOP signal (which suspends the process and sends it to the background) rather than a text-undo command.


Here are the most likely causes and how to fix it:

In most Unix-like environments (Linux/macOS), Ctrl+z is the default "suspend" shortcut. If the CLI hasn't successfully intercepted that keypress, the terminal takes over and "kills" the active session.



* The Fix: You can often resume the "killed" session by typing fg (foreground) in your terminal and hitting Enter.

* Prevention: Check if you have Vim Mode enabled. According to the CLI Commands documentation, you can toggle this by typing /vim. If Vim mode is on, shortcuts change their behavior significantly.
