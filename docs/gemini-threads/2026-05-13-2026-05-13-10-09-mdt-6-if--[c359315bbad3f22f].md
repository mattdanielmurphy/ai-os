## Gemini

Whether you should discard the exposed part of a split banana depends mostly on the environment it was in and how long it’s been open.


The Safety Check
If the peel split while the banana was sitting on your counter, the fruit is likely safe to eat, provided you follow these guidelines:



* Oxidation: The exposed area will turn brown or black quickly due to enzymatic browning (the same process as an apple slice turning brown). This isn't inherently dangerous, but the texture will be mushy and the flavor may be slightly "off" or fermented.

* Contamination: If the banana was in a backpack, a grocery bag, or on a floor when it split, you should cut away the exposed portion. Dirt, lint, or bacteria from those surfaces can easily migrate into the soft fruit.

* Fruit Flies: In warmer weather, even a small split can attract fruit flies almost immediately. If you see any activity around the fruit, it’s best to trim that section off.


When to Discard
You should definitely toss the exposed part (or the whole banana) if you notice:



* Mold: Any fuzzy white, green, or grey growth.

* Ooze: If the split area is leaking liquid or feels slimy rather than just soft.

* Smell: A sharp, vinegar-like, or "boozy" scent indicates it has begun to ferment.


The Middle Ground
If the split looks clean and happened recently, you can simply trim off about a half-inch of the fruit behind the split. The rest of the banana inside the intact peel will be perfectly fine.


If the texture of the exposed part is unappealing but it isn't "spoiled," it's a great candidate for a smoothie or banana bread where the aesthetics don't matter.
