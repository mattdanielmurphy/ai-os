## Gemini

The red solution takes the long way around because it is deriving the formula from scratch using fundamental electrostatic and magnetic forces, rather than just pulling the shortcut formula from a formula sheet.


Both methods are entirely valid, but they represent two different levels of understanding physics. Here is why the author of the key likely wrote it out that way:


1. Conceptual Demonstration (The "Why" vs. the "What")
The lengthy method explains why an EMF is generated in a moving wire in the first place.



* When a conductor moves through a magnetic field, the free electrons inside it are forced to move along with the wire.

* Moving charges in a magnetic field experience a magnetic force ($F_B = qvB$).

* According to the right-hand rule, this force pushes the electrons to one end of the wire, creating a separation of charge (a positive end and a negative end).

* This charge separation creates an internal electric field ($E$) which exerts an opposing electric force ($F_E = qE$) on the electrons.

* Eventually, the charges stop moving when the electric force perfectly balances the magnetic force ($F_E = F_B$).


By setting those two forces equal to each other, the red solution shows how the voltage (EMF) stabilizes:


$$qE = qvB$$
$$E = vB$$


Since $E = \frac{V}{d}$ (where $d$ is the length of the wire $l$), substituting that in gives:


$$\frac{V}{l} = vB \implies V = Blv$$


2. Academic Pedagogy
Often, physics keys are written this way to show students how two entirely different chapters of their textbook—Electric Forces/Fields ($F_E = qE$) and Magnetic Forces ($F_B = qvB$)—link together to create a brand new phenomenon (Motional EMF).


Your version is the practical, efficient way to solve the problem on an exam. The red version is the textbook's way of proving the physics actually works.
