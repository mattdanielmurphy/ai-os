## Gemini

John Siracusa (likely on Accidental Tech Podcast) tends to be allergic to "security theater" and marketing-led hype cycles, so his skepticism of Anthropic’s Claude Mythos rollout fits his brand perfectly.


The argument that Mythos is "marketing BS" usually centers on a few points where he’s likely onto something:


The Case for Marketing Hype

* The "Safety" Moat: By framing Mythos as "too dangerous to release," Anthropic creates an aura of god-like power without having to prove it in the wild. It’s a classic move—nothing makes a product look more advanced than saying it’s a controlled substance.

* Project Glasswing: Skeptics argue that forming a defensive alliance before a release is less about altruism and more about establishing Anthropic as the "responsible" alternative to OpenAI, especially as they court enterprise and government contracts.

* Incrementalism: As seen on forums like Hacker News, many developers suspect Mythos is just an incrementally better model (perhaps an OOM increase in compute) that has been pointed at high-value bug hunting, rather than a fundamental "step change" in intelligence.


Where He Might Be Wrong
If Mythos truly is a step-change in autonomous agentic behavior, the skepticism might be misplaced. Finding zero-days is one thing; being able to autonomously chain exploits across complex, multi-layered systems (as Anthropic claims) would genuinely shift the cybersecurity landscape from "human-led" to "machine-speed."



Is he right?
Probably 80% yes. The "dangerous release" narrative is definitely a potent marketing tool used to drive valuation and prestige. However, the remaining 20%—the actual utility in identifying vulnerabilities in critical infrastructure—is likely real, even if it's not the "digital apocalypse" the PR department is hinting at.


It feels like a repeat of the GPT-2 "too dangerous to release" cycle from 2019, which looks quaint in hindsight.


Do you think Anthropic is actually worried about the model, or are they just trying to win the "most responsible AI company" PR war?
