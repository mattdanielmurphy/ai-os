## Gemini

Yes, if you push the volume. You risk blowing the speaker.


While the electronics (the amplifier section itself) will handle the signal perfectly fine without bursting into flames, the speaker driver is the weak link.


Acoustic amplifiers are designed for full-range, high-fidelity sound, but their speaker cones are relatively thin and have limited excursion (how far the cone can move forward and backward). The massive low-frequency waves from an electric bass force a speaker cone to move much farther than a standard acoustic guitar or vocal mic ever would.


If you do it, keep these rules in mind:



* Low volume only: If you're just practicing quietly in a bedroom at low volumes, it will survive.

* Watch for farting out: If the bass notes start to sound distorted, muddy, or like the speaker is physically choking (often called "farting out"), you are pushing the mechanical limits of the cone. Back off immediately.

* The Tone: It won't sound great. Acoustic amps are clean and flat, meaning you'll get a very clinical, sterile bass tone without the low-end punch or warmth a dedicated bass cabinet provides.


Bottom line: For quiet, low-volume practice in a pinch? You'll get away with it. For jamming with anyone else or turning it up past bedroom levels? Don't risk it.
