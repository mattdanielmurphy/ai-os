To get this exact behavior in VCV Rack—where a pulse triggers a drop and immediate rise back to a held maximum—you need to invert how you approach the envelope. Instead of generating a standard upward spike from zero, you want to modulate a constant high voltage downward.


Here are the two best ways to set this up using standard or stock modules:


Method 1: The Inversion Technique (Simplest)
Instead of forcing the envelope generator into a non-standard latch mode, you can feed a constant voltage into an attenuverter, and use an inverted envelope to duck the signal.



* Generate Constant Voltage: Take a Befaco BCE or VCV Fundamental CV-1 module and set a DC output to maximum ($10\text{V}$). Route this to your target parameter (like VCA CV or Filter Cutoff).

* Set Up the Envelope: Patch your pulse trigger into a standard ADSR or AD envelope (like VCV ADSR). Set Attack to your desired rise time, Decay/Sustain to zero, and Release to zero.

* Invert and Attenuate: Send the envelope output into an attenuverter (like VCV ATTN or MINDMELD Shape). Invert the signal completely (turn the knob to the negative side).

* Mix Them: Send both the constant $10\text{V}$ and the negative envelope into a basic mixer (VCV Mixer), then send that combined output to your destination.


When a pulse hits, the negative envelope will instantly subtract voltage from the constant high level, dipping it down, and the Attack stage will dictate how fast it rises back up to the ceiling.



Method 2: Using a Function Generator (Befaco Rampage)
If you want a single module to handle this natively without external mixers, Befaco Rampage (free in the VCV library) is perfect for this.



* Patch your pulse into the Trigger input of Channel A.

* Look at the Inverted Output ($\text{Min}$) of Channel A.

* By default, this output sits at $10\text{V}$. When a trigger hits, it will immediately drop to $0\text{V}$ and then rise back up to $10\text{V}$ based on the Rise slider setting.

* Set the Fall slider to zero (fully down) so the dip is instantaneous.


This gives you a dedicated "duck and rise" CV source from a single trigger input, perfectly mimicking a latched, inverted gate cycle.

Why it "doesn't really matter"
Dentists screen for everything from oral cancer to severe infections. When they see a white line or mild roughness caused by cheek biting, they recognize it as a completely benign (harmless) tissue change.


Because it is just a superficial thickening of the tissue rather than an open, infected ulcer or a suspicious lesion, it does not pose a threat to your oral health.
