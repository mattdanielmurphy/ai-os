While a full Memory Palace involves walking through a house, "Slotting" is a streamlined version used for rapid data.

## Gemini

It’s completely normal to feel "lost" during that segment; most people’s brains are wired to process information sequentially, while Victoria was doing it associatively.


Improving working memory is less about "expanding the hard drive" and more about improving the compression software you use to store data. Here are a few ways to train your brain to handle information the way a poker pro or a memory athlete does:



1. Master "Chunking"
Working memory can generally hold about 4–7 pieces of information. If you try to remember "Alan 4, Desiree 2, Guz 5, Morgana 1, Victoria 3," you’ve already filled your capacity.



* The Technique: Instead of seeing 15 separate data points, group them. In Taskmaster, Victoria wasn't remembering the individual scores for each round; she was likely updating a single running total for each person.

* The Practice: When you see a phone number like 5550198, don't remember seven digits. Remember 555, 01, and 98. You’ve turned seven items into three.


2. The "N-Back" Exercise
This is one of the few scientifically backed methods for actually increasing working memory fluid intelligence.



* How it works: You are shown a sequence (like letters or positions on a grid) and have to identify when the current item matches the one from n steps ago.

* Why it helps: It forces your brain to constantly discard old information and "buffer" new information in real-time, which is exactly what Victoria was doing as Alex read out the ranks.


3. Dual Coding (Visual + Verbal)
Victoria likely wasn't just hearing the numbers; she was "placing" them.



* The Technique: Assign each contestant a "spot" in your field of vision (left to right). As the scores come in, imagine the number appearing over their head.

* Why it works: By using both your phonological loop (hearing the words) and your visuospatial sketchpad (seeing the location), you double the "bandwidth" your brain uses to store the data.


4. Practice "Mental Updating"
You can practice this during daily life to build that "poker muscle":



* The Grocery List Game: As you put items in your cart, keep a running total of the cost in your head. Every time you add a $4.99 item, do the math immediately.

* The License Plate Game: Try to add the digits of the license plate in front of you, then hold that total while you find the next car and add its digits to your running sum.



Why Victoria had the edge
In the Full Episode, Victoria mentions her background multiple times. Years of high-stakes poker act as "resistance training" for the brain. In poker, if you lose track of the "count," you lose money. That high-pressure environment turns "active" calculation into a "passive" background process.
