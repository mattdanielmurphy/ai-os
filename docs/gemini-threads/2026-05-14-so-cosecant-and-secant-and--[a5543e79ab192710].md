## Gemini

Actually, no—and this is a very common point of confusion. Cosecant, secant, and cotangent are reciprocals, while asin, acos, and atan are inverses. They perform completely different mathematical operations.


1. Reciprocal Functions (Multiplicative Inverse)
The functions you are looking at on your screen—$\csc\theta$, $\sec\theta$, and $\cot\theta$—are defined by flipping the fraction of the primary ratios. You are essentially dividing 1 by the original value.



* Cosecant: $\csc\theta = \frac{1}{\sin\theta}$

* Secant: $\sec\theta = \frac{1}{\cos\theta}$

* Cotangent: $\cot\theta = \frac{1}{\tan\theta}$


The Goal: You have the angle ($\theta$), and you want to find a ratio.



2. Inverse Functions (Arc-functions)
Functions like $\arcsin$ (or $\sin^{-1}$), $\arccos$, and $\arctan$ are used to "undo" the trig function to find the angle itself.



* If $\sin(30^\circ) = 0.5$, then $\arcsin(0.5) = 30^\circ$.


The Goal: You have the ratio, and you want to find the angle ($\theta$).
