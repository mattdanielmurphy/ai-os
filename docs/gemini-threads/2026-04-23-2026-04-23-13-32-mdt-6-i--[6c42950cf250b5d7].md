## Gemini

It is highly likely that your email was harvested via WHOIS data or a site-specific scrape, rather than just a general database leak.


Since these emails specifically mention your "page" or "website," the spammers are likely using a targeted list of domain owners. Here is the most probable breakdown of how they got your address and why it feels like a "new" wave:


1. The WHOIS Data Harvest
When you register a domain, your contact information (name, email, phone) is technically required to be part of the public WHOIS database.



* The "Leak": Spammers use automated scripts to "scrape" these records for every new domain registration or update.

* The Redaction: Most modern registrars (like Namecheap, Cloudflare, or Google) now include "WHOIS Privacy" for free, which replaces your info with a placeholder. If you recently registered a new domain or moved a domain to a registrar that didn't automatically enable privacy, your personal email would have been exposed to these crawlers instantly.


2. "Website Type Business BS" (The New Wave)
The reason you're seeing a sudden influx of "collab" and "business inquiry" emails is likely due to AI-driven lead generation.



* Mass Personalization: Spammers now use LLMs to crawl websites, identify the owner’s name or niche, and draft "personalized" emails. They aren't just sending "Buy Vi@gra" anymore; they are sending "I saw your work on [Site Name] and think we should partner up."

* Fresh Batches: You're spot on about the "batches." Data brokers sell lists specifically categorized as "Active Website Owners." If your email just landed in one of these "Fresh April 2026" lists, you'll see a spike in activity as multiple spammers buy and run through that same list.


3. Public "Contact" Scrapes
If your personal email is anywhere on your site—even hidden in a "Contact Me" button or the metadata of a file—spiders will find it. Even if it’s not visible to a human, it’s visible to a bot.



How to Verify and Mitigate

* Check your WHOIS status: Use a site like whois.com to look up your own domain. If you see your personal email address in the "Registrant Email" field, you need to toggle WHOIS Privacy (or "ID Protect") on in your registrar's dashboard.

* Audit "Have I Been Pwned": Since you mentioned the "hundreds of sites" theory, check your email on Have I Been Pwned. This will tell you if your email was part of a specific corporate breach (like LinkedIn, Adobe, etc.) which often feeds into these spam lists.

* Use Aliases for your Site: For future projects or "Columnar," consider using a service like SimpleLogin or iCloud Hide My Email. You can create webmaster@yourdomain.com that forwards to your personal mail; if it starts getting spam, you just kill that specific alias without losing your primary email.


A quick tip: If these "collab" emails have a common phrase or are all coming to a specific address, you can set up a filter in your mail client to skip the inbox. Most of these "business bs" emails are just "fishing" to see if your address is active.
