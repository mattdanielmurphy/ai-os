## Gemini

While often used interchangeably in casual conversation, these terms have distinct nuances in legal and formal contexts.


Illegal
The term illegal refers to an act that is a direct violation of a specific law or statute created by a legislative body. It typically implies a criminal offense or a serious breach of regulations.



* Context: Criminal law, traffic violations, or regulatory bans.

* Example: Driving above the speed limit or theft. These are explicitly defined as "illegal" because there are written laws prohibiting them.


Unlawful
The term unlawful is broader. It refers to an act that is not authorized or justified by law, or one that runs contrary to the "spirit" of the law. While an unlawful act might not always land you in criminal court, it lacks legal standing or protection.



* Context: Civil law, contracts, or administrative procedures.

* Example: An "unlawful" termination of an employment contract. The firing might not be a crime (illegal), but it violates the legal principles governing contracts and is therefore not "lawful."

Summary Note: All illegal acts are unlawful, but not all unlawful acts are necessarily illegal (criminal). For instance, a clause in a contract might be "unlawful" and therefore unenforceable, even if writing that clause wasn't a crime.
