# Without any swirling, the grounds get stuck on the sides. I 

> [!NOTE]
> Archived Gemini Thread: [Without any swirling, the grounds get stuck on the sides. I ]() | Date: 2026-08-13

## User

Without any swirling, the grounds get stuck on the sides. I find a swirl after the second pour doesn’t result in the grounds getting stuck, but it helps to stop the grounds from sticking to the sides after the drawdown.

---

## Gemini

That makes perfect sense. What you are observing is a classic fluid dynamics issue called high-and-dry grounds.


When you complete that second pour, the water level is at its peak height in the V60 cone. As gravity draws the liquid down, the surface tension of the water naturally leaves particles clinging to the paper walls. If they stay up there, they are completely isolated from the water column, meaning they stop extracting early and you lose out on their contribution to the total strength of the cup.


Your adjustment—adding a gentle swirl right after that final pour—is the exact solution.


Here is what is happening mechanically when you introduce that movement:



* The Centrifugal Wash: Swirling creates a mild vortex that gently washes the water up the walls of the filter, breaking the surface tension holding those grounds to the paper and flushing them back down into the main slurry.

* The Flat Bed Advantage: Once the grounds are washed back into the pool, they settle uniformly as the water drains. This leaves you with a perfectly flat, even coffee bed at the end of the drawdown instead of a hollow "crater" shape. A flat bed ensures that the last 50g of water passes through an equal depth of coffee everywhere, keeping the extraction uniform.


The reason you don't see them get stuck after that swirl is because the water level doesn't rise again; it only recedes.


Since your main goal is data-driven consistency, keep that final swirl exactly as you did it—just keep it smooth and consistent so the vortex settles predictably every time. With the grind stepped down finer to fix the fast drawdown, that final swirl should help you hit a balanced extraction.
