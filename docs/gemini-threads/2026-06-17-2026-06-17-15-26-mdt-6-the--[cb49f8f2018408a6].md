## Gemini

The mean lifetime of a muon is 2.2 microseconds (or $2.2 \times 10^{-6}$ seconds).


If you are looking specifically for its half-life (the time it takes for half of a sample of muons to decay), it is slightly shorter due to the exponential decay constant.


The Math
The relationship between mean lifetime ($\tau$) and half-life ($t_{1/2}$) is defined by the formula:


$$t_{1/2} = \tau \ln(2)$$


Plugging in the muon's mean lifetime:


$$t_{1/2} \approx 2.197 \mu\text{s} \times 0.6931 \approx 1.52 \mu\text{s}$$


So, the half-life of a stationary muon is approximately 1.52 microseconds.



A Note on Relativity
This 1.52-microsecond half-life only applies to muons at rest relative to the observer. Because muons are often created by cosmic rays traveling at nearly the speed of light, time dilation kicks in. From our perspective on Earth, their high velocity stretches their half-life significantly, allowing them to reach the surface before decaying.

## Gemini

Ah, that makes total sense. Keyboard Maestro treats tokens differently depending on which specific action is executing them, and the search-and-replace action just treated %Keystroke% as literal text instead of processing it.


On top of that, your original attempt with \R hitting enter early means Keyboard Maestro's typing engine was processing a literal newline character before the regex could even swap it out—likely because of how the variable was being read or passed.


To fix both issues—getting it to physically press Shift-Return without typing out the raw token string—you need to use a For Each loop to process the text line by line.


Here is the exact macro structure you need:



The Clean Solution: Line-by-Line Execution
Instead of trying to find-and-replace the newlines globally, we will split the text by its lines, type each line individually, and then explicitly simulate the Shift-Return keystroke between them.



* 
Add a "For Each" Action



* Set it to loop through: The Lines in a Collection.

* Choose The Variable (or The Clipboard) containing your original error text.

* Set the "to variable" name to something temporary, like LocalLine.




* 
Inside the "For Each" loop, add two actions:



* Action A: Insert Text by Typing

* Set the text box to just contain the single line variable: %Variable%LocalLine%




* Action B: Type a Keystroke

* Click the box and physically press Shift-Return on your keyboard so it registers that exact combination.









The Visual Setup
Your macro steps inside Keyboard Maestro should look exactly like this:



For each item in The Lines in Collection %Variable%YourOriginalText%



* Assign to variable: LocalLine

* Execute the following actions:

* Insert Text by Typing: %Variable%LocalLine%

* Type a Keystroke: Shift-Return






Why this works perfectly
By looping through the collection as "Lines", Keyboard Maestro automatically strips the trailing newline off of LocalLine during each iteration. It types the raw text of the line safely, and then immediately fires a hardware-level Shift-Return command before grabbing the next line. No regex or fragile token parsing required.

## Gemini

To set this up, you just need to populate that empty For Each block. Here is exactly what to click and fill in to get it working:


1. Set the Collection Type

* Click the green + New Collection button.

* From the dropdown menu that appears, select The Lines in a Collection (it might be under a "Text" or "Strings" sub-menu depending on your KM version).


2. Configure the Fields
Once you add that collection type, your action block will expand. Fill it out like this:



* For each: Change VarName to something like LocalLine.

* The lines in: Choose The System Clipboard (or The Variable if you are storing the build errors in a specific variable).


3. Drop the Actions Inside
Drag your two actions into the dashed box that says "No Action":



* Insert Text by Typing: Set the text box to %Variable%LocalLine%.

* Type a Keystroke: Click the box and physically press Shift-Return on your keyboard.



One Quick Catch to Avoid
Because this loop fires a Shift-Return at the end of every iteration, it will naturally add an extra trailing Shift-Return after typing the very last line of your text block.


If that extra line break at the very end causes issues in your target app, you can easily prevent it by adding an If Then Else conditional wrapper inside the loop so it only presses Shift-Return if it hasn't reached the end yet, but test the basic loop first to see if the application even cares!
