## Gemini

It definitely is a process. Unlike a steak where you just hit it with heat, breaded strips involve a "station" setup that usually leaves your kitchen (and your hands) covered in flour and egg wash.


If you're doing it from scratch, you're looking at the Standard Breading Procedure:



* Prep: Slicing the chicken into even strips.

* Dredge: Coating in seasoned flour (this is where you’d use your MSG).

* Wash: Dipping in beaten egg (to make the crust stick).

* Final Coat: Panko or breadcrumbs.

* Frying: Managing oil temps and the inevitable cleanup of oil splatter.


The "Mid-Way" Shortcuts
If you want the low sodium of homemade without the 45-minute setup and "club hand" (when your fingers get coated in breading), there are two ways to cheat:



* The "Naked" Strip: Skip the breading entirely. Season the chicken with your MSG/spice blend and pan-sear or air-fry it. You get 100% of the control and 0% of the mess. Since you're dipping them in Habanero sauce anyway, the sauce provides the texture and moisture you'd usually get from the breading.

* The Cornstarch Toss: Instead of the full flour-egg-crumb routine, just toss the chicken pieces in a bowl with a bit of cornstarch, MSG, and spices, then air-fry. It creates a thin, crispy "shatter" crust (similar to some ginger beef or popcorn chicken) with way less effort.


The Verdict
Making a full batch of breaded strips is a "Sunday afternoon" project. It’s great for meal prepping a big container that you can freeze and toss in the air fryer later, but for a random Tuesday dinner? It’s a lot of dishes.
