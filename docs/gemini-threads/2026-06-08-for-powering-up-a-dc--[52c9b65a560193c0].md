# for powering up a DC fan (12V .18A) with just 9V, do I need 

> [!NOTE]

## User

for powering up a DC fan (12V .18A) with just 9V, do I need to use a resistor? My battery is 6 AA volts in series

---

## Gemini

No, you do not need a resistor. In fact, adding a resistor would likely prevent the fan from spinning at all. Here is exactly why, along with a quick correction regarding your battery setup.



1. The Battery Math (A Quick Correction)
You mentioned using 6 AA batteries in series.



* Standard AA batteries (alkaline) are $1.5\text{V}$ each.

* $6 \times 1.5\text{V} = \mathbf{9\text{V}}$.


So yes, your total output is $9\text{V}$. (If you are using rechargeable NiMH AA batteries, they are $1.2\text{V}$ each, giving you $7.2\text{V}$, which will still work but run slightly slower).



2. Why No Resistor is Needed

* Under-volting is safe for DC motors: Running a $12\text{V}$ motor at $9\text{V}$ is perfectly safe. The fan will simply spin at roughly $75%$ of its maximum speed and run very quietly.

* The fan only draws what it needs: The current rating on your fan ($0.18\text{A}$) is its maximum draw at $12\text{V}$. At $9\text{V}$, the fan's internal resistance remains the same, meaning it will naturally draw even less current than $0.18\text{A}$ (around $0.13\text{A}$ to $0.14\text{A}$).

* Resistors choke the voltage: If you add a resistor in series, it drops the voltage even further. Because PC fans need a minimum "starting voltage" (usually around $5\text{V}$ to $7\text{V}$) to overcome the initial friction of the blades, a resistor would drop the voltage below that threshold, and the fan wouldn't start.



How to Wire It
Connect the batteries directly to the fan's power wires:



* Positive ($+$): Connect the positive side of your battery pack to the Red wire of the fan.

* Negative ($-$): Connect the negative side of your battery pack to the Black wire of the fan.

* Other Wires: If your Fractal Design fan has a yellow, white, or blue wire, just leave them disconnected. Those are for speed sensing and PWM control, which you don't need for a simple demonstration.


Once connected, the fan will start spinning immediately, safely demonstrating the energy transformation for your assignment.

## Gemini

Yes, three-pin computer fans use a standardized wiring convention. Even without labels, you can easily identify the wires by their positions on the motherboard jack (connector).


Here is the standard layout for a 3-pin PC fan connector:


The Standard 3-Pin Pinout
Looking closely at the plastic connector, you will see two raised plastic ridges (guides) on one side. With those guides facing you, the wires are ordered from left to right:































PinStandard Wire ColorFunctionConnection for Your TestPin 1Black (or dark gray)Ground (Negative)Connect to Battery Negative ($-$)Pin 2Red (or yellow on older fans)+12V Power (Positive)Connect to Battery Positive ($+$)Pin 3Yellow (or white/green)Tachometer (Speed Signal)Leave disconnected

What if all the wires are solid black?
Some premium fans (including many Fractal Design models) use all-black ribbon cables for aesthetics. If your wires are all the same color, look at the plastic connector itself:



* Pin 1 (Ground): Located on the far edge, right next to the first plastic guide slot.

* Pin 2 (+12V): The middle pin.

* Pin 3 (Signal): Located on the opposite far edge.


Safe Testing Tip
To connect your battery pack without cutting the fan's connector, you can take two stiff solid-core wires (or even straight paperclips), insert them directly into the holes for Pin 1 and Pin 2 on the jack, and hook those extension wires to your 9V battery pack.


As long as the middle pin connects to your battery positive and the correct edge pin connects to your battery negative, the fan will spin up perfectly.

## User

can I reverse polarity safely? it's a fractal fan

---

## Gemini

No, you should not reverse the polarity on a Fractal Design PC fan.


While a simple brushed DC motor (like the one in a toy car) will safely spin backwards if you flip the wires, a computer fan is a brushless DC (BLDC) motor. It contains a small electronic circuit board (PCB) with transistors and a controller chip inside the hub to handle the switching of the electromagnets.


Here is what happens if you reverse the wires:



* The fan will not spin backwards: Because of the internal electronics, reversing the polarity will not make the blades change direction.

* You risk destroying the fan: Most modern PC fans have basic protection diodes, but they are not designed to handle sustained reverse voltage. Connecting positive to negative can instantly fry the tiny control circuit on the fan's PCB, rendering it permanently dead. This would prevent you from being able to reassemble it in working order for your assignment.


How to Tell Which Way the Air Blows
If you are worried about the polarity because you want to change the direction of the airflow, you don't need to change the wiring. Instead, physically flip the fan around:



* Front Side (Open blades): This side pulls air in.

* Back Side (Side with the plastic spokes and wires): This side pushes air out.


Always stick to connecting Battery Positive ($+$) to the middle pin (Pin 2) and Battery Negative ($-$) to the edge pin (Pin 1) to keep the electronics safe.
