## Gemini

In the context of the clinical trial you are viewing (NCT06545435), medications like Ozempic, Wegovy, or Zepbound (GLP-1 receptor agonists) generally do count as being part of a "weight-reducing program."


Here is how those specific criteria usually apply to this study:


1. The "Weight-Reducing Program" Exclusion
The study explicitly excludes anyone who has participated in a "weight-reducing program" in the last 3 months. In clinical research:



* Medical Weight Management: Prescription medications specifically for weight loss are almost always categorized as a weight-reducing intervention.

* Data Integrity: Since the study is trying to create accurate equations to measure body composition (fat vs. muscle) in people with obesity, they need stable baselines. Medications like Ozempic can cause rapid changes in body composition, which would likely interfere with their data.


2. The "Medication Affecting Body Composition" Exclusion
The criteria also exclude any medication that can significantly affect body composition.



* GLP-1s: These drugs are known to cause significant loss of both fat mass and lean mass (muscle).

* Specific Goal: Because the study is validating tools like Bioelectrical Impedance Analysis (BIA) against high-end scans like MRI, being on a drug that actively shifts these ratios would make you a "confounding variable."
