## Gemini

Those classic "moo cans"—officially known as gravity reed cow bellows—are clever pieces of mechanical engineering that rely on gravity rather than electronics.


Inside the plastic canister, there are three primary components: a heavy sliding weight (the bellows), a metal reed, and a sealed chamber.


The Mechanism
The process is divided into two phases: the "reset" and the "moo."



* Phase 1: The Flip (The Reset)
When the can is upright, the heavy weight is at the bottom. When you flip the can upside down, gravity pulls the weight toward what is now the "bottom." During this movement, a one-way valve or simple gap allows air to fill the space behind the weight without making much noise.

* Phase 2: The Moo (The Activation)
When you flip the can back over (right-side up), the heavy weight begins to slide down toward its original position. As it falls, it acts like a piston, forcing the air trapped beneath it through a small opening.


Creating the Sound
The air forced out by the falling weight passes over a metal reed—similar to the reed in a harmonica or a saxophone.



* Vibration: The pressurized air makes the thin metal reed vibrate at a specific frequency.

* Modulation: The "moo" sound isn't just one flat note; it has a distinct shape. This is usually achieved by a small hole or a sliding sleeve that partially covers the reed’s output as the weight falls, causing the pitch or volume to shift slightly, mimicking the "m-oo-oo" inflection of a cow.

* Resonance: The hollow body of the can acts as a resonator, amplifying the tiny vibration of the reed so it's loud enough to hear.


Why it Stops
The "moo" lasts exactly as long as it takes for the weight to reach the bottom of the canister. Once the weight stops moving, the airflow ceases, the reed stops vibrating, and the cow goes silent until you flip it again.


Since the weight is governed by gravity, the sound is remarkably consistent, though you can sometimes "stutter" the moo by shaking the can or tilting it horizontally to slow the weight's descent.
