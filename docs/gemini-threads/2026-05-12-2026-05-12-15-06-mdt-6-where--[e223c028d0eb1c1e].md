## Gemini

It looks like you mixed up your physics concepts. You used the formula for impulse ($J = F \Delta t$) but tried to solve for distance ($d$).


Impulse relates force to time, whereas the Work-Energy Theorem relates force to distance. Since the bullet is coming to a complete stop, all of its initial kinetic energy ($E_k$) is being converted into work ($W$) done by the wood to stop it.



The Work-Energy Approach
To find the distance $d$, you need to set the work done by the average force equal to the change in kinetic energy:


$$W = \Delta E_k$$
$$F \cdot d = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$$


1. Identify your variables:



* Mass ($m$): 0.055 kg

* Velocity ($v$): 250 m/s

* Force ($F$): 9,500 N


2. Calculate the initial Kinetic Energy:
$$E_k = \frac{1}{2}(0.055 \text{ kg})(250 \text{ m/s})^2$$
$$E_k = \frac{1}{2}(0.055)(62,500)$$
$$E_k = 1,718.75 \text{ J}$$


3. Solve for distance ($d$):
$$9,500 \text{ N} \cdot d = 1,718.75 \text{ J}$$
$$d = \frac{1,718.75}{9,500}$$
$d \approx 0.18 \text{ m}$



Why your calculation failed
In your notes, you calculated the momentum ($p = 13.75 \text{ kg}\cdot\text{m/s}$) and then divided it by the force.


$$\frac{p}{F} = \frac{mv}{F}$$


If you look at the units there, you're actually solving for time ($t$), not distance. Your result of $1.4 \times 10^{-3}$ is the number of seconds it took for the bullet to stop, which is why your answer (A) is off by two decimal places.


The correct choice is C.
