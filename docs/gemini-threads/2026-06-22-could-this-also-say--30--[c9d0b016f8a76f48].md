## Gemini

No, it cannot.


In a 2D vector collision like this, the direction of the two final velocity vectors must be distinct from one another because they diverge on opposite sides of the incident path to conserve momentum.


Here is why the angles are structured the way they are:



* The Incident Line as the Reference ($0^\circ$): The original path of the incident ball establishes the reference line.

* Incident Ball Deflection: The text states the incident ball deflects at $30^\circ$ (which we can think of as $+30^\circ$, or "above" the incident line).

* Struck Ball Deflection: Because it is an elastic collision between identical masses, the two paths must form a $90^\circ$ angle relative to each other. Since the first ball went $+30^\circ$, the struck ball must go in the opposite direction, which is $-60^\circ$ (or $60^\circ$ "below" the incident line).


If you were to write $-30^\circ$ to the incident angle, you would be describing a path that mirrors the first ball's deflection on the opposite side. That would mean the two balls only have a $60^\circ$ angle between them, which violates the physics of an elastic glancing collision for identical masses.
