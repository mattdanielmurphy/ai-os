To show this relationship algebraically, you can use Newton's Second Law to set up a ratio between the two scenarios.


1. Identify the Initial State
The formula for force is $F = ma$. For the first object, we know the acceleration is $4.0\text{ m/s}^2$.
$$F = m \cdot 4.0$$


2. Identify the Second State
In the second scenario, the force $F$ remains the same, but the mass is now $2m$. We need to find the new acceleration, which we can call $a_{new}$.
$$F = (2m) \cdot a_{new}$$


3. Set the Equations Equal
Since the force $F$ is identical in both cases, you can set the two expressions for $F$ equal to one another:
$$m \cdot 4.0 = 2m \cdot a_{new}$$


4. Solve for $a_{new}$
Now, isolate $a_{new}$ by dividing both sides by $2m$:
$$\frac{m \cdot 4.0}{2m} = a_{new}$$


The mass $m$ cancels out:
$$\frac{4.0}{2} = a_{new}$$
$$a_{new} = 2.0\text{ m/s}^2$$



Alternative Method: The Ratio Approach
You can also express acceleration as a function of mass:
$$a = \frac{F}{m}$$


If you replace $m$ with $2m$:
$$a_{new} = \frac{F}{2m} = \frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{F}{m} \right)$$


Since $\frac{F}{m}$ is the original acceleration ($4.0\text{ m/s}^2$), you get:
$$a_{new} = \frac{1}{2}(4.0) = 2.0\text{ m/s}^2$$
