## Gemini

In the context of the circle you’re looking at, subtended is a fancy geometry term for "opposite to" or "carved out by."


Think of it like a flashlight beam: if you stand at the center of a circle and open your arms, the part of the circle's edge (the arc) that lies between your hands is subtended by the angle between your arms.


The Breakdown

* The Angle: The opening at the center of the circle.

* The Arc: The section of the circumference "cut off" by that opening.

* The Relationship: We say the angle is subtended by the arc, or the arc subtends the angle.


Why it matters for Radians
As your current lesson explains, 1 Radian is the specific size of an angle where the length of the arc it "opens up to" (subtends) is exactly equal to the radius of the circle.


If you have an arc length of $3r$, it "subtends" an angle of 3 radians. It’s just a way of describing which part of the circle's edge belongs to which central angle.

## Gemini

To know if two angles are coterminal, you are essentially checking if they land on the same "terminal arm" after some number of full rotations.


Since one full rotation is $360^{\circ}$ or $2\pi$ radians, you can use these two main methods:


1. The Addition/Subtraction Method
This is the most common way to check. If you can add or subtract $2\pi$ (or $360^{\circ}$) one or more times to get from Angle A to Angle B, they are coterminal.



* Example in Radians: Is $\frac{13\pi}{4}$ coterminal with $\frac{5\pi}{4}$?

* $\frac{13\pi}{4} - 2\pi = \frac{13\pi}{4} - \frac{8\pi}{4} = \mathbf{\frac{5\pi}{4}}$

* Yes, they are coterminal. This matches the logic in your u5 practice q answers for Question 4b.





2. The "Difference" Test
If you aren't sure how many rotations to add or subtract, find the difference between the two angles and divide by a full circle.



* The Rule: If $(Angle A - Angle B) \div 2\pi$ (or $360^{\circ}$) results in a whole number (integer), they are coterminal.



Example: Are $750^{\circ}$ and $30^{\circ}$ coterminal?



* $750 - 30 = 720$

* $720 \div 360 = \mathbf{2}$ (A whole number!)

* This means they are exactly 2 rotations apart.
