## Gemini

You've hit on the exact reason for the mix-up! You are thinking of the magnetic force resulting from a current, rather than the movement needed to induce it.


If a current were already flowing down through that wire in an outward magnetic field, the magnetic force ($\vec{F} = I\vec{L} \times \vec{B}$) would indeed push the wire to the left. Because nature always opposes the change (Lenz's Law), you have to do mechanical work against that opposing force to generate the current. Therefore, you have to push it in the opposite direction.

b. Which way(s) must we move the wire so that no current is generated?
.



* Explanation: To induce an electromotive force (emf), the wire must physically cut across the magnetic field lines in a way that forces charges along its length. Moving the wire up or down means the charges are being pushed parallel to the wire, so they can't travel down its length to create a current.



c. If we want a smaller current to flow..., which way must we move it?
.



* Explanation: The induced voltage depends on the perpendicular component of the velocity ($v_{\perp}$). If you pull the wire diagonally (for example, up and to the right at a $45^\circ$ angle), only the horizontal portion of that speed cuts the field lines, which reduces the induced emf and results in a smaller current.
