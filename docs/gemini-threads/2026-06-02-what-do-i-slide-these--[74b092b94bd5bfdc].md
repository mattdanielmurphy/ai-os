The derivative of the quotient $y = \frac{f}{g}$ using logarithmic differentiation is derived as follows:



* 
Start with the function:
$y = \frac{f}{g}$




* 
Take the natural logarithm of both sides:
$\ln(y) = \ln\left(\frac{f}{g}\right) = \ln(f) - \ln(g)$




* 
Differentiate both sides with respect to $x$:
$\frac{y'}{y} = (\ln(f))' - (\ln(g))'$
$\frac{y'}{y} = \frac{f'}{f} - \frac{g'}{g}$




* 
Solve for $y'$:
$y' = y \left( \frac{f'}{f} - \frac{g'}{g} \right)$




* 
Substitute $y = \frac{f}{g}$ back into the equation:
$y' = \frac{f}{g} \left( \frac{f'}{f} - \frac{g'}{g} \right)$




* 
Distribute and simplify:
$y' = \frac{f \cdot f'}{g \cdot f} - \frac{f \cdot g'}{g \cdot g}$
$y' = \frac{f'}{g} - \frac{f \cdot g'}{g^2}$




* 
Find a common denominator ($g^2$):
$y' = \frac{f' \cdot g}{g^2} - \frac{f \cdot g'}{g^2}$
$y' = \frac{f'g - fg'}{g^2}$





Thus, $\left(\frac{f}{g}\right)' = \frac{f'g - fg'}{g^2}$.
