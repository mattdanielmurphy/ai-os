## Gemini

Here is the breakdown of why doing them one-by-one in this same thread is your best bet, along with a couple of quick tips to keep things running smoothly.


Why One-by-One is Better

* Higher Accuracy & Focus: When you dump 4 large PDFs into a single prompt, the model has to process a massive amount of data simultaneously. It’s much more likely to skim details, accidentally skip a question, or give you highly generalized answers to save space. Processing one at a time forces it to give 100% of its attention to that specific chapter.

* Keeps the Formatting Consistent: Because you already have a perfect example of what you want in this thread (the Chapter 3 breakdown), the model can use it as a direct template. You can literally just say, "Do Chapter 4 next, using the exact same 'student-in-a-hurry' style as Chapter 3."

* Token Limits & UI Lag: Even though advanced models have massive context windows, uploading hours worth of heavy math text all at once can make the web interface sluggish, laggy, or cause the response to cut off halfway through because it hits a maximum output length limit.



Tips for the Rest of Your Workflow

* Keep it in this same thread: Don't start a new chat. The model needs to see the Chapter 3 output so it knows exactly how concise you want the algebra to be.

* Prompt Shortcut: For the next one, you can just upload the file and say:

"Do the exact same thing for this Chapter 4 PDF. Strip out redundant formulas, skip tedious expansion steps, and keep the math streamlined for a student in a hurry."





* Clear the Deck If It Gets Sluggish: If you get to Chapter 6 or 7 and notice the AI is starting to lag or forget the formatting, that means the chat history is getting too heavy. If that happens, just copy your favorite concise response, start a new thread, paste it as an example, and say, "Use this exact style for the next PDF."

## Gemini

Fair point! In terms of token count, 2,800 tokens is absolutely a drop in the bucket for Gemini’s context window.


The main reason to still go one-by-one isn't because the model can't handle the data volume, but rather because of instruction tracking.


When you bundle multiple chapters together and ask for specific formatting—like stripping out steps, changing the algebra style, and keeping it ultra-concise—large models have a tendency to drift or get lazy over a long response. It might do a great job on Chapter 4, but by the time it reaches Chapter 7 in a single output, it often starts defaults back to standard, verbose textbook solutions or skipping questions entirely to save output length.


Since you've already dropped Chapter 4 in, let's roll with it! Go ahead and hit submit on that one, and we'll knock them out cleanly.
