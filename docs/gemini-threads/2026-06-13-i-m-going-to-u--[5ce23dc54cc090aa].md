## Gemini

Yes, both campuses have plenty of pianos, but if you are majoring in computer science, getting official access to the soundproof practice rooms takes a specific strategy.


The short answer to your question is yes: at both institutions, general students are locked out of the dedicated music department practice rooms by default. However, registering for the right music course or ensemble unlocks that access completely.



1. University of Alberta (U of A)
The U of A has dozens of practice rooms on the 3rd floor of the Fine Arts Building (FAB), but they are locked behind OneCard proximity sensors.


Official Access Strategy
According to the U of A Department of Music protocols, you are eligible for OneCard access to the general practice rooms if you meet any of these criteria:



* By Course: You are registered in an "Applied Music" course (private lessons, usually designated as MUSIC 124, 126, or 127).

* By Ensemble: You audition for and join a Department of Music ensemble (like a choir, symphonic band, or orchestra), even if you participate as a non-credit student.



⚠️ Note: The grand piano practice rooms are strictly reserved for keyboard majors, but the general practice rooms contain upright pianos that are perfectly fine for regular practice.



The Backup Plan (No Course Required)
If your schedule gets too packed with comp sci labs and you can't fit a music course in a specific term, you still have options:



* The Open Classrooms: Almost every standard classroom in FAB has a piano in it. When classes aren't running, these rooms are often left unlocked, and students frequently drop in to play.

* Campus Housing: If you live on campus, certain residence halls (like Lister Centre and East Campus housing) have private music practice rooms with pianos dedicated exclusively to residents, completely bypassing the music department rules.

* Public Pianos: There are a couple of public pianos scattered around, including one in the pedway between HUB Mall and the Humanities building, though these are less ideal for focused, uninterrupted practice.



2. MacEwan University
MacEwan’s music program is housed in Allard Hall (the big, modern building downtown). Their facilities are top-tier and highly soundproofed, but they are equally strict about security.


Official Access Strategy
Much like the U of A, MacEwan restricts its Allard Hall practice spaces to students directly involved in the music program. To get your OneCard programmed for access, you need to be enrolled in:



* Private music instruction courses as an elective.

* One of MacEwan's directed ensembles or combos.


Because MacEwan’s music program leans heavily into contemporary music (jazz, pop, rock), their ensemble options are highly diverse, making it a fun route if you want to play with a group to earn your practice room privileges.


The Backup Plan
Allard Hall is designed around an open, collaborative layout. While the official practice labs require a swipe card, casual acoustic pianos can occasionally be found in open theater spaces or empty classrooms between instruction hours. However, relying on these is much more of a gamble than it is at FAB on the U of A campus.



The Comp Sci Game Plan
Since you already want to take a music course, your best move is to lock that down in your first-term course selection.



* Look for "Applied Music" or Ensembles: When registration opens, look for 100-level music electives that include studio/instruction time or ensemble performance.

* Visit the Music Office Early: During the first week of September, head straight to the Music Department main office (3rd floor FAB at U of A, or Allard Hall at MacEwan). Show them your registration, and they will encode your student ID card right there.

* Keep an Eye on the Clock: Once you have access, rooms are first-come, first-served. General practice rooms operate on a courtesy rule—if you step out for more than 10 to 15 minutes, you forfeit the room.


Good luck finishing up your prerequisite courses! You're incredibly close to the finish line.
