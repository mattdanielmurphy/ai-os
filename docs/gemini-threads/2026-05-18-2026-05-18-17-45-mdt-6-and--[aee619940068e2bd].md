Actually, for a Free Body Diagram (FBD), you strictly want a single arrowhead pointing away from the object.


While it's true that tension is a "tug-of-war" force acting at both ends of the string, an FBD is a snapshot of the forces acting exclusively on one specific body. If you include an arrowhead pointing down toward the balloon, you're technically illustrating the force the string is exerting on the ceiling pivot, which doesn't belong in the balloon's diagram.


By pointing the arrow only upward and inward along the string, you are correctly showing that the string is "pulling" the balloon.


A Quick Summary of FBD Best Practices:

* The "Pull" Rule: Tension always pulls. The vector should start at the center of the balloon and point toward the pivot.

* Vector Length: In your asymmetric case, since the heavier balloon ($m_1$) is hanging more vertically, its tension vector ($T_1$) will actually be longer than $T_2$ because it’s supporting more weight.

* Tail to Tip: It's standard practice to draw the "tail" of all your force vectors (Gravity, Electrostatic, and Tension) starting from the center of the balloon (the center of mass).


Flipping those arrows to point up will make your diagrams look much more professional and physically sound. It's a small change, but it's the difference between a "sketch" and a proper physics diagram.
