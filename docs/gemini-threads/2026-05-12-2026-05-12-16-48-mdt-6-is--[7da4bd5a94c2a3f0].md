## Gemini

Not quite—you're short a couple of exponents.


$1\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m/s}$ is actually the unit for momentum ($\text{mass} \times \text{velocity}$).


Because a Joule ($\text{J}$) is a unit of work or energy ($\text{Force} \times \text{Distance}$), the dimensional analysis breaks down like this:



* Force (Newtons): $$1\text{ N} = 1\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m/s}^2$$

* Energy (Joules): $$1\text{ J} = 1\text{ N}\cdot\text{m} = 1\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m/s}^2 \cdot \text{m}$$


So, the correct equation is:
$$1\text{ J} = 1\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{s}^2$$

## Gemini

You aren't crazy, but there might be a slight mix-up in how you're visualizing the relationship between the variables.


If we look at the formula for kinetic energy:
$$E_k = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$$


In the first graph, the student plotted $E_k$ against $v^2$. Since $E_k$ is directly proportional to the entire term $v^2$, the relationship is linear ($y = mx$), which is why that graph is a straight line.


The Second Graph ($E_k$ vs. $v$)
For the last question, the x-axis is changed to just $v$. Now, the relationship follows a power law where the exponent is 2. This is a standard quadratic function ($y = ax^2$), which always results in a parabola.


Your calculations are actually spot on and they confirm the shape shown in the answer key:



* When $v = 10$, $E_k = 5,000$

* When $v = 20$, $E_k = 20,000$


Notice that when you doubled the velocity ($10 \rightarrow 20$), the kinetic energy quadrupled ($5,000 \rightarrow 20,000$). That "rapid upward curve" is the definition of an exponential-looking quadratic curve.


Why it isn't "reversed"
If the graph were a square root curve (the "reverse" or inverse of what is shown), it would look like it's flattening out as it moves to the right. That would only happen if you were plotting $v$ on the y-axis and $E_k$ on the x-axis ($v = \sqrt{\frac{2E_k}{m}}$).


Since $E_k$ is on the vertical axis, it grows faster and faster as $v$ increases, giving you that steepening slope. The answer key is correct here: it’s a classic parabolic curve starting at the origin.
