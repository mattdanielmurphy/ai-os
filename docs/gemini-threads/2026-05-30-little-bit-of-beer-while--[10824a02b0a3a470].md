## Gemini

Going for a "hair of the dog" drink might temporarily mask your hangover symptoms, but it ultimately stretches out the recovery process.


Why it feels like it works
When your blood alcohol concentration (BAC) drops back down to zero, that is when the most severe hangover symptoms strike. Part of this discomfort is actually mild alcohol withdrawal. Having a beer raises your BAC slightly and triggers a small release of dopamine, which can temporarily dull the headache and shakiness.


Why it backfires
A hangover is essentially a combination of dehydration, inflammation, and the buildup of acetaldehyde (a toxic byproduct your liver creates while breaking down alcohol).



* More Dehydration: Alcohol suppresses vasopressin, the hormone that tells your kidneys to hold onto water. Another beer forces your body to lose more fluids.

* Delayed Processing: Your liver has to halt the process of clearing out yesterday's toxins to deal with the new influx of alcohol. You are essentially hitting the snooze button on the hangover; once that beer wears off, the symptoms will return, often worse.


Better Alternatives
If you want to speed up the process, your focus should be on clearing out acetaldehyde and restoring fluid balance:



* Hydration with Electrolytes: Water alone is slow. Sports drinks, hydration tablets, or even a salty broth help replace the sodium and potassium you lost.

* Complex Carbohydrates: Alcohol crashes your blood sugar, which causes the weakness and shakiness. Toast, crackers, or oatmeal will stabilize your blood sugar without irritating your stomach.

* N-Acetyl Cysteine (NAC) / Eggs: Eggs contain cysteine, an amino acid that helps your body produce glutathione, the antioxidant your liver uses to break down acetaldehyde.


Rest and hydration are the only true fixes, but a solid meal with plenty of fluids will get you to the finish line a lot faster than another drink.
