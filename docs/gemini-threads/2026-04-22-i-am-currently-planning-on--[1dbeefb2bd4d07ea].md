Designing your life around minimizing social friction isn't irrational—it’s actually a very practical way to handle high levels of shyness. The Problem with the "Remote Cybersecurity" Plan
If your primary goal is to solve loneliness, a remote cybersecurity career is arguably one of the most difficult paths you could choose.



* The Isolation: Working remotely from different cities sounds romantic, but "digital nomad" life is notoriously lonely. You are constantly the "new person" who doesn't know the local language or social cues, and you're leaving behind whatever progress you made in the previous city.

* The Work: Cybersecurity often involves a high degree of independent, deep-focus work. Unlike a product team or a startup where there’s constant collaboration and "war-rooming," security can be quite siloed.


Re-Evaluating the Major
While you don’t need to abandon tech, some specializations have a naturally higher "human" component than others.

* Computer Science with a "Human" focus: If you stay in CS, look into Human-Computer Interaction (HCI). It’s highly technical but focuses on how people use software, which pulls you out of the code and into the real world.


Utilizing "Social Glue"
You mentioned that extroverts have been your lifeline in the past. In a university setting, you can find "institutional" extroverts—people whose literal job or role is to bring people together.

A Realistic Shift in Perspective
Choosing a path because it pays well and allows you to be alone is a defensive strategy. It's a plan designed to protect you from the discomfort of your shyness, but as you've noted, it might also protect you from the things you actually want (friends and a partner).


If you prioritize the "human" aspect of your career choice now, you aren't "losing" your technical skills. You’re just choosing a different environment to apply them in. You can still be a "computer nerd" and build software, but you could do it for a small startup in a physical office where the team grabs lunch together every day.

## Gemini

Making these at home is more of an exercise in patience and freezer management than complex cooking. Since neither requires traditional baking, the "difficulty" lies in the assembly timing.


1. Billy Miner’s Pie
This is essentially a deconstructed mocha ice cream cake. It is arguably easier because it doesn't require perfectly smooth frosting or structural integrity.



* The Crust: Crush Oreo cookies (filling included) and mix with melted butter. Press this into a pie plate and freeze it hard.

* The Filling: Soften coffee ice cream just enough to spread it into the crust, then freeze again.

* The Toppings: The signature look comes from sliced almonds and a heavy drizzle of caramel and chocolate sauce applied right before serving.

* Difficulty: 2/10. If you can crush a cookie, you can make this.


2. Standard Ice Cream Cake
This is slightly more technical because you usually want distinct layers and a "finished" look.



* The Structure: Most home versions use a springform pan. You’ll layer softened ice cream, a "crunch" layer (like chocolate shells and cookie crumbles), and another ice cream flavor.

* The "Crunch": To get that classic commercial texture, mix chocolate sandwich cookie crumbs with chocolate shell topping (the kind that hardens). This keeps the crumbs crispy even when surrounded by moisture.

* The Frosting: Traditional buttercream freezes rock-hard and becomes unpleasant to eat. Use stabilized whipped cream (whipped cream with a bit of cream cheese or gelatin) or a frozen whipped topping. It stays airy even at sub-zero temperatures.

* Difficulty: 4/10. The challenge is "brain freeze" for the cake—if it melts too much during assembly, the layers will muddy together.

If you want the cold, creamy texture without the assembly stress, try a Semifreddo. It’s an Italian "half-frozen" dessert made by folding whipped cream into a custard or meringue. It has the texture of frozen mousse and doesn't require an ice cream maker or multiple freezing stages.

How long to make a billy miner?

---

## Gemini

Since you don’t have to bake anything, the "active" work takes about 20 minutes. However, the total clock time is roughly 4 to 6 hours because success depends entirely on the pie being frozen solid.


The Timeline Breakdown

* Crust (10 mins + 30 mins freeze): Crushing the cookies and pressing them into the pie plate is fast. You must freeze the crust before adding the ice cream, or the warm friction of spreading the ice cream will pull the crumbs apart.

* The Filling (5 mins + 4+ hours freeze): This is the "danger zone." You want the coffee ice cream soft enough to scoop, but not melted. If it’s soup, it will develop ice crystals. Once it’s packed into the crust, it needs a deep freeze to regain its structure.

* The Toppings (5 mins): Wait until the very last second to add the caramel, chocolate, and almonds. If you freeze the sauces, they lose their gloss and become tacky.



Efficiency Tips

* The "Hack" Method: If you’re in a rush, buy a pre-made Oreo crust from the grocery store. This cuts out the butter-melting and crumb-smashing, allowing you to go straight to the filling.

* The Softening Rule: Leave the ice cream on the counter for 10–15 minutes to soften naturally. Don't microwave it—microwaving creates "hot spots" that ruin the texture.

* Make it Ahead: This is the perfect "day before" dessert. It actually benefits from sitting in the back of the freezer overnight to ensure it’s hard enough to slice cleanly.


If you're planning on serving this tonight, do you have the coffee ice cream on hand, or are you looking to pivot to something with a faster turnaround?

## Gemini

You should move those tomatoes immediately. Tomato plants—specifically the vines, leaves, and stems—are part of the nightshade family and contain a substance called solanine.


While the ripe fruit itself is generally considered non-toxic to cats, the green parts of the plant are a different story. If a cat ingests the vine, it can lead to "solanine poisoning."


Symptoms to Watch For
If your cat has already eaten a significant amount, keep a close eye out for:



* Gastrointestinal upset: Vomiting or diarrhea.

* Lethargy: Extreme weakness or appearing "spaced out."

* Hypersalivation: Excessive drooling.

* Slow heart rate: Or other signs of physical distress.


What to Do

* Clear the area: Put the tomatoes in a cupboard, the fridge, or a hanging basket where the cat can't reach them.

* Monitor behavior: If you notice any of the symptoms above, or if your cat seems unusually uncoordinated, it’s best to call a vet. Most cats will only take a small nibble and be fine, but the toxins can be hard on their system.


If you’re looking for a "safe" green alternative for them to chew on, keeping a small pot of cat grass (usually oats or wheatgrass) on the counter can sometimes distract them from your produce.
