## Gemini

That is exactly what’s happening. This phenomenon is known as internal carpellary hymenode, or more commonly, an "internal proliferated ovary."


It occurs due to a minor hormonal or genetic slip-up during the pepper’s development, causing a secondary fruit to start growing inside the primary one.


Quick Facts:

* Is it edible? Yes. The "baby" pepper is perfectly safe to eat, though it usually tastes just like the parent pepper (or slightly more bitter/firm).

* Is it a parasite? Nope. It’s essentially a sterile twin that developed without seeds of its own.

* Why does it happen? It is often triggered by rapid temperature fluctuations or inconsistent watering while the plant is flowering.


It's actually quite common in bell peppers, but finding one that looks like a miniature, fully-formed pepper—complete with its own little stem—is always a fun discovery.
