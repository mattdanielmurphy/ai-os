## Gemini

There isn't a hard-coded number in the Terms of Service like "thrice and you're out," but Amazon’s internal systems definitely track manual overrides and concessions.


Since you are asking for a human to step in and grant a "favor" (waiving a fee or booking a special courier), you are moving from the automated system to the Account Health system. Here is how that usually plays out:


1. The "Concession" Balance
Amazon tracks what they call "Concessions"—every time they give you a credit, a free month of Prime, or waive a return fee, it adds to a total on your account.



* The "Safe" Zone: If you spend thousands a year and ask for this once every 10–15 returns, the system won't even blink.

* The "Flag" Zone: If the cost of your returns (including these special pickups) starts to approach or exceed your total profit to Amazon, their system may start to "gray out" certain options for agents. An agent might say, "The system won't let me waive this today," which is often code for your concession limit being reached for the month.


2. Return Rate vs. Request Type
Amazon is much more sensitive to why you are returning items than how you are returning them.



* Frequent "Changed my mind" or "Found a better price" returns get flagged much faster.

* Returns for "Defective" or "Inaccurate description" items are almost never held against you, even if you ask for a manual pickup, because it's technically Amazon's fault.


3. The "Account Ban" Myth (and Reality)
You've probably seen headlines about people getting banned for too many returns. This is extremely rare and usually only happens if:



* You return more than 10% of your total order volume consistently.

* You have a pattern of "Missing Item" or "Empty Box" claims.

* You frequently use "I can't leave the house" as an excuse for every single order.


How to stay under the radar:

* Spread it out: If you have three items to return, try to get them handled in one manual UPS pickup request rather than three separate chats. It counts as one concession rather than three.

* Save it for the big stuff: Use the "manual pickup" request for heavy items or high-value electronics. For a $5 charging cable, it's usually not worth the "concession hit" on your account.

* Keep your account profile healthy: Since you're a student and likely ordering textbooks or tech frequently, that high volume of "successful" (non-returned) orders gives you a lot more leverage to ask for these favors.


The bottom line: Doing this 2–3 times a year is perfectly fine. Doing it every other week will eventually lead to the agents being "unable" to help you, even if they want to.
