1. Luck vs. Fortune
Seelig makes a sharp distinction between two types of chance:

The RAS acts as a filter. If you decide to look for "luck" (opportunities), your brain starts flagging relevant information that it would have otherwise ignored as background noise. It's the same effect as when you buy a specific car and suddenly see that car everywhere.


4. Why the "Manifesting" Label?
Marketing. "Manifesting" is a trending buzzword that drives podcast downloads. However, the actual academic approach is closer to Planned Happenstance Theory. It’s the idea that while you can’t predict a specific lucky break, you can behave in a way that makes some lucky break statistically inevitable.


In short: It's not about the "Law of Attraction." It's about high-probability behavior and training your brain to spot opportunities that are already there.

## Gemini

It isn't necessarily ruined, but it is definitely a nuisance. Double-walled mugs (whether glass or stainless steel) usually rely on a vacuum seal to provide insulation. When water gets in, it means that seal has failed, often due to a small pinhole or a crack in the adhesive at the base.


Here is the breakdown of what to expect and how to handle it:


The Impact on Performance

* Loss of Insulation: Since the vacuum is gone and replaced by water and air, the mug won't keep your coffee hot or your water cold nearly as well. It effectively becomes a heavy, single-walled mug.

* Condensation: When you put hot liquid inside, the trapped water between the walls will turn to steam and fog up the interior, which looks messy.


Safety and Hygiene

* Mold Risk: Because the gap is so narrow and poorly ventilated, that trapped water is a prime breeding ground for mold and bacteria over time.

* Heat Expansion: Do not put it in the microwave. If there is trapped water and no way for steam to escape quickly, the pressure could theoretically cause the inner or outer wall to shatter or pop.


Can You Fix It?
You can try to "drain" it, though it requires patience:



* Locate the Entry Point: Look for a tiny silicone plug or a small hole at the bottom.

* The Heat Method: Place the mug in a warm (not hot) spot, like a sunny windowsill or a low-temp oven (around 65°C / 150°F), with the hole facing down. This can take hours or even days to fully evaporate.

* Reseal: If you manage to get all the water out, you can try dabbing a tiny bit of food-grade silicone sealant or waterproof epoxy over the hole to prevent it from happening again.



The Verdict: If it’s a cheap mug, it’s probably heading for the "pencil holder" phase of its life. If it’s a high-end glass one, it’s likely to stay foggy forever. Moving forward, hand-washing is the only way to prevent this, as the high pressure and heat cycles of a dishwasher are exactly what force water through those microscopic seals.
