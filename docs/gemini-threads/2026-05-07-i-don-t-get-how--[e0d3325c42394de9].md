## Gemini

It is a very common point of confusion because we usually think of "adding forces" to get a total. You are correct that both forces contribute to the motion, but the key is in how we define the Net Force ($F_{net}$) and the Centripetal Acceleration ($a_c$).



1. The Direction of the "Win"
In circular motion, $a_c$ is always a vector pointing directly toward the center of the circle.



* At the Top: The center is down. Therefore, $a_c$ is downward.

* The Net Force Rule: $F_{net}$ must point in the same direction as acceleration. So at the top, the downward forces must "win."


2. Setting up the Equation
To make the math work, we pick a direction to be positive. If we pick down (toward the center) as positive:



* $F_g$ is positive (pointing down).

* $F_N$ is negative (pointing up, because the seat is pushing up on your bottom).


Following the Physics Unit 3 Answer Key, we start with $F_{net} = ma_c$:
$$F_g + (-F_N) = ma_c$$
$$F_g - F_N = ma_c$$


3. Why it isn't $F_g + F_N$
The only time you would use $F_g + F_N$ is if the Normal Force was also pointing down.


Think about a Roller Coaster Loop: at the very top of a loop, you are upside down. The track is above you, pushing down on the wheels to keep you from flying off into space. In that specific case, the equation would be $F_g + F_N = ma_c$ because both vectors point toward the center.


On a Ferris Wheel, however, you are sitting upright. The seat is always underneath you, pushing up against gravity. Since $F_g$ and $F_N$ are fighting each other, the net force is their difference, not their sum.



The "Apparent Weight" Reality Check
If you rearrange that equation to solve for $F_N$ (which is your "apparent weight" or what you feel in your gut):
$$F_N = F_g - ma_c$$


This explains why you feel lighter at the top. Gravity is providing the force to turn you, so the seat doesn't have to push up as hard as it does when you're just standing still.


Does that distinction between the seat pushing up (Ferris wheel) versus the track pushing down (Roller coaster loop) help clear up the signs?
