## Gemini

Not quite—it’s actually the other way around.


$\ln$ is the shorthand for $\log_e$ (a logarithm with base $e$).


When you see $\log(e)$ written without a base specified, it usually means $\log_{10}(e)$, which is just a number (approximately $0.434$).


For Example 10, where you have the equation $P = 100e^{-0.124a}$, using $\ln$ is your "shortcut" because $\ln$ and $e$ are inverse functions. They essentially cancel each other out.


The Shortcut in Action
If you have to solve for the altitude ($a$) when pressure is $3.5$:



* Isolate the $e$ term: $$0.035 = e^{-0.124a}$$

* Take the natural log ($\ln$) of both sides:
$$\ln(0.035) = \ln(e^{-0.124a})$$

* The "Magic" Step: Because $\ln(e^x) = x$, the right side simplifies instantly:
$$\ln(0.035) = -0.124a$$

* Solve for $a$:
$$a = \frac{\ln(0.035)}{-0.124}$$


If you used regular $\log$ (base 10), you’d end up with an extra $\log(e)$ term floating around that you'd have to divide by later. Using $\ln$ just saves you a step.


On your calculator, look for the LN button—it's usually right next to or below the LOG button. Does your calculator have them separated like that?

## Gemini

The reason your answer is off by a factor of 100 is likely a small decimal slip-up in the first step.


When you divide $3.5$ by $100$, you should get $0.035$. If you accidentally used $0.35$, you would end up with an altitude of about $8.5\text{ km}$, but if you calculated $3.5 / 100$ as $0.035$, the math works out to the provided answer.


The Correction
Here is the step-by-step to reach the $27.04\text{ km}$ shown in Example 10:



* Divide by 100: $$\frac{3.5}{100} = e^{-0.124a}$$
$$0.035 = e^{-0.124a}$$

* Take the Natural Log ($\ln$):
$$\ln(0.035) = -0.124a$$

* Calculate $\ln(0.035)$:
$$-3.3524 \approx -0.124a$$

* Isolate $a$:
$$a = \frac{-3.3524}{-0.124} \approx \mathbf{27.04\text{ km}}$$


Where did $.282$ come from?
If you got $0.282$, it looks like you might have calculated $\ln(0.965)$ or something similar. For instance, if you accidentally did $1 - 0.035$ before taking the log, or if there was a typo in the division.


In the future, a quick "sniff test" for atmospheric pressure: $3.5\text{ kPa}$ is extremely low (normal sea level is about $101\text{ kPa}$). Since the air is very thin at that pressure, you'd expect to be way up near the top of the stratosphere, so a result in the $27\text{ km}$ range makes much more sense than $282$ meters!

## Gemini

Ah, the classic "magic wand" trap! I see what happened. You likely saw $\ln$ and $e$ together and thought they just vanished, leaving you with:


$$0.035 = -0.124a$$


If you solve that (dividing $0.035$ by $-0.124$), you get exactly $-0.282$.


Why they don't just "cancel" yet
In algebra, you can only cancel them out once the $\ln$ is applied to the $e$. Think of $\ln$ like a key and $e$ like a lock. You can't open the door until you actually put the key in.



* Wrong: $0.035 = -0.124a$ (This ignores the fact that the right side was an exponent).

* Right: $\ln(0.035) = \ln(e^{-0.124a})$


Once you "put the key in" on the right side, the $\ln$ and $e$ do indeed cancel out, but you are still left with the $\ln(0.035)$ on the left side. Since $\ln$ of a decimal is always a negative number, that’s where the $-3.35$ comes from to balance out the negative on the other side.


The "Mental Rule" for next time:
If your variable is stuck in the "attic" (the exponent), you need the "log ladder" to bring it down. You can't just delete the base ($e$) and hope the variable falls down on its own—the log is the tool that physically moves it.
