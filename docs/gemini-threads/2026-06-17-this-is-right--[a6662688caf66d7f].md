Here is a quick breakdown of why the calculation is solid:



* The Process: A cesium-133 atom has 55 protons. During electron capture, a proton in the nucleus absorbs an inner-orbital electron and converts into a neutron.

* The Math: * The mass number ($A$) remains completely unchanged because the total number of nucleons (protons + neutrons) stays the same: $$133 + 0 = 133$$

* The atomic number ($Z$) decreases by 1 because you lose a proton: $$55 - 1 = 54$$




* The Identity: Looking at the periodic table, atomic number 54 belongs to Xenon (Xe).


The resulting daughter atom is indeed iodine's heavy neighbor, Xenon-133 ($^{133}_{54}\text{Xe}$). Everything balances out perfectly.

## Gemini

m = 1.0e-9 # kg
c = 3.00e8 # m/s
E = m * (c**2)
print(f"E = {E:.2e} J")



E = 9.00e+07 J


That is very close, but the exponent is slightly different. Using the correct conversion for a microgram ($1.0 \text{ }\mu\text{g} = 1.0 \times 10^{-9}\text{ kg}$), the final answer is $9.0 \times 10^7\text{ J}$ (or $90,000,000\text{ J}$).


Here is how the math breaks down:


$$E = mc^2$$
$$E = (1.0 \times 10^{-9}\text{ kg}) \times (3.00 \times 10^8\text{ m/s})^2$$
$$E = (1.0 \times 10^{-9}\text{ kg}) \times (9.00 \times 10^{16}\text{ m}^2/\text{s}^2)$$


When you multiply the powers of 10, you add the exponents:
$$-9 + 16 = 7$$


$$E = 9.0 \times 10^7\text{ J}$$
