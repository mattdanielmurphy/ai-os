## Gemini

While the standard recommendation is 2 hours, the "actual" time depends heavily on your specific logistics and risk tolerance. Here is a breakdown of how to shave that time down or when you definitely shouldn't.


The 60-Minute Rule
At most major airports, 60 minutes before departure is the absolute "danger zone." This is typically when:



* Checked bag drop-offs close: Many airlines will not accept luggage less than 45–60 minutes before a flight.

* Boarding begins: Most domestic flights start boarding 30–40 minutes before takeoff.

* Security lines peak: If you hit a sudden rush at the TSA or CATSA checkpoint, 60 minutes can vanish instantly.



Factors That Allow for 90 Minutes
You can usually arrive 90 minutes before your flight if you meet these criteria:



* Carry-on only: You skip the check-in counter and head straight to security with a mobile boarding pass.

* Trusted Traveler Status: Having TSA PreCheck or NEXUS/Clear significantly reduces the variance in security wait times.

* Off-peak hours: Flying mid-week or late at night.


When You Really Need 2+ Hours
Stick to the full 2-hour window (or even 2.5) if:



* You are checking bags: Bag drop lines can occasionally be longer than security lines.

* Peak Travel Windows: Monday mornings (business travelers) or Friday afternoons.

* Massive Hubs: Airports like Pearson (YYZ), Atlanta (ATL), or O'Hare (ORD) require significantly more walking time to reach distant gates.



Pro-Tips for Cutting it Close

* Check the App: Download your airline's app. It will notify you if boarding is delayed, but more importantly, it's your ticket to bypassing the check-in desk.

* Monitor Security Wait Times: Many airport websites now offer live-streamed wait times for their checkpoints.

* Know the "Door Closing" Time: Remember that the departure time on your ticket is when the plane leaves the gate. The boarding door usually closes 15 minutes prior to that. If you arrive at the gate 5 minutes before departure, you've already missed the flight.
