## Gemini

To see why these units are equivalent, it helps to break down the Newton into its base components using $F = ma$.


A Newton ($N$) is defined as the force required to accelerate one kilogram at one meter per second squared:
$$1\text{ N} = 1\text{ kg} \cdot \text{m/s}^2$$


Now, look at the units for Impulse ($N \cdot s$):



* Start with the definition:
$$N \cdot s$$

* Substitute the base components of a Newton:
$$\left( \frac{\text{kg} \cdot \text{m}}{\text{s}^2} \right) \cdot \text{s}$$

* Cancel out one second from the top and bottom:
$$\frac{\text{kg} \cdot \text{m} \cdot \text{s}}{\text{s}^2} = \frac{\text{kg} \cdot \text{m}}{\text{s}}$$


This leaves you with kg·m/s, which is exactly the unit for momentum ($mass \times velocity$). Because the math simplifies perfectly, the two units are interchangeable.
