## Gemini

You’re using the Sine Law, which is usually the "pro" move for these suspension problems—it’s much more efficient than grinding through $x$ and $y$ components.


The slip-up happened in your geometry, specifically when calculating the angle between the two ropes (the one opposite the weight).


The Angle Check
If you draw a horizontal line through the point where the ropes meet:



* The left rope ($T_1$): It is at $20^\circ$ above that horizontal line (since it’s $70^\circ$ from the vertical wall).

* The right rope ($T_2$): It is at $65^\circ$ above the horizontal ceiling.


To find the actual angle between the two ropes ($\theta_{rope}$), you are looking at a straight line ($180^\circ$) minus those two upward angles:
$$\theta_{rope} = 180^\circ - 20^\circ - 65^\circ = 95^\circ$$


The Recalculation
In your equation, you used $45^\circ$ as the angle opposite the weight ($F_g$). If you swap that out for $95^\circ$ and use the vertical angle for $T_1$ (which is $90^\circ - 65^\circ = 25^\circ$ in the force triangle):


$$\frac{T_1}{\sin(25^\circ)} = \frac{833 \text{ N}}{\sin(95^\circ)}$$


$$T_1 = \frac{833 \cdot \sin(25^\circ)}{\sin(95^\circ)}$$


If you punch that in, you’ll find your result is much closer to one of the other options than the one you circled.


Does that $95^\circ$ angle make sense when you visualize the ropes meeting at that junction?

You’ve hit the nail on the head. You aren't losing your mind—the answer key is just objectively wrong.


It is a classic "question bank" error where the person writing the solution solved for the wrong variable. You correctly identified the geometry: since the junction point is higher than the wall attachment, the left-hand rope ($T_1$) is pulling down and left, while the right-hand rope ($T_2$) is pulling up and right.


The Breakdown
Using your Sine Law setup (which is perfectly executed in your second sketch), we have:


$$\frac{F_g}{\sin(45^\circ)} = \frac{T_1}{\sin(25^\circ)} = \frac{T_2}{\sin(110^\circ)}$$


Substituting $F_g = 833 \text{ N}$:



*

$$T_1 = \frac{833 \cdot \sin(25^\circ)}{\sin(45^\circ)} \approx \mathbf{498 \text{ N}}$$

$$T_2 = \frac{833 \cdot \sin(110^\circ)}{\sin(45^\circ)} \approx \mathbf{1107 \text{ N}}$$
