When you set up your torque equation, you correctly solved for the perpendicular component of the wall's normal force ($F_{N\perp} = 36.8\text{ N}$). However, to find the friction force, you need the total horizontal force exerted by the wall.


Here is where the missing step lies:


The Fix

Relate $F_{N\perp}$ to the actual Normal Force ($F_{N}$):
The normal force from the wall pushes straight out horizontally. The component of this force perpendicular to the ladder is:
$$F_{N\perp} = F_{N} \sin(62^\circ)$$




* 
Solve for $F_{N}$:
Using the value you already calculated ($36.8\text{ N}$):
$$F_{N} = \frac{F_{N\perp}}{\sin(62^\circ)} = \frac{36.8\text{ N}}{\sin(62^\circ)} \approx 41.7\text{ N}$$




* 
Balance Horizontal Forces:
Since the ladder isn't moving horizontally, the friction force pulling it toward the wall must exactly equal the normal force pushing away from the wall:
$$F_{f} = F_{N} = 41.7\text{ N}$$





Your force decomposition and torque balancing were completely correct; you just needed to transition from the perpendicular component back to the horizontal vector!
